## Executive Summary

The AI-safety nonprofit METR assessed that Anthropic's capability gains have run about 1.9 times faster since October 2025 than at their earlier rate. The estimate is wide, 1.5 to 2.5 times, and METR says the acceleration is not due to faster scaling of training compute. The finding points toward possible recursive self-improvement (RSI), though AI has not yet shown it can make substantial contributions on its own to the most critical stages of model training.

## What the assessment covers

According to Storyboard18's report of 6 November 2026, which cites Reuters, METR's assessment is as follows.

- Period: an 11-week assessment between August and October 2026
- Inputs: confidential research, interviews and performance data from models not yet released
- Finding: benchmark performance has improved 1.9 times faster since October 2025 than the earlier rate of progress
- Quote: "As of October 2026, Anthropic is likely to improve the capabilities of their models on well-specified tasks at around twice the rate they did between March 2024 and October 2025" (METR)
## Uncertainty and limits

METR itself says the estimate carries considerable uncertainty: the actual acceleration could be 1.5 to 2.5 times the historical rate. It also notes that AI has not yet demonstrated the ability to independently make substantial contributions to the most critical stages of model training.

The same caution applies to internal productivity. Anthropic employees report considerable gains from AI tools for coding and experimentation, and informal internal polls indicated gains of around 12 times. METR said these figures were not sufficiently reliable.

## Not explained by more compute

The article quotes METR: "We are confident that the acceleration was not due to an acceleration in the scaling of training compute." If compute is not the driver, using AI within the development process itself is one candidate factor, which is what ties the finding to the RSI discussion.

## Implications

Facts first, then the inference drawn from them.

**Facts**

- The central estimate is about 1.9x, within a range of 1.5x to 2.5x.
- METR is confident the cause is not compute scaling.
- Staff self-estimates of productivity (about 12x) were judged unreliable.
**Implications**

- The gap between self-reported and independently measured effects is large. Anyone describing the effect of adopting AI needs outside evaluation to back self-reports, and that holds for organizations embedding AI in their work too.
- The article says concern is growing over whether existing risk-evaluation methods can keep pace with the technology. The speed at which evaluation frameworks are updated becomes a practical limit on verifiability and trust.
## Context reported alongside

The article also reports that Anthropic may list on the stock market as early as November, and that its CEO called for a slowdown in advanced AI development in September. These are context attached to the report, not conclusions of the assessment.
