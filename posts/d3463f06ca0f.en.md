## Executive Summary

U.S. District Judge Rita Lin ruled on Thursday that the Pentagon's designation of Anthropic as a supply-chain risk was unlawful, finding that the government's actions violated Anthropic's constitutional rights. Axios calls it a major legal victory for Anthropic in a months-long fight with the Trump administration over how the military can use its AI models. The government is expected to appeal, and a separate designation under a different statute is still being contested in the D.C. Circuit.

## What the ruling says

According to Axios, the ruling came from U.S. District Judge Rita Lin. The question before her was whether the Pentagon's designation of Anthropic as a supply-chain risk was lawful. She held that it was not, and that the government's actions violated Anthropic's constitutional rights.

The decision runs 59 pages. In the passages Axios quotes, Lin writes:

- "The empty invocation of national security is not a blank check to punish and retaliate against government critics."
- "Though the Department of War is undisputedly free to select the AI vendor of its choice, the evidence demonstrates that the broad measures imposed on Anthropic were illegal and baseless."
Note what the judge does not do: she does not question the government's discretion in procurement. The freedom to choose a vendor is conceded; what she separates from it is imposing a penalty through a designation.

## The inconsistency the judge pressed

Lin noted that the Pentagon kept pursuing work with Anthropic even after the designation. In her words: "None of that is consistent with a genuine fear that Anthropic is a saboteur who would poison its software to harm national security."

The logic is that the severity of the risk used to justify the designation does not square with the government's own later conduct. If the concern were genuinely a security one, continuing to do business would be hard to reconcile with it.

## How the dispute began

Per Axios's "Catch up quick," the dispute began earlier this year when Anthropic and the Pentagon clashed over the military's use of the company's AI models.

- The Defense Department sought to use Claude for "all lawful purposes," even in the most sensitive military and intelligence applications.
- Anthropic insisted that two areas remain off-limits: mass surveillance of Americans and fully autonomous weapons.
- The standoff escalated, with the Pentagon designating Anthropic a national security and supply-chain risk, leading the AI company to sue.
## Anthropic's response and what comes next

"We welcome the court's ruling that this supply chain risk designation was unlawful," an Anthropic spokesperson said in a statement. "We remain focused on working productively with the government to harness AI for our national security so all Americans benefit from this technology."

On what's next, Axios gives two points: the government is expected to appeal the ruling, and Anthropic is also fighting a separate Pentagon designation under a different statute in the D.C. Circuit.

## Evidence, and what we read into it

**What the source establishes.** The district court found the designation unlawful. The judge found a violation of constitutional rights and cited continued collaboration after the designation as inconsistent with the stated fear. The background is a disagreement over scope of use (all lawful purposes versus two carved-out areas). An appeal is expected, and a separate challenge to another designation is pending.

**What we read into it.** The ruling is an example of a court drawing a line between an AI vendor attaching conditions to how its models are used and a government penalizing the vendor for it. But this is a district-court decision, and the picture could change depending on the appeal and the separate case. The source reports the gist of the ruling and the parties' statements; it says nothing about the appeal's prospects or the outcome of the other designation. Treating this single decision as a decisive precedent for procurement generally would be premature.
