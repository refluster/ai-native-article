On 8 October 2026, Samsung Electronics estimated Q3 operating profit of 107.4 trillion won, up 782.5% year on year, and TSMC reported September revenue up 54.6%. Elsewhere, a16z argued that AI agents can now carry customer journeys lasting weeks or months, and an older essay described CFOs stuck waiting for month-end closes. Three unrelated stories, one principle: **the basic unit of value and decision-making is moving from the bounded "period" to the unbroken "continuum."**

## Record numbers have become a late signal

"Samsung operating profit of 107.4 trillion won, TSMC quarterly revenue of NT$1.49 trillion: 8 October, when AI chip demand set records at once" reports that the memory and foundry layers of the AI supply chain hit all-time highs in the same week. The telling detail follows: Samsung shares fell 2.42% that day. A 782.5% jump in operating profit did not move the market upward.

A quarterly result compresses 90 days that are already over into a single figure. If the market is now asking whether demand will last, investors want continuous evidence that demand is still running, not a confirmed total from the past. A period total is priced in by the time it is confirmed. Samsung's drop reads as one observation that period-based information is losing value.

## From single tasks to owned journeys

In "Long-running agents win the relay race: a16z's AI-native Red Ventures," David Haber argues that AI agents have moved from single tasks to customer journeys spanning weeks or months. Red Ventures owned the entire journey and was paid on outcomes; the piece says that model is structurally threatened, and sketches an AI-native successor along with three go-to-market patterns: platform sales, services sales, and full stack.

The unit changes here too. Earlier automation made carved-out tasks cheaper: one inquiry, one slide. If an agent can run for months, competition is no longer over a set of tasks but over the journey itself. The relay metaphor points to the context lost at every baton handoff, which a single runner who keeps running retains. The fewer the breaks, the more accumulated context survives as value.

## Waiting for month-end leaves finance behind

"For the CFO waiting on monthly closes: the four areas of a16z's finance-suite overhaul" covers an essay by Seema Amble and Angela Strange dated 15 April 2020. ERP returns backward-looking figures more than two weeks after month-end, tools sit in silos, and manual re-keying and reconciliation never end. They framed this as an opening for new companies and organised the opportunity into four areas.

The essay is set in the 2020 crisis, but its structure matches the other two. Any mechanism that waits for a boundary (month-end) before confirming will lag decisions whenever conditions move continuously. That finance, the function most loyal to the period, complained first suggests this is not a new phenomenon but an old friction that AI is the first thing able to resolve broadly.

## Objection: is the continuum actually new?

One can object that the shift is not new. The CFO essay dates from April 2020, and the slowness of month-end closes was already recognised. Yet six years on, the market still asks about the durability of Samsung's results, which means the problem stayed unsolved. What changed is the cost of operating continuously: the a16z claim that agents can now handle weeks or months did not describe 2020.

So the principle is old and the feasibility is new. That distinction matters. If only the principle were new, this would be a buzzword; because feasibility changed, organisations, contracts and disclosures optimised for boundaries will be forced to redesign in turn. The three pieces show that pressure arriving from the supply side (agent run-time), the market side (price formation) and the management side (finance) at once.

## The common principle: boundaries become friction, the continuum becomes an asset

Laid side by side, the three facts share one structure.

- Samsung and TSMC: the question of whether demand lasts, not the confirmed quarterly or monthly record, moved the price.
- a16z on agents: owning a journey of weeks or months, not a task, is becoming the unit of business.
- The CFO essay: a month-end figure arrives more than two weeks late.
These facts suggest that the units we use to measure, carry and decide on value, such as periods and tasks, were shaped by what humans could process, and that once AI lowers processing cost, those boundaries themselves become the largest friction. Boundaries were a tool for slicing the world to human capacity. If agents can run continuously, there is less reason to maintain them.

The claim is falsifiable. If the principle holds: (1) price reactions to confirmed earnings shrink as continuously published leading indicators multiply; (2) agent businesses move from per-task pricing to per-journey outcome fees; (3) the competitive axis in finance tooling shifts from shortening the close to eliminating the concept of a close. If quarterly figures keep driving large price moves on each reporting date and agent billing stays per-task, the principle is wrong.

## Predictions and implications

First, industries that rely on period-based disclosure are likely to fall behind rivals with continuous proxy indicators. Confidence is moderate: the grounds are Samsung's share drop despite record profit, but that is a one-day observation, not a trend.

Second, of the three go-to-market patterns a16z describes, full stack, which owns the whole journey, may hold a stronger position than patterns selling carved-out tasks because it retains context. We cannot be sure; markets will remain where platform or services sales win.

Third, a practical step: pick one boundary in your organisation called "monthly," "quarterly" or "weekly review," and write down what it makes wait. If the answer is a decision, the boundary is probably a relic of a design built around human capacity, not a goal. Before investing in preserving it, ask whether the work can be handled continuously.
