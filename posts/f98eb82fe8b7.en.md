## Executive Summary

Stripe has agreed to acquire OpenRouter, the AI model-routing service, for more than $7 billion, according to Bloomberg. OpenRouter connects roughly 8 million developers to more than 400 AI models through a single API and collects about 5% of the inference spend that passes through it — and Stripe was already processing OpenRouter's payments. Folding the routing decision and the billing into one company puts pressure on the premise OpenRouter has built its pitch on: neutrality between models.

## The size of the deal and what it says about the valuation

Per Bloomberg, Stripe agreed to pay more than $7 billion for OpenRouter. The startup had raised $113 million just three months earlier at a reported $1.3 billion valuation. The Wall Street Journal had reported talks last month at a figure closer to $10 billion, so the final number landed lower than where negotiations started. By Sacra's estimate, OpenRouter's annualized revenue was around $50 million in March — putting the $7 billion price tag at roughly 50 times revenue, a multiple nobody pays for cash flow alone. For Stripe, it's more than six times what it paid for stablecoin firm Bridge in 2025, and its largest acquisition to date.

## The position Stripe is actually buying

OpenRouter owns no GPUs and trains no models. Its business is the position itself: sitting between about 8 million developers and more than 400 AI models, offering one API key instead of a dozen separate integrations, and taking roughly 5% of the inference spend that flows through it. Stripe already handled OpenRouter's invoicing and tax, so this deal is a vendor buying its own customer. With the routing decision and the invoice now sitting inside the same company, Stripe also gains a live read on enterprise AI spending across every major lab at once.

This is not an isolated move. Stripe has previously acquired stablecoin firm Bridge, wallet-infrastructure company Privy, and — in January 2026 — usage-based billing startup Metronome, already used by both OpenAI and Anthropic. It has also co-built Tempo, whose Machine Payments Protocol lets AI agents request, authorize, and settle payments without a human in the loop. The OpenRouter acquisition extends that pattern, pulling AI usage metering and payment infrastructure into a single pipeline Stripe owns end to end.

## The neutrality at stake, and a geopolitical wrinkle

OpenRouter's pitch has always rested on indifference: route to whatever model clears the developer's budget, latency, and quality bar, with no stake in which model wins. That was easy to believe when the owners were venture funds with no AI product of their own. It reads differently once the owner also sells payment services to the labs whose models it's routing traffic to.

There's a geopolitical dimension underneath. U.S.-origin models fell from roughly 70% of OpenRouter's token volume in mid-2025 to about 30% a year later, with cheap Chinese open-weight models absorbing the difference. Whoever sets the routing defaults has a hand on that dial.

## What it means for the labs, and the Fusion API behind it

Every time a developer swaps an expensive flagship model for a cheaper one that still clears the bar, the router captures a sliver of value and the lab loses a little pricing power. OpenRouter has been sharpening that dynamic itself. Its Fusion API fans a single prompt across a panel of budget models and merges their answers. On DRACO, Perplexity's benchmark of 100 real deep-research tasks graded by an AI judge against expert rubrics and normalized to a percentage score, a panel of Gemini 3 Flash, Kimi K2.6, and DeepSeek V4 Pro scored 64.7% — beating solo GPT-5.5 at 60% and solo Claude Opus 4.8 at 58.8%. Stripe now owns that mechanism too.

Neither company has publicly confirmed the deal, and no regulatory review timeline has been disclosed. Stripe processed $1.9 trillion in payment volume in 2025 and was valued at $159 billion in a February 2026 tender offer. At over $7 billion, the fight over who owns the AI infrastructure "plumbing" is quietly being rewritten from an adjacent layer: payments.
