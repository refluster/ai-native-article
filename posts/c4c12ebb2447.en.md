## Executive Summary

In an essay posted on July 7, 2026, Decagon CEO Jesse Zhang highlighted a paradox: even as mature AI deployments are shifting to lightweight, open-source models, total spending on frontier models has barely declined. Real-world data from Vercel and OpenRouter supports this “two-tier structure”: open-source models such as DeepSeek lead in token volume processed, while Anthropic still accounts for more than half of spending. This division of labor — frontier models for “discovery,” open source for “production” — may become a durable feature of the AI economy for the time being.

## Zhang’s Argument: Not Competition, but Two Stages in the Same Lifecycle

The starting point is an essay published by Decagon CEO Jesse Zhang under the title “Everyone is wrong about open source AI in the enterprise.” Zhang acknowledges that mature AI deployments, including at his own company Decagon, are switching to lighter-weight models, while arguing that total spending on expensive cutting-edge models has barely moved.

- Mature use cases are shifting to lighter-weight models, even as new use cases continue to emerge one after another.
- As a result, total spending on frontier models does not fall.
In Zhang’s reading, frontier models and open-source models are not competitors. They are two phases in the same lifecycle: expensive frontier models prove out use cases, and once those use cases mature, they are handed off to cheaper open-source substitutes. The core of his argument is that the very premise of a zero-sum struggle is wrong.

## Real-World Data ①: Vercel AI Gateway — DeepSeek Leads in Volume, Anthropic Still Takes More Than Half of Spending

Zhang himself offers little hard data to support the claim, but writer Russell Brandom draws corroborating evidence from public dashboards. According to Vercel’s AI Gateway dashboard, over the past week DeepSeek surged into first place by token volume processed, handling more than one-third of all tokens passing through the company’s infrastructure. Z.ai, the lab known for GLM-5.2, also climbed to 4th place over the same period.

- Token volume processed: DeepSeek is in first place, with a little over one-third of all traffic moving through Vercel infrastructure.
- Z.ai (developer of GLM-5.2) jumped sharply to 4th place over the same period.
- But when you look at total spending, Anthropic still accounts for more than half of all AI spending across the platform (over 50%).
Anthropic’s share has slipped slightly over the past month, but the main reason is Anthropic’s own price increase, not any major decline. What emerges here is a split between the model that leads in “volume” and the model that leads in “dollars.”

## Real-World Data ②: OpenRouter — Usage Goes to V4 Flash, Spending to Opus 4.8

OpenRouter’s rankings, which capture a broader market (and one somewhat less enterprise-heavy), tell a similar story. The biggest winner by usage is DeepSeek V4 Flash, which processes 5.3 trillion tokens per week. By contrast, Opus 4.8 — the most popular frontier model — handles only a little over 2 trillion tokens weekly.

- Usage: DeepSeek V4 Flash is the largest at 5.3 trillion tokens per week.
- The most popular frontier model, Opus 4.8, handles a little over 2 trillion tokens per week.
- OpenRouter does not rank models by total spending, but it records Opus 4.8’s average price per token as about 23 times that of V4 Flash ($1.37 per 1 million tokens versus just 6 cents).
Given that price gap, it is reasonable to infer that Opus still captures the bulk of spending. And these figures do not yet include Nvidia’s newest entrant, Nemotron. Nemotron is seen as being in position to vault quickly to the front, thanks both to Nvidia’s powerful relationships and to the model’s own high adaptability. Preserving that unit-price premium is the foundation of frontier labs’ revenue base.

## Implications: A Two-Tier Economy of “Frontier for Discovery, Open Source for Production”

These numbers do not fully prove Zhang’s lifecycle thesis, but they do show that frontier labs have not been hurt all that much by the rise of open source — at least not yet. Zhang puts it this way: “The frontier labs will keep owning discovery. Open source will increasingly own production.”

The article offers two explanations for that resilience.

- The market for AI-addressable tasks is expanding rapidly, so top models can maintain their position simply by dominating early-stage deployments.
- Some use cases are so difficult that they cannot be fully replaced by cheaper alternatives.
Brandom also revisits a prediction he made in September 2025: that foundation labs would end up “selling coffee beans to Starbucks” — in other words, relegated to commodity-like inputs while the application layer reaped the rewards. Part of that prediction has come true. Vertically specialized AI has switched to lighter-weight models, and the economics of “GPT wrapper” startups have remained broadly stable. But on a per-token basis, frontier providers have continued to hold the most desirable part of the market: premium-priced tokens. And there is little sign that this structure will change anytime soon. This economy, split into two layers in which usage and spending accrue to different players, may end up becoming not a temporary paradox in the AI market but a relatively stable feature.
