## Executive Summary

According to the Financial Times, Amazon is seeking to offload about $8 billion of advanced Nvidia chips to external investors, moving them into a special purpose vehicle (SPV) that Amazon would then lease back. The report reached us through an Investing.com wire item dated October 2, 2026 that cites the FT, so this is second-hand reporting. It reads as an attempt to take the largest cost of AI data centers, the chips, off the balance sheet.

## The reported structure

The source states the following.

- Amazon is seeking to offload about $8 billion of advanced Nvidia chips to external investors (FT, citing people familiar with the matter).
- It has held talks with investors in recent weeks to gauge interest.
- The plan is to spin off thousands of Grace Blackwell chips into a special purpose vehicle.
- Amazon would lease the chips back from the vehicle. The chips are being used in data centers across the U.S.
- The vehicle would then tap outside investors through debt issuance, the FT reported.
- Amazon also plans to offer an equity stake of up to 10% in the vehicle.
One passage in the source is hard to read. The 10% stake is followed by the remark that this indicates "the company will not hold any stake in the entity", and the source does not reconcile that with Amazon offering a stake of up to 10%. The ownership split cannot be settled from this text; the FT's original is the place to check. This article goes no further than the source's wording.

## The aim: a lighter balance sheet

The source says the measure is aimed at strengthening Amazon's balance sheet by unloading expensive chip costs to investors and adopting a more asset-light approach.

For context, it describes a broader push by U.S. hyperscalers to find less balance-sheet-heavy means of financing their data center buildout, noting that a bulk of data center costs comes from the advanced chips used to train AI models.

The source also gives scale. Amazon said it will spend over $200 billion in capital expenditure this year, a bulk of which will go toward its Web Services cloud unit to buy more chips and build more data centers. Against that, $8 billion is a fraction.

## Reading it: facts versus implications

What follows is inference drawn from the facts above, not claims from the source.

1. **Financing is shifting from the building to the chip.** Carving out the chips and leasing them back makes the chips themselves the basis of the financing. The source names a concrete asset: thousands of Grace Blackwell chips. 2. **A leaseback means Amazon keeps using the compute.** Per the source the chips stay in U.S. data centers. What changes is who owns them and where the money comes from, not the use. 3. **For investors, the instrument is debt tied to AI infrastructure.** The source says only that the vehicle would raise debt; it gives no yield or maturity.

## Limits

This piece rests on a single second-hand report. Terms (yield, tenor, who the investors are), any formal Amazon announcement, and the final equity split are not in the source, and the deal is not confirmed to close. We treat it as a reported plan, not as an executed transaction.
