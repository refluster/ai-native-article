The same capability acts as a complement in one place and a substitute in another. What separates the two is not the size of the capability but whether a loop closes on the far side of it.

Shopify's earnings call shows AI-driven traffic tripling while conventional search has not declined. A two-year study of 100 Indian technology firms found that what moved survival by ten points was not sophisticated HR technology but three unremarkable practices. And US data centres are projected to demand 194 gigawatts by 2035 — roughly a fifth of national electricity consumption. The three look unrelated. They are in fact explained by one principle.

## The same AI search adds on one side and subtracts on the other

The figures reported in "AI Search Is Not Replacing Google — What Shopify's Q2 Earnings Reveal About 'Complement'" look unremarkable in isolation. In Q2 2026, AI-driven traffic and orders at Shopify tripled year over year. At the same time, conventional search sessions grew 1.3x over two years and still account for roughly a third of all storefront sessions. Shopify president Harley Finkelstein characterised this as "a complement to search, not a replacement."

The numbers matter because there is a comparison case. As the same piece notes, **that identical AI search is depressing click-through rates in online publishing**. One technology, opposite outcomes. So the difference does not live in the technology. It lives in what happens after the AI returns an answer.

In publishing, the AI's answer *is* the deliverable. The reader's need is met, no click occurs, and a click that never occurred is observed by no one. In commerce, the answer is not the deliverable. The need is met only when an order lands, and that order is recorded in the merchant's own books. **One path terminates; the other returns to the far side as a purchase.** Finkelstein can say "complement" because he holds the record of what came back.

## What made delegation work was not technology but the round trip

"Three Practices That Separated Delegating From Abandoning — the 28% Gap in a Two-Year Study of 100 Indian Tech Firms" shows the same shape in an entirely different domain. Researchers from Duke University, Stanford Graduate School of Business and Harvard Business School tracked the founders of 100 Indian technology companies for roughly two years. Firms whose executives received advice on formal people management employed 28% more people, saw failure probability fall by ten points, and survival rise from 75% to 85%.

What is striking is what did *not* work. The difference was not advanced HR technology. Three things carried it: set clear goals, check progress on a regular cadence, and give feedback that makes the next action obvious.

Laid side by side, those three are not features but a **structure**. The goal is information going out; the progress check is information coming back; the feedback is information going out again in light of it. Only all three together complete a circuit. What the advice added was not executive capability. It closed the circuit on the far side of the delegation. That is why the study can distinguish delegating from abandoning: one has a loop, the other does not. The capability is identical in both.

## Intelligence has no terminating condition; power does

The third piece, "Data Centre Power Demand Reaches 194 Gigawatts by 2035 — Inside the Claim That Power Is AI's Next Bottleneck," appears to be about something else entirely. The Kobeissi Letter projects that US data centre power demand will rise 253% from 2026 levels to 194 gigawatts by 2035. Data centres would then account for about 20% of US electricity consumption, with the load concentrated in a handful of grid regions, PJM foremost among them. Its conclusion: AI's next constraint is not model intelligence but power.

Why is the constraint not intelligence? Because intelligence has no terminating condition. Whether a model has become smarter can be restated indefinitely by moving the definition of smart; there is no physical instant at which one declares it sufficient. Power is different. The watts demanded are either supplied or not supplied, in a specific region, at a specific hour. **And they come back as a bill.** The constraint moved to power less because power became scarce than because power is the variable that closes. The 253% and the 194 gigawatts are meaningful as forecasts precisely because the place where they close is known to be a handful of regions.

## The common principle: value settles on the side where the loop closes

These facts suggest the following. **Whether a new capability becomes a complement or a substitute is determined not by its strength but by whether an observable loop closes where the capability lands.**

In commerce, the loop closes. A visitor the AI brought lands as an order, the merchant counts it, and because it was counted the next investment decision can be made. So the same AI search is booked as a complement. In publishing, the loop does not close. The need is satisfied inside the answer, nothing returns, and the added convenience appears on no one's ledger while the lost clicks are plainly visible. So the same technology appears as a substitute.

Delegation is the same. The three practices build a path by which the state of delegated work returns. Because it returns, it can be corrected; because it can be corrected, the delegated scope can be widened. The 28% employment gap reads less as a gap in the quality of decisions than as a gap in how many times a decision could be updated. Without the loop, delegation is a one-shot bet.

So does power. Intelligence does not close; power does. Only closing variables become planning objects, and the moment something becomes a planning object it stands up as the rate-limiting step.

The principle is falsifiable. If AI search gave publishers an attribution path for revenue that does not run through a click — if in-answer citations converted into measurable consideration — then publishing should also start posting "complement" numbers. Conversely, if order attribution in commerce became ambiguous and merchants could no longer hold AI-driven revenue as their own record, the same technology would begin to be described as a substitute there too. The claim predicts that the conclusion reverses when the recording path changes, with the technology held constant.

## What follows, and what to do about it

Take the principle as given and several consequences come into view.

First, **most arguments about AI's impact are arguments about measurement paths, not about capability.** The question "did AI have an effect" is, in most current usage, indistinguishable from "did the effect land on somebody's ledger." The gap between Shopify and the publishers demonstrates it. Over the next two to three years, cases of two industries reaching opposite conclusions from the same model will multiply. What to examine then is not the model but whether there is somewhere for the result to return. Confidence here is high: the evidence is that opposite observations already exist for one technology.

Second, **whether "delegate it to an agent" works inside an organisation is likewise decided by the design of the round trip, not by capability.** What the Indian study showed about human delegation transfers directly to delegation to agents. Automation lacking explicit goals, a regular return of progress, and feedback that names the next action falls on the abandonment side no matter how far capability rises. The converse also holds: an organisation that implements the three dull practices gets results from an unremarkable model. The practical implication is blunt — building the return path is cheaper than upgrading the model.

Third, **the constraint migrates, in order, to whichever variable closes next.** Power was named as the next constraint because it was the first variable whose satisfaction could be adjudicated unambiguously. Even if power is solved, what becomes rate-limiting next is not intelligence but the following closing variable — land, transformers, cooling water, transmission capacity, or the consent of the host region. The observation that 194 gigawatts concentrate in a handful of regions should be read exactly that way: the place where it closes has been identified.

For a reader, the takeaway reduces to one practice. When evaluating a new capability, before counting what it can do, **write down one way its results return to you**. If you cannot write the return path, that adoption will arrive not as a complement but eventually as a substitute eating your own numbers. If you can write it, the gains accumulate even from a mediocre capability. There is no other difference.
