## Executive Summary

After a limited preview, OpenAI has begun general availability of the GPT-5.6 family, consisting of the flagship “Sol,” the everyday-work model “Terra,” and the lowest-cost “Luna.” The company’s core claim is not absolute performance alone, but “results per token” and “results per dollar,” arguing that Sol outperforms competing frontier models with fewer tokens and at lower cost. It has also introduced a new `ultra` setting that runs multiple agents in parallel by default, making execution efficiency a central product differentiator.

## Three Model Tiers and New Execution Settings

GPT-5.6 adopts a naming system that separates the generation number (5.6) from capability tiers (Sol/Terra/Luna) that evolve on their own update cadence.

- **Sol** — The flagship. Aims for top-tier performance in coding, knowledge work, cybersecurity, and science.
- **Terra** — A model for everyday work that delivers performance comparable to GPT-5.5 at lower cost.
- **Luna** — The fastest and least expensive model.
- As execution settings, `max` allows even longer reasoning time than `xhigh`, while `ultra` coordinates 4 agents in parallel by default (with 16-agent configurations also presented for BrowseComp and SEC-Bench Pro). In the API, the same capability is offered as the multi-agent beta in the Responses API.
In short, OpenAI is shifting its pitch from “how smart a single model is” to “shortening time-to-completion through parallel agent orchestration.” On BrowseComp, SEC-Bench Pro, and Terminal-Bench 2.1, it says that adding parallel agents pushed the score-versus-latency frontier up and to the left—toward higher scores in less time.

## Benchmark Claims Framed Around “Performance per Dollar”

The company foregrounds efficiency ratios rather than absolute scores. The following figures are drawn from the main text and appendix tables.

- **Agents' Last Exam** (a long-horizon work evaluation across 55 domains): in the main text, Sol is said to set a new high at 53.6, beating Claude Fable 5 (adaptive reasoning) by 13.1 points. Even at a medium reasoning setting, it exceeds Fable 5 by 11.4 points at roughly 1/4 the estimated cost. In the appendix table, Sol is 52.7% and Fable 5 is 40.5%.
- **Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index**: at maximum reasoning, Sol is within 1 point of Fable 5, while estimated completion time is 61% shorter and estimated cost is about half. In appendix table (v4.1), Sol is 58.9 and Fable 5 is 59.9.
- **Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index**: at maximum reasoning, Sol sets a new record at 80 (+2.8 versus Fable 5), with less than half the output tokens, less than half the time, and about 1/3 lower cost. Terra at 77.4 is said to edge out Fable 5, while Luna at 74.6 is said to surpass Opus 4.8.
- On **OSWorld 2.0**, Sol posts 62.6%, outperforming Opus 4.8 while cutting output tokens by 85%. On **BrowseComp**, it reaches as high as 92.2%.
The core of the argument is improved performance per dollar: “more results for the same spend, or similar results at lower cost.”

## Cybersecurity and Safety Measures

The article presents capability gains in dual-use areas alongside strengthened defensive controls to match them.

- **ExploitBench**: 73.5% (GPT-5.5 was 47.9%, at an equivalent token budget).
- **ExploitGym**: with a 2-hour limit, nearly doubling GPT-5.5’s peak 15.1% to 24.9%; at 6 hours, 33.7%.
- **SEC-Bench Pro**: 71.2% (GPT-5.5 was 45.8%).
- On the safety side, Sol’s cyber protections block about 10x more potentially harmful activity than previous versions. Before general availability, the company conducted about 700,000 A100e GPU-hours of black-box automated red teaming.
OpenAI says GPT-5.6 is more capable than prior models in both biology and cyber, but “does not exceed the Critical threshold.” It also argues that overblocking can itself hinder defensive validation, and says it has adopted a design that layers reasoning monitors that inspect the conversation itself rather than relying only on classifiers.

## Availability, Pricing, and the Acceleration of Internal Research

- **Availability**: starting today in ChatGPT, Codex, and the OpenAI API. Rollout has begun globally and will proceed gradually over 24 hours to full availability.
- **Pricing** (per 1 million tokens): Sol input $5 / output $30, Terra $2.50 / $15, Luna $1 / $6. Caching introduces a minimum retention of 30 minutes and explicit cache breakpoints; writes are billed at 1.25x non-cached input, while reads are discounted 90%.
- **Internal use**: during the internal testing period, average daily output tokens per researcher were more than double the peak level under GPT-5.5. Over the past 6 months, compute resources for internal coding reasoning increased 100x, and internal agent-like token usage rose about 22x. On a composite evaluation of recursive self-improvement (RSI), GPT-5.6 improved by 16.2 points versus GPT-5.5.
These adoption metrics do not themselves measure research progress directly, but they are presented as indicators of how rapidly agent use is expanding inside the model-development loop.
