## Introduction

OpenAI’s model contributed to disproving a conjecture in discrete geometry. At I/O 2026, Google overhauled the Gemini app into “Daily Brief,” “Gemini Spark,” and “Gemini Omni,” making its challenge to ChatGPT and Claude unmistakable. And in individual development and software engineering, people are now talking about redesigning workflow itself on the assumption that AI assistance is always present. At first glance, mathematics research, AI product competition, and day-to-day development practice may seem unrelated. But in fact, all of them can be explained by the same principle. **The axis of AI competition is moving from “answer generation” to “cognitive infrastructure that binds together exploration and execution.”**

## Analysis 1: What happened in mathematical research is the clearest possible sign of AI’s changing role

### What the fact of disproof tells us

The important point in the first article is not simply that “AI solved mathematics.” More fundamentally, **OpenAI’s model “helped disprove a conjecture” in discrete geometry**. More than proof automation, searching for counterexamples is the work of scanning a vast candidate space and finding constructions that humans are likely to overlook. Here, the value of AI lies not in producing a single correct answer, but in **compressing the search space and surfacing anomalous candidates**.

The core phrasing in the article summary is “*helped disprove a conjecture in discrete geometry*,” and it is further framed as “*AI’s growing role in advanced mathematical research*.” Those two points show that AI is no longer just a device for replaying learned knowledge; it is beginning to be integrated into the research process itself. The fact that this shift has been confirmed in a domain like mathematics, where verifiability is high, is significant. That is because counterexamples can ultimately be confirmed by humans and by formal verification, which means AI’s contribution can be measured not as a matter of “vibes,” but as a concrete result.

### From research support to part of the research process

These facts suggest that AI is shifting roles from a “support tool” to a “search engine.” Conventional uses of AI centered on local optimization: literature summaries, code completion, and routine proof assistance. But contributing to the discovery of a disproof means AI has entered the entire research loop of problem formulation → candidate generation → verification → iteration.

What matters is that the kind of performance required here is hard to capture with simple benchmark scores. In mathematical research, what matters is not one perfect answer, but **the ability to preserve promising paths out of 100 or 1000 attempts**. This has the same structure as Google’s product overhaul and the AI-first redesign of development workflows discussed below.

## Analysis 2: Google’s Gemini overhaul is evidence that AI apps are moving from “general-purpose chat” to “contextualized ongoing support”

### What “Daily Brief,” “Spark,” and “Omni” mean

The headline for the Google I/O 2026 article puts three elements side by side: **“Daily Brief,” “Gemini Spark,” and “Gemini Omni.”** Even if the detailed specifications are still limited, the naming itself tells us the direction.

- **Daily Brief**: daily information organization, prioritization, proactive summaries
- **Gemini Spark**: idea stimulation, concept generation, drafting
- **Gemini Omni**: integrated support across multiple modes and multiple contexts
In other words, Google is trying to redefine AI not around one-off Q&A, but around three points of contact: **the flow of the day, the initial burst of ideation, and multimodal integration**. The article summary also says it is “*competing more directly with OpenAI's ChatGPT and Anthropic's Claude*,” which suggests this is not just a UI update, but a reset in competitive strategy.

### The competitive axis is moving from model quality to “time-on-task design”

These facts suggest that the main battleground in the AI app market is shifting from “which one gives smarter answers” to “which one can most frequently and most naturally insert itself into the user’s decision-making loop.” Daily Brief targets the morning routine, Spark the moment of getting started, and Omni cross-functional support during active work. In other words, Google is trying to connect AI to its existing assets—search, mail, calendar, documents, and more—and turn it into a **resident cognitive layer**.

The number that matters here is not a published performance metric, but the fact that **the points of contact have split into 3 distinct categories**. Instead of one “all-purpose chat window,” Google has separated AI into three layers: daily, generative, and integrative. That implies an understanding that user value arises not from the abstract performance of the model, but from workflow design optimized for each situation. ChatGPT and Claude remain strong, but through this app reorganization Google is trying to capture not just usage, but the very **mode of use**.

## Analysis 3: Redesigning development workflows around AI shows that the production function of the software industry has changed

### Not coding assistance, but process redesign

The third article centers on the “**redesign of software creation workflows on the assumption of AI-assisted development**.” What matters here is the phrase not “using AI,” but “assuming AI assistance.” This is not the idea of bolting AI onto an existing process after the fact. It is a declaration that every stage—requirements definition, design, implementation, review, testing, and documentation—should be reorganized on the assumption that AI is a constant participant.

This shift in thinking brings practical changes such as the following:

- Structuring specifications sufficiently in natural language before implementation
- Preparing review prompts and evaluation criteria before code generation
- Increasing small task decomposition and cutting work into units that are easier to delegate to AI
- Prioritizing the speed of the verification loop over the “speed of writing”
This is not mere efficiency improvement. It means that **the bottleneck in software development is shifting from writing ability to the ability to eliminate ambiguity**.

### Human work shifts from “implementation” to “constraint definition”

These facts suggest that high productivity in the AI era will depend not on an individual’s coding speed, but on **the ability to design good questions, good constraints, and good evaluation criteria**. If AI can generate implementation candidates in large volume, then the source of value shifts to deciding “what to build,” “what is sufficient to satisfy the goal,” and “which parts humans must retain final responsibility for.”

This structure corresponds both to mathematical counterexample search and to Google’s Daily Brief. In all cases, even if AI is not the final decision-maker, it dramatically increases the speed of candidate generation, organization, prioritization, and iterative trial. As a result, the center of work shifts from “producing the answer” to “designing the environment in which answers are likely to emerge.”

## Presenting the common principle

So what deeper principle can explain these three sets of facts in a unified way? My inductive hypothesis is this:

### Hypothesis: The essential value of AI lies not in substituting for knowledge, but in becoming cognitive infrastructure that continuous-izes “exploration, selection, and execution”

In OpenAI’s mathematics case, AI did not speak a finished truth; it functioned as a search mechanism for finding counterexample candidates. In Google’s Gemini overhaul, AI was embedded not in one-off chat, but in continuous touchpoints: daily summaries, ideation support, and multimodal integration. In AI-first development workflows, AI became not a code generator, but part of a process that moves back and forth from requirements to implementation and review. **These facts suggest that AI’s competitive advantage is shifting away from “the ability to produce the right answer once” and toward “the ability to generate many candidates, select among them according to context, and connect them to the next action.”**

From this principle, the “Why” becomes clear. Most real-world knowledge work is not about outputting textbook-correct answers. It is iterative work: generating candidates under ambiguous goals, comparing them, revising them, implementing them, and verifying them. Generative AI can enter each part of that cycle and reduce friction. That is why value resides less in the standalone model and more in **context retention, history use, external tool integration, and verification-loop design**.

And the “So What” is even more important. The winners in future AI products and AI adoption will not be the companies with the single smartest model, but the ones that **most deeply redesign the user’s exploration loop**. The changes now happening simultaneously in mathematics, products, and development practice are early signs of that shift.

## Future predictions and implications

The future that follows from this principle is fairly concrete.

### Prediction 1: AI products will evolve from “chat apps” into “work OSes” (70% probability)

The basis for this is Google’s functional split of Gemini into “Daily Brief,” “Spark,” and “Omni.” That reflects the judgment that capturing the entire daily workflow offers more competitive advantage than providing a single conversation window. Over the next 2-3 years, major AI products are likely to become deeply integrated with mail, calendars, IDEs, documents, meetings, and task management, and the chat UI will become only one entry point among many.

**Implication**: Companies should not stop at “rolling out an internal chatbot” as their AI strategy. They should redesign work at the level of touchpoints: pre-meeting summaries, spec creation, reviews, daily reports, search, and decision logs.

### Prediction 2: The definition of high-value talent will shift from “implementation skill” to “the ability to define verifiable problems” (80% probability)

In OpenAI’s mathematics case, the value of AI lay in discovering counterexample candidates, but turning that into value still required human verification and human understanding of the problem. In development workflows as well, the more AI generates, the more specification ambiguity and weak evaluation criteria become the bottleneck. Accordingly, in the talent market ahead, what will rise in value is not simply people who can write, but **people who can define constraints, articulate evaluation criteria, and correctly reject AI output**.

**Implication**: Engineers and PMs should strengthen, before prompt technique, their ability to verbalize requirements definition, acceptance criteria, test design, and review perspectives.

### Prediction 3: Gaps in research and development speed will appear less as model gaps and more as “loop design gaps” (75% probability)

Even using the same model, outcomes will differ dramatically depending on iteration count, the granularity of task decomposition, automation of verification, and how history is used. That is equally true in development teams and research organizations. The more AI is democratized, the more the gap will reside not in access, but in operational design.

**Implication**: There are 3 practical things readers should do now.

1. Break your work down into “exploration,” “selection,” “execution,” and “verification.” 1. Clarify which parts of each stage should be entrusted to AI, and which parts humans remain responsible for. 1. Build mechanisms to accumulate history, rules, and evaluation criteria, rather than relying on one-off use.

In the end, the disproof in mathematics, Gemini’s reorganization, and AI-first development are all pointing to the same future. **AI is changing from a tool that returns clever answers into a foundation that amplifies the trial and error of knowledge work.** Only the companies and individuals who understand that shift will be able to ride the next productivity curve.
