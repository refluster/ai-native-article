## Executive Summary

After the U.S. midterm elections, fears of tighter regulation of AI data centers triggered heavy selling in technology stocks in the first week of November. Morgan Stanley called the decline "clearly an overreaction": roughly 5GW of U.S. data center capacity originally slated for 2028 may slip to 2029, but it has not been canceled, and hyperscaler spending plans are largely unchanged. The open question is whether the delay stays a matter of timing or turns into cutbacks if regulatory pressure exceeds expectations.

## What happened (facts from the source)

- After the election, investors worried that AI data centers would face a stricter regulatory environment, and technology stocks were hit by a fierce selloff in the first week of November.
- In a new research report, Morgan Stanley said the slump in AI-related stocks is "clearly an overreaction" and that the broad direction of AI capital spending has not changed.
- By its estimate, roughly 5GW of U.S. data center capacity originally scheduled for completion in 2028 may be pushed back to 2029, but it will not be scrapped wholesale.
- According to Reuters, several Wall Street investment banks spoke out in succession on Monday, November 9, arguing that investors' reaction to AI policy risk exceeded a reasonable range and that a full-scale restriction on data center expansion is unlikely.
## The banks' views

| Institution | View | |---|---| | Morgan Stanley | Selloff is overdone; data centers delayed, not canceled; bullish on Amazon, Microsoft, Meta, Alphabet | | UBS | Investors overly pessimistic on regulation, credit risk and capex; fundamentals have not fundamentally changed | | HSBC | Added to U.S. and Asian tech stocks during the correction; sees Anthropic's expected IPO as an important catalyst in the coming weeks | | Jefferies | The sharp drop on November 5 was a sentiment reaction, not a fundamental repricing |

Jefferies analyst Jane Gibbons noted that after single-day drops of more than 4% in the S&P 500, the index has risen an average of 11.9% over the following six months and more than 20% over the following year.

## How to read the slippage

Morgan Stanley argues that this fine-tuning of construction pace could ease short-term supply chain bottlenecks, support computing power pricing, and extend visibility into the overall AI investment cycle. It says the market has over-interpreted election variables as a structural headwind while ignoring the cloud giants' solid cash flows and rigid demand for computing power.

Beyond the four cloud names, the bank also named Nvidia, Broadcom, Seagate, Western Digital, and optical communications players Coherent and Lumentum as offering investment value.

## Conclusion: what would settle it

The source frames the fork plainly. If the delay is a shift in timing rather than a disappearance of demand, the long-term growth trajectory of the cloud giants and the chip supply chain remains intact. If regulatory pressure exceeds expectations, delays in capital spending could turn into cutbacks. The source says earnings reports and policy signals must be watched closely for this variable, and names Nvidia's quarterly earnings on November 17 and Anthropic's IPO progress as the next tests.

A caveat on the evidence: the article is a secondary compilation of investment bank research and market coverage, and the 5GW figure is a stated possibility, not a confirmed change to any project plan.
