## Executive Summary

On Thursday, July 30, 2026, Okta agreed to acquire AI identity security startup Permiso Security. Okta has not disclosed the terms of the deal, but TechCrunch reports, citing sources familiar with the transaction, that it values Permiso at just under $200 million and is structured as almost entirely cash. What this acquisition signals is that the center of gravity in the identity management industry is shifting from “verifying users at login” to “continuously monitoring what users, applications, and AI agents do after they have been granted access.”

## Deal Terms and Timing — What Was Disclosed, and What Wasn’t

It is important to distinguish between the facts that were officially announced and the figures that came through sources.

- **What Okta disclosed**: The fact that it has agreed to the acquisition, and that the deal is expected to close in the **third quarter of fiscal year 2027**, subject to customary closing conditions.
- **What Okta did not disclose**: The transaction terms.
- **What TechCrunch learned from sources**: A valuation of “just under $200 million,” with an “almost all-cash” structure.
What is notable is Okta’s response. When TechCrunch asked CEO Todd McKinnon for comment, an Okta spokesperson did **not deny** the $200 million figure. However, the company declined to comment on the details of the deal terms. In other words, the number is not part of the official announcement, but neither has the company actively pushed back on it.

## What Permiso Brings — Visibility Into What Happens After Authorization

Permiso emerged from stealth in 2022. It was co-founded by two former FireEye executives, **Paul Nguyen** and **Jason Martin**, and is headquartered in Palo Alto, California.

The company’s product operates not in front of authentication, but behind it.

- **Core function**: Detecting suspicious behavior in cloud environments **after** users and applications have been granted access.
- **Specialty area**: Detecting attacks that use stolen or compromised credentials to move laterally through cloud infrastructure.
- **Recent expansion**: Extending the platform to monitor AI agents and other machine identities.
- **SandyClaw**: A platform announced in April 2026 that analyzes AI agent skills in a sandbox environment and identifies malicious behavior **before deployment**.
Okta Chief Product Officer Ely Kahn said in a statement: “Permiso extends Okta's identity security fabric with proven identity threat detection and response capabilities, and an exceptional threat research and security team that advances Okta’s threat detection and prevention capabilities.”

## Why “Machine Identity” Has Become the Battleground

The industry backdrop described here is clear. Identity management vendors are trying to expand beyond simply verifying users at login and move toward **continuously monitoring what users, applications, and AI agents do after authorization**.

What is driving this shift is the spread of autonomous software across the enterprise. Okta is effectively betting that “as enterprises embed AI more deeply into their day-to-day operations, demand will grow for protecting AI agents and other machine identities.” That is the area where competition is intensifying.

This is where SandyClaw’s role becomes important. Its design—analyzing agent skills in a sandbox **before deployment**—adds a layer of pre-launch review, not just runtime anomaly detection, making it a complement to the company’s core post-authorization monitoring capabilities.

## The Valuation Trajectory Shows the Scale of the Repricing

Set Permiso’s fundraising history against the value of this transaction, and the scale of the repricing in this category becomes clear.

- Permiso has raised about **$29 million** in total.
- Of that, its **Series A** in April 2024 was **$18.5 million**, led by Altimeter Capital.
- According to people familiar with the fundraising, that Series A valued the company at roughly **$80 million post-money**.
- This acquisition values the company at **just under $200 million**.
In a little over two years since the Series A, Permiso’s valuation moved from roughly $80 million post-money to just under $200 million. It is also a sizable multiple relative to the company’s total funding of $29 million. What the numbers suggest is that monitoring AI agents has been valued not as a category best left to independent fundraising rounds, but as something to be folded into an existing identity management platform sooner rather than later. This acquisition is being positioned as a move that strengthens Okta’s push to protect AI agents and non-human identities, alongside its core identity management business.
