## Executive Summary

On September 23, 2026, OpenAI CEO Sam Altman, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and Hugging Face co-founder Clément Delangue briefed the UN Security Council on the risks of increasingly powerful AI systems, with Amodei warning that "if managed poorly," AI "could be a risk to humanity as a whole." The same UN General Assembly session saw President Trump reject any "globalist scheme to control" AI, making the split between the industry's own risk warnings and the White House's anti-regulation stance visible on the Security Council floor itself.

## Executives sound the alarm before the Council

According to Reuters, as carried by The Express Tribune, the 15-member UN Security Council held a briefing on AI during the 81st UN General Assembly on September 23, chaired by French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot. Among those addressing the Council were three of the industry's most prominent executives: OpenAI's Sam Altman, Anthropic's Dario Amodei, and Hugging Face co-founder Clément Delangue. The briefing came amid warnings that increasingly powerful AI systems could soon improve themselves, slip beyond human control, and pose a threat to international security.

Amodei told the Council: "If managed poorly, I even believe that AI could be a risk to humanity as a whole." Altman framed the concern in governance terms: "If AI is to be democratic, the most important decisions cannot be made by labs in San Francisco alone. They must be shaped through democratic processes and by governments accountable to the people they serve. At the international level, this will require cooperation." He added: "In our history, there have been times when countries who compete and don't always like each other very much still come together for shared interests and the collective good in the face of a powerful new technology. We believe this must be one of those times."

Yoshua Bengio — the Canadian researcher regarded as one of the "Godfathers of AI" and co-chair of the Independent International Scientific Panel created by the UN General Assembly last year — also addressed the Council, saying "the dangers are real and imminent." He warned further: "This council faces an unprecedented threat, one that none of its members would choose, that none can contain alone, and that does not respect the borders we defend."

## A US–China divide, and a quiet channel underneath it

The report notes that the United States and China — the two dominant players in AI development — remain divided over whether the technology needs global guardrails or should be left largely to market forces, even as governments in general struggle to keep regulatory pace with the technology's advance.

At the same time, ahead of a meeting between President Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping in Washington on Thursday, the two countries have reportedly been discussing setting up a notification system covering AI-related incidents that rise to a national-security level. The public rhetoric of division sits alongside a more practical, working-level effort at incident coordination.

## Trump's stance, and friction with the UN Secretary-General

Speaking at the UN General Assembly the day before the Security Council briefing, Trump said he "rejects any attempt to construct a globalist scheme to control" AI, calling the technology "amazing" while noting the US would be careful. He was quoted separately as saying: "We're leading now over China by a lot, and everyone else, and we're going to keep it that way… Whoever wins AI — you have to remember this — and now I say, whoever wins SI, whoever wins superintelligence, wins. That's the group that wins." On the Council briefing itself, Trump said: "We're going to encourage it, not rein it in. We're going to watch it closely through the Department of Justice."

That position stands in contrast to UN Secretary-General António Guterres, who has long pushed for global governance of AI and has warned that rapidly advancing artificial intelligence poses risks that "cannot be ignored" — a stance the report frames as putting him "at odds with Trump."

## Continuity with 2023 and 2024

This was not the Security Council's first engagement with AI risk. The Council first met on the subject in 2023, when China said the technology should not become a "runaway horse" and the United States warned against its use to censor or repress people. A further Security Council meeting on AI took place in 2024, chaired by then-US Secretary of State Antony Blinken.

What distinguishes this latest session is that the industry's own chief executives stood before the Council and used the starkest language yet — "a risk to humanity as a whole" — on the same day that the US president, at the same UN gathering, rejected the very premise of an international control framework. The result is a Security Council record that now holds both warnings in the same breath.
