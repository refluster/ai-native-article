## Executive Summary

On 12 November 2026 Anthropic released Claude Meetings in beta: Claude joins a video call as a visible participant, answers questions asked aloud or in chat, builds docs and whiteboards with the team during the call, and writes the notes and action items. The shift is from a tool that records to a participant that starts work mid-meeting, and the product puts its permission design at the center: it stays out of calls with outside guests by default, uses connected tools only with the asking person's permission, and saves a transcript but not audio.

## What was announced

The source is Anthropic's product-update article "Introducing Claude Meetings" (12 November 2026, five-minute read). It says Claude can now join video calls as a visible participant, follow the conversation, and start work during the meeting. The beta is rolling out on Team and Enterprise plans. Google Meet is the supported platform for now; other video conferencing platforms are "coming soon".

Claude joins in one of two ways: someone adds it, or auto-join is turned on. When it joins, it appears as a participant and posts a notice in the call's chat.

## What Claude does in the meeting

The article lists these behaviors.

- **Answering questions.** The team can ask Claude questions during the meeting, out loud or in chat. By default Claude answers in writing, and it can use connected tools with permission.
- **Building artifacts live.** Claude builds docs, whiteboards or other artifacts while teammates on the invite refine them.
- **Recording.** Claude writes the notes and action items, so no one on the call has to.
In the article's example, a team planning a customer event says, "Claude, start a doc for this plan." Claude writes up what has been said so far, and teammates on the invite edit the plan while the conversation continues. When the budget comes up and someone asks what last year's event cost, Claude asks for approval to look it up, posts the answer in the meeting chat, and compares it to the current plan. When timelines come up, someone asks Claude to put them on a whiteboard, and the team works on the schedule alongside the doc. Before the meeting ends, someone asks Claude to share a recap with the team.

After the call, the notes, action items, plan and whiteboard are on the meeting's page in claude.ai. People who were in the meeting can later ask Claude about it, or add Claude to the next one from their calendar.

## Permissions and records

What defines the product is where the permission lines sit more than the feature list. The article's "Security and privacy" section says the following.

**What is stored.** Claude saves a transcript, a summary and screen descriptions, not audio. Each meeting gets its own page in claude.ai holding the notes, the transcript and anything Claude built. People from the organization on the invite can see and edit it, and it can be shared with others in the organization from the page. The article asks you to make sure everyone on your calls is OK with being transcribed before you add Claude or turn on auto-join.

**Outside guests.** By default Claude stays out of calls when someone from outside the organization is invited, and leaves if someone from outside joins partway through. An admin can allow Claude on calls with outside guests. On those calls Claude posts its notice in the call's chat but does not reply there, and what guests say is included in the notes and transcript.

**Tool use.** Claude uses each person's connected tools with that person's permission. It asks the first time, and a yes applies going forward. For now, when someone asks out loud, Claude asks each time to confirm who is speaking. An answer posted in the call's chat is visible to everyone on the call.

**Availability.** On Team plans, inviting Claude to meetings is available by default and an admin can turn it off. On Enterprise plans an admin turns it on for all or part of the organization. During the beta it is not available to some organizations, including those with zero data retention (ZDR), HIPAA-ready configuration, or customer-managed encryption keys (CMEK).

**Pricing.** During the beta, Claude's notes and transcripts are free on Team and Enterprise plans. So are its answers in calls, unless an answer uses one of your connected tools, which counts toward your plan's usage. The docs, whiteboards and other artifacts Claude makes in a call are free through 10 February 2027.

## Assessment: what changes and what is unsettled

What follows is our reading of the source, not a claim the article makes.

**First, the meeting's output leaves the call.** Earlier minute-taking tools produced notes and a summary. Here Claude builds a plan and a whiteboard during the call while participants edit them at the same time. The meeting's conclusion exists as a work product during the meeting rather than as post-processing, so the home of the action items shifts from "someone writes it up later" to "it is on the meeting's page".

**Second, authority is split per person.** Permission to use tools belongs to the person who gave it, and the current habit of confirming the speaker every time reads as a way to keep it clear whose authority a spoken request runs under. Yet an answer posted in the call's chat is visible to everyone. Information from a person's connected tools can reach people other than the one who granted access. In the "last year's cost" example, Claude asks permission and then posts the result to a chat everyone sees. What gets shared in the room remains the requester's call.

**Third, the default for outside participants is closed.** Claude does not enter a call with outsiders and leaves if one joins late. Allowing it takes an admin setting. Even when allowed, guests' speech goes into the notes and transcript. The article says so plainly, which means calls with external parties need notice in advance.

**Fourth, the set of organizations that can adopt it is narrow.** Organizations on ZDR, HIPAA-ready configuration or CMEK are out, and Google Meet is the only platform for now. The present fact is that organizations with stricter regulatory or storage requirements cannot use it during the beta.

**What the article does not settle.** It does not cover how long transcripts are retained, how a meeting page is deleted, or how versions of what Claude built are managed. It also does not say how the product checks that someone who joins mid-call has agreed to being transcribed for what was said before they arrived; the only request is that everyone be OK with it before Claude is added. An organization considering adoption has to settle those three points in contract documents or the Help Center.

## Conclusion

Claude Meetings positions Claude not as a tool that records a meeting but as a participant that sits in and starts work. At the same time, what that participant is allowed to do is cut finely: an outside-guest default exit, per-person tool permission, no audio retained, and admin-controlled enablement. The deciding factor is not the number of features but whether an operator can design meetings on the premise that work products now come into being during the call and that anything posted in the call's chat is seen by everyone.
