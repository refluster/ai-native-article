## Executive Summary

TSMC's board has approved up to $1 billion in AI investments: up to $500 million for Anthropic shares bought at the IPO price and up to $500 million for OpenAI preferred stock. The contract chipmaker that manufactures the processors behind most current AI models is becoming a shareholder in the developers who buy them, adding a second channel of exposure to the industry's capital spending cycle. The risk is concentration: both valuations rest on future AI demand assumptions, and a weak listing would mark the stakes down quickly.

## What was decided

According to BigGo Finance, TSMC's board approved up to $1 billion in AI investments, split as follows:

- Anthropic: up to $500 million for shares at the IPO price
- OpenAI: up to $500 million for preferred stock
The news lifted TSMC's U.S.-listed shares 2.56% on Tuesday.

## Anthropic's terms: price range and valuation

The report says Anthropic priced its offering at $70 to $78 a share, targeting a valuation of up to $1.4 trillion, which would place the debut among the largest listings ever recorded. No launch date has been disclosed, though Bloomberg has reported the offering could arrive as early as the week of Nov. 16, 2026.

At that range, TSMC's half-billion-dollar commitment would translate into roughly 6.4 million to 7.1 million shares. This is the full $500 million divided by the high and low ends of the pricing range.

Anthropic is also assembling a $26 billion private placement alongside the listing, with Alphabet's Google, Nvidia, AMD and Microsoft among the participating investors.

## OpenAI's side: terms still thin

OpenAI has filed confidentially but has not set terms, and its IPO details remain thin. TSMC's allocation would go toward preferred shares, the same instrument Amazon has targeted with a $35 billion commitment. ForgeGlobal's estimate puts OpenAI's valuation at $1.25 trillion.

## Why a chipmaker takes equity

The report frames the move as an extension of a strategy of pairing supply relationships with equity. TSMC is the world's largest contract chipmaker and the manufacturer behind the advanced processors that Nvidia designs and that power most current AI models, giving it a direct line to the industry's capital spending cycle. Taking stakes in the developers themselves gives it a second channel of exposure should those customers' valuations keep climbing.

TSMC's own results have tracked the boom. Last month it posted a sixth straight quarter of record profit and described demand for AI-related chips as "extremely robust," guiding to roughly 44% revenue growth for full-year 2026 and warning that orders continue to exceed what its fabs can produce. Management has nonetheless taken a cautious view of 2027. Capacity work is running in parallel, including a $265 billion buildout of manufacturing sites in Arizona and a review of possible projects in Texas.

## Risk and the market's read

The investment carries concentration risk. Anthropic's mooted $1.4 trillion valuation and ForgeGlobal's estimate of $1.25 trillion for OpenAI both rest on assumptions about future AI demand that have drawn increasing scrutiny as spending across the sector escalates. A weaker-than-expected reception for either listing would mark TSMC's stakes down quickly.

In the near term, Wall Street remains constructive on the chipmaker: nine analysts rate the stock a Buy, with an average price target of $570, implying about 43.7% upside from current levels.

## Evidence versus conclusion

**What the source states (evidence):** the board's approved amounts, Anthropic's price range and target valuation, the undisclosed listing date, OpenAI's unset terms, TSMC's results and outlook, and the valuation concerns.

**What follows (this article's judgment):** TSMC's move shows an upstream supplier in the AI chain taking a capital position on the demand side, so that both the upside and the downside of the cycle now run through its own profit and loss. For TSMC, a customer's valuation is no longer only a revenue forecast variable; it is also the value of an asset it holds. The concentration risk the report names comes from that double dependence.
