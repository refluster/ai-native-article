## Executive Summary

Google has announced that it will extend to the whole world, from January 2027, a verification scheme that requires Android app developers to prove their identity, together with new restrictions on sideloading unverified apps. It covers not only Google Play but also manufacturer stores such as Galaxy Store, Xiaomi and OPPO. Unverified apps can still be installed, but only after several extra steps, including a 24-hour wait after a settings change.

## What was announced

According to an article published on 20 November 2026 by Sumaho Digest (a Japanese smartphone news site), Google will roll out developer verification and the new sideloading mechanism for unverified apps worldwide from January 2027. The policy was first outlined in 2025, and a limited rollout in some countries began in September 2026.

The first phase works with seven distributors:

- Google Play
- Galaxy Store
- HONOR
- OPPO
- Xiaomi
- Transsion
- vivo
Google says it will widen the countries and distribution channels over the following months, until every app installation source on verified Android devices is covered.

## Not a ban, but extra steps

The key point is that apps from unverified developers do not become impossible to install. Sideloading remains allowed. The steps the article describes, however, are many:

- Open Developer options and select "apps from unverified developers"
- Enable "allow apps from unverified developers"
- Authenticate the device and confirm that no third party is pressuring you to make the change
- Restart the Android device
- Wait 24 hours
- Open Developer options again and choose to allow sideloading for seven days or indefinitely
The biggest change is the 24-hour wait after the setting is enabled. Even a user who wants to install an app immediately cannot skip it. Google frames the procedure as protection against scam apps and unauthorised manipulation by third parties.

## Track record and reach

Google says that since developer verification began in March 2026, millions of apps worldwide have registered, and claims they account for more than 99% of Android app installs. This is Google's claim; the article offers no independent check.

The scope is not limited to the newest Android. Per the article, devices running Android 7.0 or later are covered, so older-device users may be affected as well as owners of recent Pixel or Galaxy phones. Because stores other than Google Play are included, users of manufacturer stores are not exempt either.

## Assessment: freedom against safety

What follows is this article's interpretation, built on the source's facts.

The source supports two points: apps from verified developers install as before, and unverified apps still install but with more friction. Taking Google's 99%-plus figure at face value, most installs are untouched by the new procedure. The impact concentrates, as the article notes, on users of independently developed apps and apps not distributed through official stores.

The question therefore shifts from whether sideloading is allowed to how much friction it should carry. A 24-hour wait can help a user being rushed by a scammer, but it imposes the same burden on people running legitimate unverified apps. The article gives no figures on this trade-off, so its effect will have to be judged from how the scheme operates after January 2027.
