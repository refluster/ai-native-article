The longer the unit of work becomes, the more cost and quality are governed not by the model but by the infrastructure around it. Databricks found that simply changing the harness can move task cost by more than 2x, Anthropic explicitly states that Fable 5’s runs—stretching into hours—force a rewrite of scaffolding, and OpenAI reports that the unit of knowledge work itself has shifted from “interaction” to “delegated long-running tasks.” These three points look separate, but in fact they can be explained by the same principle.

## Even if the Model Stays Fixed, Cost Can Still Double

“The coding agent Databricks measured on its own multi-million-line codebase” showed empirically that efficiency gains do not begin and end with model selection alone. Published on July 8, 2026, this evaluation is valuable because it was not based on synthetic benchmark problems, but on an internal benchmark measuring models and harnesses on coding tasks actually handled by Databricks engineers. The result: the open model GLM 5.2 was statistically tied with Opus 4.8 on quality, while task cost was lower at $1.28 versus $1.94. Up to that point, this is a story about models. But the core of the article lies beyond that. **Even with the same model, differences in the harness alone moved task cost by more than 2x.**

### Why the Metric of “Cost per Token” Breaks Down

Databricks concluded that cost per token is inadequate as a measure of a task’s real cost. The reason is straightforward: what determines how many tokens are consumed to complete a single task—how many retries happen, how much context is loaded, and where backtracking occurs—is not the model but the harness. Put a cheap model on an expensive harness, and the unit-cost advantage disappears. The rate-limiting factor in efficiency has moved outside the model.

## As Execution Gets Longer, Rebuilding the Surrounding System Becomes Mandatory

“The Claude Fable 5 Migration Guide” confirms this shift from another angle. Anthropic’s official document, “Prompting Claude Fable 5,” explicitly states that the new model Claude Fable 5 (and Claude Mythos 5) behaves differently from Opus 4.8 and requires updates to prompts and scaffolding. The biggest change is execution time. **A single request can take several minutes at high effort, and in autonomous execution can extend to several hours.** Better instruction-following means the model can be steered with shorter prompts, but the design on the receiving side cannot remain unchanged if you want to capture that benefit.

### Swapping the Model Alone Is Not Enough

What matters here is not the model’s intelligence, but the design of the plumbing around it. To withstand runs lasting several hours, client timeouts must be reconsidered, and the harness must be reworked from synchronous blocking to asynchronous checks. In addition, older instructions that ask for reasoning to be output in the response body can trigger rejections from reasoning_extraction, which means existing skills must be audited. The model got stronger, yet in its existing environment it cannot simply be used as-is. Making it usable requires redesigning the surrounding code, and that becomes the new center of the work.

## The Unit of Work Itself Is Getting Longer

“What OpenAI shows about the reality of agents” explains why this shift is happening now. OpenAI argues that AI agents are changing the unit of knowledge work from “one-off interactions” to “delegated long-running tasks,” and backs that claim with internal usage data. As of May 2026, **80.6% of individual-user requests exceeded 30 minutes in human-equivalent time, and 25.6% exceeded 8 hours.** The coding support tool Codex has also spread into non-engineering departments such as legal, finance, and recruiting.

What this spread into non-engineering departments shows is that the delegation of long-running tasks is no longer a story confined to coding as a single domain. And OpenAI suggests that for the benefits to spread across the organization as a whole, a rebuild of workflow design is a prerequisite. Simply handing out tools is not enough; the side doing the delegating—the organization itself—must be rebuilt. The same pattern appears here too. If you were assigning people tasks that take more than 8 hours, you would redesign the workflow; agents are no exception.

## The Common Principle: The Longer It Gets, the More the Decisive Factor Escapes Outward

These three facts suggest a single principle. **The more the unit of work extends from a “one-off interaction” to a “delegated long-running task,” the more the decisive factors constraining cost and quality shift away from the model itself and toward the infrastructure around it—harnesses, scaffolding, workflows, and organizational design.**

Why? In one-off interactions, success or failure was mostly determined by a single output, so model capability was dominant. But a delegated long-running task is made up of chains of dozens of retries, context loading and unloading, backtracking, and waiting time. What controls each link in that chain is not the model but the machinery that runs it. That is why Databricks saw task cost move 2x with the same model just by changing the harness; why with Fable 5, asynchronous execution and a skills audit became mandatory; and why for OpenAI, redesigning workflow becomes a prerequisite for capturing the benefits. The longer the task, the more often non-model factors touch the outcome, and the decisive factor slips outside the model.

So what? The intuition that replacing a model with a smarter one will automatically improve both efficiency and quality is an intuition from the age of interaction. In the age of delegation, the same model can produce results that differ by 2x depending on the quality of the surrounding infrastructure. Put differently, even with the model held constant, there is still substantial room for improvement through better harnesses and workflow design alone. This principle is falsifiable: if in the future, with harnesses and workflow design held constant, changing only the model comes to dominate cost and quality for long-running tasks, then the principle is wrong. The three primary sources all indicate the opposite.

## Forecast and Implications: Investment Will Shift from Models to Plumbing

If this principle is correct, several changes are likely to continue.

First, **the discussion of “which model” will be replaced by the discussion of “which harness to run it on”** (probability: high). The basis is Databricks’ empirical measurement, where the harness produced larger variation than the model difference. Now that an open model has appeared that matches on quality while being 30% cheaper, the main battleground for differentiation moves outside model selection.

Second, **tool design will tilt from synchronous to asynchronous** (probability: medium to high). The basis is Fable 5’s multi-hour execution and the behavioral difference whereby older instructions that force reasoning into the response body can trigger rejection. Existing clients and skills built on the assumption of synchronous blocking will become obsolete as models grow more capable. Organizations that neglect skills audits and asynchronous redesign will not gain speed even if they buy the latest model.

Third, **rebuilding the organization itself will become a competitive differentiator** (probability: medium). The basis is OpenAI’s suggestion that redesigning workflow is a prerequisite for benefits to spread across the whole organization, together with the reality of delegation shown by the figure of 25.6% for tasks exceeding 8 hours. Anyone can buy a model; workflow design cannot be bought.

There is one practical implication readers should focus on. The next time you evaluate “which model to use,” make sure to ask in parallel how the outcome would change if the same budget were redirected toward the design of the harness, scaffolding, and workflow. Databricks’ gap of $1.28 versus $1.94 is large enough that changing the harness could fully reproduce—or even reverse—it within the same model. What works in the age of delegation is the cheapest adjustment with the biggest impact: the move outside the model.
