## Executive Summary

The latest edition of RBC Capital Markets’ semiannual spending survey of more than 100 chief information officers (CIOs) and technology leaders shows that enterprise AI spending is continuing to accelerate broadly into 2H 2026, without losing momentum. The clearest change is a shift in the stage of adoption: more than half of respondents said “AI is already in production,” and another 35% expect to move into production within six months. By vendor, OpenAI’s ChatGPT was the most frequently used at 57%, far ahead of Anthropic’s Claude at 12%, and it also led on performance perception, 44% to 24%.

## Where the survey comes from, and why these results carry weight

The survey is conducted about every six months by RBC Capital Markets tech analysts including Rishi Jaluria, who ask more than 100 CIOs and technology leaders about enterprise IT spending. The annual budgets covered by the respondents total several billion dollars. What matters here is the stance of the people running the survey. Jaluria is not an unabashed AI bull; as of October 2025, he had actually been arguing for caution on enterprise AI adoption, warning of an “adoption chill.” That same analyst now concludes that he is “encouraged by the broad-based strength in enterprise spending” and that “AI adoption is starting to move from pilot to production.”

- Survey organizer: RBC Capital Markets (Rishi Jaluria and other tech analysts)
- Respondents: More than 100 CIOs and technology leaders, surveyed on a semiannual basis
- Shift in view: In 2H 2025, concern centered on slowing adoption → this time, the survey confirms acceleration into 2H 2026
The fact that a cautious observer has reversed course does as much as any individual figure to support the credibility of the report.

## Fears of runaway token budgets did not materialize

In recent months, investors had worried that ballooning token-based billing would become AI’s biggest weakness. The survey showed the opposite.

- Nearly nine in 10 respondents said token budgets are “manageable”
- That said, almost half have already exceeded their original spending plans
- Even so, most companies still plan to increase token spending further
That coexistence of budget overruns and “manageable” costs is the key point. Even when spending has come in above plan, enterprises are not pulling back to cut costs; they are instead expecting to spend more. The article argues that this makes sense because token prices themselves are likely to fall over time, improving the return on AI spending. In other words, the bear case that “runaway token billing will derail AI investment” is, at least for now, not borne out by the evidence.

## OpenAI pulls away — in both usage and perceived performance

The gap between vendors was not close; it was one-sided. The article describes it this way: “OpenAI is not just ahead, it’s lapping the field.”

- Most-used AI model-based service: ChatGPT at 57%, Anthropic’s Claude at 12%
- Best-performing model provider: OpenAI at 44%, Anthropic at 24%
OpenAI held roughly a twofold lead on both usage frequency and performance perception. That is about more than brand strength. The article notes that successful IPOs for both OpenAI and Anthropic would require sustained and very large-scale enterprise adoption. In that sense, enterprise share is being read as a metric that maps directly to how the capital markets will ultimately value both companies.

## No “SaaSpocalypse” — only the pricing model changed fast

The long-predicted “SaaSpocalypse” (the idea that AI would devour SaaS) has not, for now, shown up in this survey.

- A large majority of respondents expect software spending to increase
- Not a single respondent said they expect to reduce spending
- Even companies increasing AI spending are not funding it by cutting their existing software stack
At the same time, enterprise buying behavior itself is changing quickly. Hybrid pricing models that combine seat licenses with usage-based billing have rapidly become the preferred way enterprises want to buy AI. The article says this is a notably fast shift in a market where adoption of new technology is usually glacially slow. The implication is straightforward: AI is not cannibalizing software budgets, but expanding the overall pie while rewriting how software gets priced.

## The “100% chart” — everyone has an AI budget, and 90% say it’s new money

The simplest and most symbolic chart in the report was a fully filled-in blue circle showing that 100% of respondents have budget allocated to AI and large language model (LLM) projects.

- Respondents allocating budget to AI/LLM projects: 100%
- Of those, the share saying this was not a reallocation from existing spending but a “completely new AI budget”: 91%
The fact that the share of new budget has reached 90% means AI has moved beyond an internal fight over existing dollars and become a destination for incremental investment. A cautious analyst reversing his view, a shift from pilot to production, and the creation of new budget—all three point to the same conclusion: the enterprise AI investment cycle has entered an acceleration phase.
