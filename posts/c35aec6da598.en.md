An AI tool reportedly used in a breach of Korean banks that its author cannot take back. Satya Nadella's call to separate the supply of intelligence from authority over systems. A LayerX engineering manager who gives AI the job of widening what he can observe, not deciding for him. The three look unrelated, but one principle explains them: **once a capability has been distributed it cannot be recalled, so trust lives only in the observation and authority designed around the capability, never inside it.**

## Three facts: what has become irrecoverable

### ARTEX: withdrawn code does not disappear

The L2 explainer "ARTEX developer closes public repository after 9-bank breach in Korea: the question left by code that cannot be recalled" (Agentic AI) covers an AI penetration-testing agent said to have been used in a breach of Korean banks. On October 8 the developer pulled the public GitHub page and announced a move to closed source. But the tool had been public since July 26, so forks and mirrors may already exist. The source article judges that the withdrawal protects the developer's name more than the next bank.

The point is not who is to blame. For roughly two and a half months between release and withdrawal, the capability could be copied out of the developer's hands. The withdrawal was a control applied from outside the capability after it had been handed out, and it worked on the developer's position, not on the code already in circulation. When control lags the spread of capability, it stops preventing harm and starts allocating responsibility.

### Nadella: the system that needs to trust the model least is the most trustworthy

According to the L2 explainer "The 'most trustworthy system' is the one that has to trust the model least: Nadella's separation of intelligence supply from authority" (Verification & Trust), Microsoft chairman and CEO Satya Nadella proposed on October 10, 2026, in an article on X, treating frontier models as an insider risk. The premise is not malice: any powerful actor with access to critical systems can err or be compromised. The design therefore separates the supply of intelligence from authority over it and places control outside the model. Its backbone is seven principles of observability and the conclusion that the system trusting the model least is the most trustworthy.

The core of the claim is a move in where trust is placed. Trust is not staked on the model's goodwill or accuracy but on a structure that limits damage when the model is wrong. Read beside ARTEX, the proposal becomes concrete: if a capability cannot be recalled, the designer's only remaining lever is to narrow, on the authority side, what the capability can touch.

### LayerX: delegate observation, not judgment

In the L2 explainer "Output more than doubled in six months with one manager: the design and limits of a LayerX EM's home-built 'AI-accompanied' management" (Org Transformation), the Engineering Manager of LayerX's Ai Workforce division (mootoh) built a system to oversee a team of 13 to 20 people together with AI, without adding managers or splitting into sub-teams. What he hands to AI is not "automation of judgment" but "extension of the range he can observe" and prompts for conversation. Outputs are kept as hypotheses to be confirmed with the person concerned. The challenge after six months of operation: the resolution is "still coarse."

A powerful capability, AI, has been brought into the organization while final authority stays with the human EM. Treating AI output as a hypothesis and inserting a confirmation step is what Nadella calls separating the supply of intelligence from authority, implemented at the scale of team management.

## The common principle: bundle capability with authority and control becomes an afterthought

Set side by side, the three share a structure.

- In ARTEX, the capability (the agent's code) was released without separating it from the question of who may use it, and the withdrawal came afterward.
- Nadella asks that the supply of capability (intelligence) be separated from authority over critical systems from the start.
- The LayerX EM brings in a capability (AI-driven observation) while keeping authority over judgment with a human.
These facts suggest the following. **A powerful capability cannot be recalled once distributed. Trust therefore depends not on the power to recall a capability but on the power to design, in advance, the range of authority it can reach.** ARTEX shows what follows when capability is distributed without that design; the Nadella and LayerX cases show what the design looks like when it comes first.

Why hold this? Capability can be copied, and the cost of stopping it grows with time. Authority, by contrast, is granted by a party that decides it, and at design time it is cheap to narrow. This asymmetry between how hard recall is and how easy design is gives control a rational home outside the capability rather than inside it.

There is a second implication, about observation. It is no accident that Nadella builds on seven observability principles and that LayerX delegated "extension of observation" to AI. Once authority is separated from capability, observation is the only way to know whether that authority is being used correctly. Narrowing authority and making it visible work as one design.

The conditions that would falsify this are explicit. If many major AI deployments appear that keep capability and authority bundled and still hold trust for years without serious incident, the principle weakens. And if the "coarse resolution" LayerX admits does not improve even as observation is made finer, the very idea of control through observation comes into doubt.

## Predictions and implications

If the principle holds, the following changes are likely.

First, the axis on which organizations evaluate AI agents shifts from model accuracy to the design of authority and the machinery of observation. The grounds are that Nadella put his conclusion on the design side rather than on model reliability, and that LayerX deliberately avoided automating judgment. Confidence is moderate: accuracy competition continues, but the center of gravity of adoption decisions moves.

Second, the recognition spreads that deciding whether to release a capability cannot be settled by withdrawing it later. In ARTEX the withdrawal protected the developer's name, but it cannot reach copies already out. Developers in domains where a tool can also be used for attack will be expected to think through authority and use boundaries before release, on the assumption that recall is impossible. This is an inference in line with the source article's assessment; confidence is moderate to low.

Third, the resolution of observation becomes a new field of competition. LayerX's own verdict that resolution is "still coarse" is the flip side of observation determining the quality of control. If observation is coarse, misuse slips past even with authority separated. Over a few years, the gap between organizations that invest in finer observation and those that do not should show up as a gap in operational incidents.

Three practical takeaways follow. When introducing AI, write down first whose authority an output acts under and on what. Treat AI output as a hypothesis to be confirmed, not a conclusion, and keep a human checkpoint in the design. And settle decisions about release and delegation up front, on the assumption that withdrawal and correction will no longer be available. In an age of capabilities that cannot be recalled, control is not something applied afterward; it is what you put in place first.
