## Grok AI Briefing as of May 2026: User Scale, Slowing Growth, and Severe Regulatory Risk

## Executive Summary

According to an article published by fatjoe, as of May 2026 Grok had “50–64 million monthly active users” and “8–10 million daily active users,” while on the web it recorded “326.3 million website visits in March 2026.” At the same time, in the U.S. mobile app market it posted a “13.5% U.S. mobile app DAU share (March 2026),” marking its first decline from February’s “15.3% peak.”

Behind that growth, the “$1.25 trillion” xAI-SpaceX merger, the provision of Colossus 1 compute resources to Anthropic, and the deepfake controversy that has continued since late 2025 are all unfolding at once. The article frames Grok as both a “rapidly growing AI assistant” and a product “facing tests of regulation, reputation, and growth sustainability.”

## User Scale: 50–64 million MAU, but estimates vary widely by source

On Grok’s user numbers, the article clearly states that “Grok itself doesn’t publish these figures,” and presents multiple outside estimates side by side. That means these should not be read as a single definitive figure, but with the understanding that measurement targets differ.

- Evidence
- “The most widely cited figure comes from xAI’s own internal reporting: 64 million monthly active users as of late 2025, according to Business of Apps”
- “mid-2025, when MAU sat at around 35 million”
- “December 2024, when Grok reportedly had just 44,800 monthly active users”
- “SEOProfy reports around 60 million MAU for the Grok app in January 2026”
- “DemandSage cites a lower figure of around 30 million unique visitors based on Semrush data”
- “estimates point to around 8 to 10 million DAU”
- “giving a DAU/MAU ratio of around 25–30%”
- In the comparison table, Grok is listed at “50–64 million,” versus ChatGPT at “900 million+ (weekly),” Gemini at “750 million,” DeepSeek at “~40 million,” Perplexity at “~34 million,” and Claude at “~30–40 million (rising fast)”
- On X/Twitter’s installed base, “roughly 400–600 million monthly users”
- “Every X Premium subscriber gets Grok access by default”
- Conclusion
- Within the scope of the article, the clearest conclusion is that Grok has already reached a level of usage comparable to DeepSeek, Perplexity, and Claude, while still trailing far behind ChatGPT and Gemini.
- As the article itself indicates, the main reason for the wide spread in estimates is whether they include “unique web visitors,” “app users,” or “people interacting with Grok within the X app itself.”
- Bundled distribution through X Premium functions as an installed-platform distribution advantage similar to Google/Gemini.
## Web Traffic and Market Share: 326.3 million visits, but the growth curve is flattening

Web metrics show that Grok has scaled into a major service in a short period of time. At the same time, however, the article also notes that “the curve is clearly flattening,” pointing to slower growth rates.

- Evidence
- “March 2026 saw Grok.com reach 326.3 million visits – its highest recorded total ever”
- “up 9.3% month-on-month and 61% year-on-year”
- “February 2026 dipped to 298.6 million visits”
- “January 2026 came in at 314 million visits”
- “Users spend an average of 12 minutes 57 seconds on the website”
- “An average of 21.41 pages viewed per visit”
- “A low bounce rate of 26.48%”
- “~73% of web traffic comes from desktop devices”
- “The United States has the highest traffic share for Grok”
- “Grok.com ranks 4th in the AI Chatbots and Tools category on Similarweb”
- “around 140–150 million visits per month as recently as August 2025”
- “By November 2025, that had risen to 234 million; by January, 314 million; by March, 326 million”
- Country share (Feb 2026): “USA 23.68%” “India 8.00%” “Brazil 5.21%” “South Korea 3.56%” “Hong Kong 3.13%”
- “90.63% of all outbound links from the site point to x.ai”
- By comparison, “OpenAI only has a 10.48% share of ChatGPT’s outbound links”
- “60.22% of social traffic comes from YouTube – X sits in second with 14.56%”
- Conclusion
- On the web, Grok more than doubled in seven months, from “140–150 million” around August 2025 to “326.3 million” in March 2026.
- The figures for time on site — “12 minutes 57 seconds” — pages per visit — “21.41” — and bounce rate — “26.48%” — indicate that this is not just an increase in inflows, but usage involving meaningful on-site navigation.
- At the same time, the article pairs record absolute totals with “flattening,” suggesting a transition away from the exponential-growth phase.
- Given that “90.63%” of outbound links point to x.ai, Grok.com reads less like a standalone media property than a funnel into the xAI ecosystem.
## U.S. Mobile App: Down from 15.3% to 13.5%, entering its first “plateau” phase

The clearest sign of a shift in the entire article is the decline in share within the U.S. mobile app market. After fourteen straight months of growth, March 2026 is described as the first pullback.

- Evidence
- “Apptopia’s April 2026 data brief”
- “Grok’s U.S. chatbot mobile app market share slipped from 15.3% in February to 13.5% in March”
- “its first monthly decline after fourteen straight months of growth”
- “Downloads fell 13%, and churn ticked up”
- In the comparison table, as of March 2026: “ChatGPT 38.7%” “Gemini ~25%” “Grok 13.5%” “Claude 10.0%” “Copilot ~10%”
- In January 2025, Grok stood at “1.6%”
- “After a near-vertical climb from 8% to over 15% between September and February”
- The growth drivers are described as being “fuelled by X integration and the Grok 4.20 multi-agent release”
- “Apptopia’s VP of Research Tom Grant suggested the deepfake scandal and regulatory pressure in Europe may finally be biting”
- “Anthropic’s app jumped from 4% to 10% in a single month – a 167% MoM DAU jump”
- “Claude has effectively taken the “fastest growing” crown that Grok held for most of 2025 and early 2026”
- Conclusion
- As framed in the article, Grok’s problem is not “lack of scale” but “the end of acceleration.” A 13.5% share is still substantial, but the one-way upward phase is over.
- While avoiding firm attribution, the article presents the “deepfake scandal” and “regulatory pressure in Europe” as plausible leading causes behind the decline.
- With Claude surging from “4% to 10%” in the same month, Grok’s deceleration stands out even more sharply in relative terms.
- In other words, in the U.S. mobile market, both of the following can be true at once: Grok has “established itself as the third pole,” and it has “lost the title of fastest-growing.”
## Engagement and User Demographics: Usage intensity is rising, but the audience is skewed

In contrast with the drop in share, the article describes increased depth of use as “The Quiet Bright Spot.” Even so, the demographic skew — especially toward male users — is extremely pronounced.

- Evidence
- “Grok power users now spend 120 minutes per day in the app”
- “up from under 22 minutes per general DAU just a couple of months earlier”
- For comparison, “behind Microsoft Copilot (154 minutes) and roughly level with Claude’s power users (139 minutes)”
- Web gender split: “67.46% male users” “32.54% female users”
- “Sensor Tower data goes even further, estimating Grok’s male user share at around 82.5% when looking at the app specifically”
- Age mix: “18-24 19.40%” “25-34 31.47%” “35-44 ~16%” “45-54 ~14%” “55-64 ~11%”
- “Around 50–51% of all Grok users are under the age of 35”
- “Grok’s top user personas include crypto traders, gamblers, and peer-to-peer payment users”
- In a Marketing LTB survey: “Grok has a 42% retention rate after 30 days”
- “65% of new users come from X integrations”
- “9% of Grok users pay for Premium”
- According to Fritz.ai, “61% of Grok users prefer its tone over ChatGPT for informal use”
- “25% MoM growth in paid users”
- “paid users with a SuperGrok subscription will send 15–22 prompts per day”
- Conclusion
- Despite slower new-user acquisition, existing core users are sharply increasing the time they spend in the app. In the article’s phrasing, Grok is “acquiring fewer new users than it was in February, but the ones it has are spending more time in the app than ever.”
- The article characterizes Grok’s audience as the most male-skewed among the major AI platforms. It explains this through overlap with Elon Musk and X’s power-user base.
- The figure that “65% of new users come from X integrations” reinforces the idea that X is not just a traffic source but a distribution channel embedded in actual usage.
- The statement that “9% of Grok users pay for Premium” is presented in the article as a higher monetization rate than “only around 1-2% of ChatGPT users pay for its premium version.”
## The xAI/SpaceX Merger, Compute Supply to Anthropic, and the Deepfake Crisis

The key 2026 questions around Grok cannot be understood through product metrics alone. The article gives equal weight to corporate restructuring, compute resources, and regulatory and litigation risk.

- Evidence
- “On February 2, 2026, Elon Musk merged xAI with SpaceX in a deal valuing the combined entity at $1.25 trillion — with SpaceX at $1 trillion and xAI at $250 billion”
- “the largest corporate merger of all time”
- “xAI was valued at $230 billion in its January 2026 Series E round ($20 billion raised), then $250 billion as part of the SpaceX merger announced two weeks later”
- “Total funding raised by xAI exceeds $45 billion across all rounds”
- “Grok generated an estimated $350 million in revenue in 2025”
- “Revenue is projected to reach $2 billion in 2026”
- “xAI secured a $200 million U.S. Department of Defense contract in July 2025”
- “half of the company’s co-founders left, and by late March 2026, Musk was the only remaining co-founder”
- “The CFO also departed in April 2026, replaced by a SpaceX executive”
- “xAI has now signed an agreement with Anthropic to provide access to Colossus 1”
- Colossus 1 is described as “around 230,000 GPUs”
- Colossus 2 is described as “1.5 gigawatts of power and over 550,000 Nvidia Blackwell GPUs – completed in late April 2026”
- “plans to expand further toward 1 million GPUs and 2GW of capacity”
- “Together, the Memphis facilities consume roughly the output of two large nuclear power plants”
- Model updates include “Grok 4.20 Beta (Feb 17, 2026)” “Grok 4.20 Heavy (Feb 18)” “Grok 4.20 Beta 2 (March 3)” “Grok 4.3 Beta (April 17)”
- “Grok 4.20 Heavy” features “16 specialised agents with a 2M token context window”
- “Grok 4.3 Beta” features “a 1M token context window, native video input (up to five minutes at 1080p), and native PDF/PPTX/XLSX file generation directly from a prompt”
- On the deepfake issue, “A New York Times review found Grok generated 4.4 million images over just nine days, with 1.8 million being sexualised depictions of women”
- “Researchers at the Center for Countering Digital Hate estimated Grok produced over 23,000 sexualised images of children over 11 days”
- “6,700 sexually suggestive images per hour”
- “Malaysia and Indonesia became the first countries to ban Grok outright”
- “The European Commission opened formal proceedings against X under the Digital Services Act”
- “Ofcom in the UK launched an investigation under the Online Safety Act”
- “Apple privately threatened to remove Grok from the App Store in January and rejected multiple updates between January and March 2026”
- “Apple approved Grok’s latest update” but “technical safeguards remain imperfect”
- “California’s Attorney General has launched a formal investigation”
- Reuters is cited as noting that “Grok’s U.S. market share jump to 17.8% came amid the sexualised images backlash”
- After that, “The March decline to 13.5% suggests that effect, if it existed, has now reversed”
- Conclusion
- The article’s central throughline is that Grok’s competitiveness is tied less to “app quality” than to “gigawatts” and “GPU capacity.” The provision of Colossus 1 to Anthropic is presented as the flip side of that compute advantage.
- On the corporate side, valuation and fundraising scale are expanding rapidly, but the departure of co-founders and the CFO transition are placed alongside that growth as signs of instability after the merger.
- The deepfake issue is not treated as a mere reputational risk, but as something that has escalated into country-level bans, investigations by EU, UK, and U.S. state authorities, and a near-removal from App Store distribution.
- The article suggests that part of the market-share rise from late 2025 into early 2026 may have been boosted by “controversy itself drawing attention,” and is structured in a way that implies the backlash from that attention may have shown up in March’s drop to “13.5%.”
