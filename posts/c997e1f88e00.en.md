## Executive Summary

Effective June 1, 2026, Mercari appointed Toshiya Kimura, Executive Officer and CTO Japan Business, as Chief Human Resources Officer (CHRO) and Chief AI Officer (CAIO). The core of the announcement is not the personnel change itself, but the decision to place AI strategy and HR strategy under one leader and redesign the “operating foundations of people and organizations”—including ways of working, decision-making and approval processes, organizational structure, and resource allocation—on the assumption that AI is foundational.

The company has already said that “employee AI usage has reached 100%” and that “development output per engineer increased 1.9x year over year.” This initiative is positioned as a move away from AI utilization at the individual level and toward AI-native transformation at the systems and organizational level.

## What It Means to Assume the Role of “CHRO, CAIO, and CTO Japan Business”: Integrated Accountability

In short, Mercari has stopped treating the promotion of AI utilization and the design of HR systems as separate matters, and has assigned unified responsibility for both to Toshiya Kimura.

- Evidence
- As of June 1, 2026, the company announced that Toshiya Kimura would assume the roles of Chief Human Resources Officer (CHRO: Chief Human Resources Officer) and Chief AI Officer (CAIO: Chief AI Officer)
- Kimura will serve as “CHRO, CAIO, and CTO Japan Business”
- The newly stated vision is “HR for an AI-Native Company”
- The objective is to “integrate responsibility for AI strategy and HR strategy, and accelerate transformation into an AI-Native Company by redesigning, on the assumption that AI is foundational, the operating foundations of people and organizations, including ways of working, decision-making and approval processes, organizational structure, and resource allocation”
- “A successor for the CTO role is scheduled to assume the position on July 1”
- Conclusion
- This announcement is not about the creation of a new CHRO role or a routine leadership replacement; it is a reallocation of authority intended to bridge the divide between AI implementation and organizational design
- By having one executive serve concurrently as CAIO and CHRO, Mercari is shifting to a structure that treats AI adoption not as a matter of “tool usage” but as an operational design issue encompassing evaluation, approvals, staffing, and organizational structure
- Because the company explicitly states that a successor CTO is scheduled to take office, this reads not as a temporary measure but as a transition premised on a deliberate reallocation of roles
## From “Realizing an AI-Native Company” to Redesigning the “Operating Foundations of People and Organizations”

Mercari explicitly states that it is not enough for AI use to have spread at the individual level, defining the next stage as updating organizational operations themselves.

- Evidence
- In July 2025, the Mercari Group declared its intention to “realize an AI-Native Company”
- As a policy, it stated that it would “radically transform both the organization and its products with AI as the foundation of everything”
- It subsequently launched an “AI Task Force” of around 100 people
- That task force “promoted AI utilization across every area of products, business processes, and the organization,” and “completed the visualization of AI potential and identification of priority areas after decomposing business processes, as well as the development of a foundation for AI governance”
- As outcomes, the company says that “employee AI usage has reached 100%” and that “development output per engineer improved 1.9x year over year”
- At the same time, the company states: “Unless we update the very ‘operating foundations of people and organizations’—including ways of working, decision-making and approval processes, organizational structure, and resource allocation—on the assumption that AI is foundational, we will not be able to fully capitalize on that speed”
- Previously, “AI strategy and HR strategy were handled by separate leaders, leaving the transformation of organizational mechanisms and the promotion of AI utilization structurally divided”
- Conclusion
- In Mercari’s own assessment, the bottleneck in AI adoption has shifted from employee usage rates to existing organizational design
- The fact that the company is moving on to systems redesign despite having reached “100% AI usage” shows that it does not view AI adoption as something that can be completed through “individual skill acquisition” alone
- This personnel move represents the next stage: connecting the analysis and foundational work carried out by the AI Task Force to formal personnel authority and systems design
## The Two-Stage Execution Plan Indicated by “HR for an AI-Native Company”

In the announcement, Mercari redefines the role of HR not as a functional department limited to hiring and labor administration, but as a driver of AI-native organizational evolution through institutional design.

- Evidence
- The vision, “HR for an AI-Native Company,” is described as aiming “not merely to transform the HR function itself, but to drive from the HR side the evolution of the organization into one where each employee develops and masters AI agents, and repeatedly makes decisions with an order-of-magnitude greater speed and quality”
- Execution will proceed in “two steps”
- Step 1 is for “HR itself to become an embodiment of AI-Native by fundamentally understanding AI’s potential”
- As a concrete measure, “the HR department itself will embed AI into everyday operations and expand opportunities for all employees to participate in hands-on experiences such as ‘AI Agent Day’ *3”
- Step 2 is to “redesign the ‘operating foundations of people and organizations’ on the assumption that AI is foundational, and implement this across the company”
- The first specific initiative is “redesigning organizational structure and ways of working,” which “will indicate a direction for redesigning ways of working, decision-making and approval processes, organizational structure, and resource allocation on the assumption that AI is foundational, beginning with the full-scale rollout of a development structure centered on AI”
- The second specific initiative is “implementation in evaluation and talent management,” under which Mercari “will build mechanisms to fairly evaluate individuals and teams that produce overwhelming results through AI, and evolve hiring, development, and talent management into forms appropriate for an AI-Native Company”
- Conclusion
- HR is positioned not as a support function for AI adoption, but as a proving ground that will implement AI-native transformation ahead of the rest of the company
- The sequence—Step 1 focused on experience and understanding, Step 2 on institutional implementation—shows that this is not simply a top-down rollout, but one that makes HR’s own operational transformation a precondition
- Because the scope extends to evaluation systems, AI utilization is envisioned not as a recommended behavior, but as something built into the management infrastructure that determines recognition of results and talent mobility
## Ways of Working Built Around AI Agents and Stronger Hiring of “AI-Native Talent”

Under the new structure, Mercari will not only redesign the organization internally, but also shift its external hiring requirements to an AI-native basis.

- Evidence
- Mercari explicitly describes the style of work it is aiming for as “an organization in which it has become standard for each employee to teach AI their own knowledge and decision criteria, develop multiple AI agents as partners, move across specialized domains, and continue making decisions”
- “Under the new structure, we will also focus on hiring talent that embodies this AI-Native way of working and will advance transformation together with us”
- As hiring targets, the company says it is “broadly seeking AI-Native talent, including engineers and product managers”
- As background, it cites “a product platform used by approximately 23 million customers” and “a frontline environment for AI utilization”
- It is scheduled to hold the recruiting event “Mercari AI Career Fes 2026” on “Wednesday, July 8, 2026”
- It says job openings and event information will be updated on the recruitment site “https://careers.mercari.com/”
- Conclusion
- The type of talent Mercari is seeking is no longer people who use AI merely as an aid, but people who work by externalizing their own knowledge and judgment into AI agents
- The stronger hiring push is not a supplementary measure to institutional reform, but part of a shift toward a talent portfolio suited to AI-native organizational design
- Because the company explicitly names “engineers and product managers,” its AI-native transformation is clearly not limited to research or R&D, but is centered on job categories directly tied to business operations
## Toshiya Kimura’s Career and the Continuity from AI Adoption to Organizational Transformation

Kimura’s appointment is notable less for the surprise of moving a technology executive into HR, and more for the continuity of entrusting institutional reform to the person who has led AI adoption within the company.

- Evidence
- Since 2007, at mixi, Inc., Kimura worked on “recommendation engine development and data utilization” and on “advertising development and marketing data development utilizing machine learning”
- Since 2017, at Mercari, he was responsible for “establishing the R4D research and development organization” and for “research across a broad range of AI-centered research areas”
- He then “established and became Director of the engineering organization in the AI and search engine domain, leading the introduction of AI into Mercari”
- Since July 2022: “Executive Officer, VP of Platform Engineering”
- Since July 2024: “Executive Officer, CTO Japan Region and General Manager Japan Region Platform”
- Since March 2025: “Executive Officer, CTO Japan Region”
- Since July 2025: “Executive Officer, CTO Japan Business”
- Since June 2026: “Executive Officer, CHRO, CAIO, and CTO Japan Business”
- The announcement itself also explains that, as CTO, Kimura “led the ‘AI Task Force’ and drove organizational and business transformation toward the realization of an AI-Native Company”
- Conclusion
- The basis for Kimura’s appointment is not a conventional description of HR experience, but a track record spanning AI research, implementation, platform leadership, and organizational transformation
- For that reason, this CHRO appointment is best understood not as the appointment of an HR specialist, but as a decision to restructure the HR function into the strategic core necessary to make AI-native transformation work
- The expansion from CTO to CHRO/CAIO is not about improving the efficiency of the technology organization, but about changing the chain of command so company-wide systems can be migrated to an AI-first basis
