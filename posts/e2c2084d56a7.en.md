## Executive Summary

On September 29, 2026, Gartner predicted that by 2028, 70% of enterprises will abandon agentic AI built by vendor forward-deployed engineering (FDE), "trapped by soaring costs and unable to evolve it on their own." Gartner frames FDE failure as a problem of structure that comes before technology, and it asks software engineering leaders to settle scope, governance, ownership and exit from day one, across three phases: before signing, during the engagement, and at exit.

## What the forecast says, and how FDE is defined

The release (Stamford, Conn., September 29, 2026) rests on one core sentence.

- By 2028, 70% of enterprises will abandon agentic AI built by vendor FDE, trapped by soaring costs and unable to evolve it on their own, according to Gartner.
- Vendor FDE is defined as "a model in which vendor engineers work directly with customers to build and deploy solutions."
- Mukul Saha, Sr Director Analyst at Gartner, said: "FDE success starts with getting the engagement structure right, from scope and incentives to governance, ownership, and exit."
- The release states that "FDE engagements often fail structurally before they fail technically." The outcome is shaped by decisions made before contracting, during delivery, and at transition, when software engineering leaders still control scope, incentives, governance and ownership.
## The three-phase prescription

The release presents the three tenets of a genuine FDE model (Figure 1) as three phases in time. What follows is limited to what the release text says.

### Before signing

- Use FDE only for problems that require deep product expertise, rapid adaptation, or close integration between vendor technology and your operating environment.
- Saha gives two pieces of advice: identify an executive sponsor accountable for business outcomes, not simply the implementation budget; and establish contractual requirements beyond procurement's scope, including deliverables, knowledge transfer, intellectual property rights, and transition or exit responsibilities.
### During the engagement

- Embed FDEs with internal domain experts, engineers and end users so that critical knowledge is shared and the solution reflects how work actually gets done.
- Saha calls for a cadence of iterative business validation that evaluates not only technical performance but also "the operating model required to scale AI responsibly." Each increment should define decision rights, set the balance between autonomy and human oversight, clarify how confidence and exceptions are communicated, and build the governance mechanisms that sustain user trust and accountability.
### Exit and prove independence

- Execute the exit plan established at the start rather than extending the engagement because internal teams are not ready.
- By Saha's account, success is measured not by implementation completion but by the enterprise's ability to manage, optimize and scale the technology, including adapting human-AI decision models as business processes, priorities and risk profiles evolve.
## A warning about "FDE washing"

The release argues that FDE must be viewed as a delivery model. The underlying pattern is familiar from enterprise software, technically strong, product-literate people placed close to the customer; what is new is "the intensity of the hype."

- Saha: "Many providers now use 'forward deployed' as a label for implementation, professional services, solution engineering, or AI consulting; some thoughtfully, others because it sounds more strategic." He adds: "Some charge premium fees without the delivery depth, program management, or change management maturity to justify them."
- Customers may see faster early progress but fail to build internal capability, and end up paying premium rates for work that, once the scope is clear, a traditional services or partner model could potentially deliver more cost-effectively and with more predictable results.
- Gartner's second prediction: through 2028, less than 20% of FDE engagements will turn recurring customer needs into capabilities in the vendor's core product, exposing a growing risk of "FDE washing," where consulting services are marketed as FDE.
Further detail sits in "The Forward-Deployed Engineering Playbook," which is available to Gartner clients and is not readable from the release.

## What we read into it (evidence first, inference second)

The sections above are the release. What follows is our inference, not Gartner's claim.

- The release names two causes of abandonment: soaring costs and the inability to evolve the system independently. It says nothing about model performance. How the 70% was derived, from what population, or whether any survey sits behind it is not stated in the text. Treat it as a forecast; nothing in the release lets a reader check the precision of the number.
- The substance of the prescription is knowledge transfer, IP, ownership and exit. All three phases are about making what the vendor builds something the customer can take over. It reads as a claim that outsourcing succeeds or fails on the design of the handover, whether or not the work is agentic AI.
- The under-20% prediction suggests that the vendor's learning is at stake too. If what is learned on the customer's site never returns to the vendor's product, the customer keeps paying for bespoke work and the vendor gains no reusable asset. This figure also comes with no stated basis.
- The release is written for software engineering leaders at customer firms, and the detailed version is paywalled. That the source also offers an FDE playbook to its clients is a reason to read it with some discount.
