## Executive Summary

In "Charts of the Week: Winds of Thematic Change" (a16z, Aug 21, 2026), Moses Sternstein assembles data showing the AI boom has moved well past ETF flows into hard indicators of the real economy: construction-site wages and enterprise token consumption. Data center construction is delivering unprecedented wage premiums to skilled trades, even as AI agent usage splits sharply between a typical firm and a fast-pulling-away top decile. Alongside that, legacy automation tools are losing site traffic while a single 2023-founded challenger, Gumloop, is the only platform still gaining.

## ETF Themes Have Completely Flipped Since 2020

According to data from Citadel, ETF net inflows are pacing for their strongest year ever, with July setting an all-time record. What draws that money has also changed dramatically. Back in 2020, the top five ETF themes included clean energy, emerging markets tech, and healthcare; by 2026, AI, Nuclear, Space, Defense and Infrastructure dominate the leaderboard instead. The piece calls this "a total ETF takeover for the ambitious, capital-intensive world of atoms over bits."

## Data Centers Are a Blue-Collar Bonanza

Data center construction now anchors non-residential construction spending in several states. New Mexico and Wyoming aren't building much in absolute terms — under 3 gigawatts each under construction — but because neither state builds much of anything else, data centers make up roughly 60% of all private non-residential construction there. In larger Pennsylvania, about 3 GW of data centers is nearly 30% of non-residential spending. Texas, which is building far more capacity than either state, sees data centers account for just 10% of its total construction spending.

Wells Fargo's tabulation of the "significant economic benefits" running alongside data centers found that since 2024, counties with already-operating data centers have uniformly seen more housing, higher home values, less unemployment, and more job growth. Counties still building data centers show a better employment picture too, but with a sharper drawdown in new housing and weaker home-value appreciation — a pattern the piece flags as confounded by cases like Loudoun County, VA (one of the wealthiest counties in the country) and Texas's already-elevated pre-2024 housing boom.

Indeed data shows the data center wage premium runs as high as 64% for a facilities manager down to a still-substantial 10% for electrical engineers. A heavy industrial construction contractor quoted in a Dallas Fed report put it directly: "We have been paying what I believe to be a very competitive wage for skilled concrete workers, $28–32 per hour. The data centers are offering $45 per hour and a $150 per diem for concrete workers" — roughly a 50% wage premium. ADP data on job-switchers (a proxy for where labor demand is hottest, since a pay bump is usually needed to induce a switch) shows wage growth 6–9.5 percentage points higher than for job-stayers in Construction, Manufacturing, and Natural Resources/Mining — a larger job-switching premium than in any other sector.

## AI Agent Token Use Is Splitting Sharply at the Top Decile

OpenAI data cited in the piece shows output tokens have roughly doubled across the typical firm over the past year-plus, while the top decile of firms keeps pulling further away. Across all industries there is an roughly 8-fold gap in token output between the typical and top-decile enterprise, with the top decile increasing output by more than 17x since April 2025. Within Information (tech), the gap widens to almost 12-fold, with the top decile outputting 32.5x as many tokens as it was a little over a year ago. Plug-in adoption among the top decile runs at roughly 2x the typical firm, and Skills adoption at roughly 6x — though even the power users remain well behind OpenAI's own internal adoption levels.

By sector, Codex adoption has grown fastest in Legal, up a striking 108x since February 2026. OpenRouter data (via chartmaker Peter Walker) shows agents using nearly 5x as many tokens as humans, with agent usage itself up roughly 14x since February. More than 85% of agentic token burn now comes from cached prompts — a reflection of how agents, unlike humans' single prompt-and-response exchanges, iterate toward a goal and progressively build up the cache as they work.

## The Automation-Tooling Landscape Is Shifting — From Zapier Toward Gumloop

Similarweb data shows traffic to automation tooling sites has been in serial decline everywhere except Gumloop. N8N, Zapier, and Make — all of which predate LLMs — are each seeing double-digit percentage declines on a trailing 12-week basis, while Gumloop, launched in 2023 as an "AI native agent builder," is the only automation platform gaining traction by Similarweb's numbers. The piece cautions it's "still far too early to stick a fork in Zapier et al" — they have AI at their disposal too — but concludes that agents, despite barely having arrived, are already beginning to reshape the automation-tooling landscape.
