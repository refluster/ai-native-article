## Executive Summary

The Financial Times reported that OpenAI would fall about $20 billion short of $70 billion in annualized revenue by the end of 2026, and tech stocks sold off sharply on Thursday. Bloomberg, citing unnamed sources, countered that OpenAI expects to hit or top $70 billion, calling the lower figure "a projection." Part of the gap may come from how revenue is counted, and analysts say demand strength is the better gauge of industry health than any single number.

## What was reported

The source is a Proactive Investors article published on 9 October 2026. Its facts:

- Bloomberg, citing unnamed sources, reported that OpenAI expects to hit or even top $70 billion in annualized revenue by the end of 2026.
- That pushed back on a Financial Times report that the company would fall about $20 billion short of that figure.
- The FT report put OpenAI under scrutiny on Thursday. Tech stocks sold off sharply: the Nasdaq fell more than 1% and chipmakers fell more than 3%.
- Bloomberg's sources said the lower number was merely "a projection."
## Reading the discrepancy

Wedbush, citing the FT and Reuters, said OpenAI is currently tracking to $50 billion in annualized revenue, compared with the $70 billion that had been floated.

Reuters suggested the higher figure may have come from applying Anthropic's methodology to OpenAI's revenue streams. According to the article, OpenAI's approach nets out expenses paid to cloud partners on sales that pass through those partners.

The point is that the two numbers may not be measured on the same basis. The article does not say the whole gap is explained by methodology, and the figure from Bloomberg's sources is itself anonymous, not an official statement.

Wedbush also noted that both numbers are well above the $20 billion run rate OpenAI reportedly had entering the year.

## Market reaction and analyst views

Kathleen Brooks, research director at XTB, attributed the decline to the revenue figures, saying they raised questions about the sustainability of the AI capital spending boom just ahead of earnings season.

Wedbush said it does not believe the recent recovery in AI supply chain stocks rested on an explicit OpenAI revenue growth number, and pointed to robust demand as the better signal of the industry's health.

Brooks said AI trade skepticism has never lasted long, noting that recent months have brought waves of inflows alongside waves of outflows. Doubts over OpenAI's revenue faded Friday as US equities opened higher. She added that signs of cooling AI growth and a fall in Treasury yields could trigger a rotation back into cyclical sectors, giving non-tech names a chance to catch up.

## A separate development

Also on Friday, OpenAI issued a statement defending the firing of three safety researchers, who had suggested they were let go for raising safety concerns. The article gives no further detail and does not link this to the revenue debate.

## Implications

What the source establishes is that differently sourced revenue estimates for a single unlisted company moved a broad market in a day. OpenAI is not listed, and the only checks on these numbers are anonymously sourced reports.

Two implications follow. First, "annualized revenue" can differ sharply depending on method — whether sales passing through cloud partners are counted gross or net — so comparisons require checking definitions first. Second, on the analysts' demand-based view, the strength of demand, not one company's headline number, is the better basis for judging industry health. Both points restate what the source says; neither measures OpenAI's actual revenue.
