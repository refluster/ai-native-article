## Gemini app revamped at Google I/O 2026 takes on ChatGPT and Claude with “Daily Brief,” “Gemini Spark,” and “Gemini Omni”

## Executive Summary

At Google I/O 2026, Google announced that it is adding “Daily Brief,” a new UI, “Gemini Omni,” and the personal AI agent “Gemini Spark” to the Gemini app. The move explicitly marks a shift from a stand-alone chatbot to an “all-purpose AI hub,” expanding its competitive footing against OpenAI’s ChatGPT and Anthropic’s Claude from conversational AI into everyday work support, always-on agents, and video generation.

As for its existing user base, Google said the Gemini app is available to “more than 900 million monthly users” across “over 230 countries” and in “more than 70 languages.” The latest update is positioned as an effort to broaden use cases while retaining existing users.

## “Daily Brief”: A morning starting point for inbox, schedule, and tasks

Google introduced “Daily Brief” as a personalized digest designed to be the first thing users check each morning. It is being framed not as a simple summary, but as a tool that also prioritizes items and suggests next actions.

- **Evidence**
- Google announced the new feature “Daily Brief.”
- The feature is described as “a personalized digest designed to be your first stop each morning.”
- Its information sources are “a user’s inbox, calendar, and most important tasks.”
- According to Google, the feature does more than summarize: it “prioritizes tasks and suggests next steps,” with “the most important items shown first.”
- Availability is “rolling out today.”
- It is for “Google AI subscribers in the United States.”
- **Conclusion**
- Gemini’s role is expanding from a chat UI that responds whenever a user issues a prompt into an operational layer that proactively presents daily information organization and suggested actions.
- By pulling Gmail- and Calendar-derived information into a single morning view, the basis for comparison with ChatGPT and Claude shifts from conversational performance alone to the density of integration with Google’s existing services.
## “Neural Expressive”: A redesign aimed at breaking up walls of text

Google said it rebuilt the Gemini app “from the ground up,” introducing a new design language called “Neural Expressive.” The way responses are presented has also changed, moving away from the long, uninterrupted blocks of text common in AI chatbots toward a structure that puts the most important information first.

- **Evidence**
- Google said “it rebuilt the app from the ground up.”
- The new design language is “Neural Expressive.”
- Its elements include “fluid animations, vibrant colors, new typography, and haptic feedback.”
- As for how responses are displayed, Gemini is “no longer presented as a wall of text.”
- Instead, “key information appears in bold at the top,” and as users scroll, “Additional text and possibly other elements such as images and timelines appear.”
- **Conclusion**
- The point of differentiation is no longer just model performance, but the experience of consuming AI responses itself.
- A structure that places key points in bold at the top, with details, images, and timelines layered beneath, suggests a mode of use closer to information cards or task screens than to simply reading through a chat log.
## “Gemini Spark”: A personal AI agent that runs in the background 24/7

The most functionally significant change in this announcement is “Gemini Spark.” Google positions it as a “24/7 personal AI agent,” expanding the in-app assistant into a cloud-based presence that continuously works on the user’s behalf.

- **Evidence**
- Google announced “new personal AI agent called Gemini Spark.”
- Spark is described as “a 24/7 personal AI agent that helps you navigate your digital life.”
- Google said Spark “transforms Gemini from an assistant into an active partner that does real work on your behalf.”
- Spark is a “cloud-based agent” that “keeps working in the background even when you lock your phone.”
- Within the Gemini app, Spark lets users create “their own custom workflows.”
- It is currently “in testing.”
- Availability is “next week.”
- It is for “Google AI Ultra subscribers.”
- **Conclusion**
- Google is pushing Gemini beyond a conversational responder and toward an agent responsible for continuous execution, background processing, and workflow automation.
- The fact that it is limited to “Google AI Ultra subscribers” points to a product strategy of placing the most advanced agent experience in the highest paid tier.
## “Gemini Omni”: Bringing knowledge-grounded video generation into the Gemini app strategy

Google also made access to the new AI video model “Gemini Omni” part of the Gemini app update. The defining feature is that it combines Gemini with generative media models to produce outputs that are “grounded in knowledge.”

- **Evidence**
- Google announced access to “new AI video model called Gemini Omni.”
- The model “combines Gemini with Google’s generative media models to create outputs grounded in knowledge.”
- As an example, users can enter a simple prompt such as “claymation explainer of protein folding.”
- According to Google, users can upload audio, images, and video to generate “a consistent, high-quality video.”
- The model is “rolling out to Google Flow and YouTube Shorts for Google AI subscribers.”
- TechCrunch wrote that this intensifies Google’s “competition among major AI platforms in the ongoing race to lead multimodal content generation.”
- **Conclusion**
- The Gemini app’s competitive scope now extends beyond text interaction and task support into multimodal generation, especially video generation.
- Because “Google Flow” and “YouTube Shorts” are explicitly named as rollout destinations, Gemini is being positioned not as a stand-alone app but as a generative foundation spanning Google’s creation and distribution products.
## Scale and product positioning: From a “stand-alone chatbot” to an “all-purpose AI hub”

While highlighting Gemini’s existing scale, Google framed these updates not as a routine feature expansion but as a change in the product’s definition. The aim is to preserve its current user base while competing head-on with ChatGPT and Claude for more time and more use cases.

- **Evidence**
- Google said the Gemini app has “more than 900 million monthly users.”
- It is available in “over 230 countries.”
- It supports “more than 70 languages.”
- TechCrunch described the updates as a signal that Google is trying to turn the Gemini app into “an all-purpose AI hub rather than a stand-alone chatbot.”
- It also wrote that the updates are meant to make Gemini more competitive with “apps like ChatGPT and Claude.”
- The article said Google “clearly wants more and without losing its existing customers.”
- **Conclusion**
- Google is redefining Gemini, already a product with major distribution scale, into a unified hub for chat, information organization, agent execution, and video generation.
- The focus of competition is shifting away from the intelligence of a single model and toward total user value generated through integration into daily workflows, always-on operation, multimodal generation, and tight linkage with Google’s broader product suite.
