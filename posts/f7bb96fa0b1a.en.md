## Executive Summary

Results from Amazon and Intel, reported after the US market closed on Thursday 29 October 2026, showed that the cost of the AI infrastructure race is reaching beyond the technology industry's own balance sheets and into demand for consumer devices. Once debt-light technology giants have become major borrowers to fund data centres, and a memory chip shortage is lifting prices for smartphones and personal computers. This article draws on the Reuters report carried by AFP and published by NST on 30 October 2026, separating what the source states from what follows from it.

## What the results showed

The report gives the following facts.

- Amazon forecast holiday-quarter sales below Wall Street expectations. The report frames this as a possible sign of growing pressure on consumers as inflation picks up.
- Amazon's shares fell 7 per cent in after-hours trading. The AI cloud stocks CoreWeave, Nebius and Iren fell between 5.8 and 6.7 per cent.
- Alphabet closed regular trading nearly 13 per cent lower and fell another 1.5 per cent after hours.
- Intel cited "near-term headwinds across the PC industry", and its shares fell 18 per cent. A memory chip shortage had raised its costs.
- Intel also said a factory explosion in Israel had caused a two-week shutdown and would cost about US$600 million in lost revenue over two quarters.
- AMD fell 4 per cent and Broadcom 2 per cent.
## AI spending is changing how big tech funds itself

The article's central claim is that the AI spending spree is eating into the once-enviable cash flows of major technology companies.

- Amazon recorded a massive cash burn.
- Alphabet and Meta both burned cash in the September quarter.
- Microsoft, the only exception, joined the borrowing spree with a US$25 billion debt sale on Thursday.
- Surging data centre spending has turned once debt-light technology giants into major borrowers.
The report states plainly that the giants have deep pockets to sustain their spending. The wariness is concentrated on smaller AI cloud firms borrowing heavily to meet enormous order backlogs. The declines in CoreWeave, Nebius and Iren are consistent with that concern.

## Spillover to consumer devices

The second theme is how the infrastructure race reaches consumers. According to the article, price increases are squeezing global demand for devices such as smartphones and PCs. In Intel's case, the memory chip shortage drove up costs, and lower PC demand and disappointing forward guidance combined to produce a revenue miss. Kim Caughey Forrest of Bokeh Capital Partners said: "As Google learned today, earnings matter."

The report also carries the opposing view. Some analysts said Intel's problems in PCs, an area that does not attract the same scrutiny on Wall Street as the AI buildout, did not necessarily point to major problems for other chipmakers with greater exposure to AI. Michael James of Rosenblatt Securities said the broader semiconductor weakness after hours was "primarily a function of Intel guide and negative sentiment spillover". The report adds that investors in the less-active after-hours market appeared to react first and ask questions later.

## Where the semiconductor index stands

- The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index (SOX) has been one of 2026's standout performers, rising more than 60 per cent as shortages of chips and equipment drove up prices and profits at companies ranging from Nvidia and Micron to Marvell Technology.
- The momentum has been tested over the past month by tighter financing conditions, which have kept some anticipated initial public offerings on the sidelines and put some highly leveraged AI infrastructure firms under pressure.
- The SOX has declined 13 per cent since its recent high in October and is down 21 per cent from its all-time high in June.
- The Direxion Daily Semiconductor Bull ETF, which offers three times the daily performance of the SOX, fell 9 per cent in the session.
## Evidence and implication

**Evidence (what the source says):** AI data centre spending is cutting into the cash generation of the large technology companies and increasing their reliance on borrowing. At the same time, price increases tied to the memory chip shortage are squeezing device demand, as Intel's PC business showed. The market reacted most sharply to smaller, highly leveraged AI cloud firms.

**Implication (our inference):** The cost of the infrastructure race is leaking out through two channels: capital markets (borrowing and share prices) and real demand (device prices and volumes). As the source indicates, the financial strength of the giants and the fragility of smaller firms borrowing against order backlogs need to be assessed separately. Whether Intel's PC weakness signals broader trouble for AI-exposed chipmakers is disputed even within the source, so this article alone cannot settle it.
