## Executive Summary

In a June 16, 2026 essay, Pragmatic Engineer’s Gergely Orosz argues that since its June 2025 acquisition of Scale AI, Meta has been dismantling its long-praised engineering culture through an AI-first organizational overhaul. As 30–50% of engineers on core teams were forcibly reassigned into a new organization responsible for data labeling, mandatory keystroke monitoring and token-count-based performance reviews expanded, even as the company announced a 10% workforce reduction amid record profits. The essay interprets this series of changes as a structural shift in which engineering is being redefined from a “profit center” into a “cost center.”

## The AI Reorg and the ADO Organization — Forced Transfers on a “One in Five or Six” Scale

As a matter of fact, the reorganization began with the integration of Scale AI.

- In June 2025, Meta acquired 49% of Scale AI for $14.8B. Scale AI CEO Alexandr Wang now leads Meta’s AI strategy. Meanwhile, a $2B acquisition of Manus AI was blocked by Chinese authorities.
- The newly created ADO (Agent Data Optimisation) organization has around 6,500 people, including 4,000–5,000 software engineers. That amounts to roughly one in every five or six of Meta’s approximately 25,000 engineers.
- Since late April 2026, 30–50% of engineers on core teams have been reassigned to data labeling work. Infrastructure and security teams were “especially hard hit,” and Instagram’s Trust and Safety team lost about 50% of its staff.
- The work in these reassigned roles is routine. The article summarizes the procedure as: “Come up with a task that the AI should do / Then write the tests that confirm the result / Package all of this up into a Docker container”
In conclusion, the essay characterizes this reassignment as a move that shifts engineers’ roles away from product development and toward the production of AI training data. CTO Andrew Bosworth himself acknowledged that “the AI reorg was atrocious” and promised better communication going forward. On employee morale, Wired reported one employee saying, “It's literally the gulag. You have zero purpose in life all of a sudden, you barely interact with anyone”.

## The Normalization of Surveillance and “Tokenmaxxing”

As a matter of fact, monitoring and performance metrics changed at the same time.

- In late April 2026, keystroke tracking became mandatory. At first there was no opt-out, though employees were later allowed to pause it in 30-minute increments. Reuters reported: “New controls will allow employees to pause the data collection for up to 30 minutes at a time and request exemptions from the initiative”. In the UK, the policy has not been rolled out because of data protection regulations.
- Token consumption has reached record levels. The article says that “Meta employees used a total of 60.2 trillion AI tokens (!!) in 30 days,” pointing out that 60.2 trillion tokens were used over a 30-day period. At Anthropic’s API rates, that would be equivalent to $900M, though the actual cost is believed to be more than $100M.
- Token counts have been introduced as a metric in PSC (Performance Summary Cycle) reviews, raising concerns about “tokenmaxxing” — inflating token usage to boost performance ratings. PSC is described as “very stringent” compared with Google and Apple, with managers fighting over compensation pools and “knock[ing] down” engineers on other teams.
In conclusion, the essay argues that the normalization of surveillance, combined with evaluation metrics that measure consumption rather than output, is creating incentives that distort engineer behavior.

## Layoffs Amid Record Profits and the Collapse of Reliability

As a matter of fact, workforce cuts and service failures coincided.

- On April 20, a 10% reduction across the entire workforce was announced, with May 20 set as the target date for the first wave. The four-week notice period amplified anxiety inside the organization.
- Security incidents continued. On May 30, 2026, Instagram suffered an account-takeover incident that was described as “most embarrassing outage in Meta's history”. On June 12, a SEV0 (full outage) affected Facebook, Instagram, and Messenger. Security engineer Siddharth Sundharam described it as “The first proper zero auth password reset I've seen in production”. CISO Guy Rosen resigned on June 2, 2026.
- For comparison, the company’s worst outage in 2021 lasted 7 hours and was caused by DNS/BGP issues.
In conclusion, the essay links the hollowing out of infrastructure and security teams through reassignment to the series of outages, portraying the decline in reliability as a consequence of organizational choices.

## The Origins of the Culture and Where It Stands Now

As a matter of fact, Meta’s culture was once codified.

- In 2012, Facebook codified its engineering culture in the roughly 70-page “Little Red Book.” Its slogan, “move fast and break things,” later evolved into “move-fast-with-stable-infra.”
- Llama’s release history shows a loss of momentum: Llama 1 (February 2023), Llama 2 (June 2023), Llama 3 (April 2024), Llama 4 (April 2025, judged “deeply disappointing”). The essay places the last positive assessment of Meta’s engineering culture in 2022.
In conclusion, drawing on Chief Product Officer Chris Cox’s reaction — “It's like what the fuck. It is like what the fuck.” — and HashiCorp founder Mitchell Hashimoto’s observation that “I strongly believe there are entire companies right now under heavy 'AI psychosis',” Orosz argues that Meta’s all-in tilt toward AI is burning through cultural capital built up over more than a decade. This is not just an HR problem at one company; it can also be read as part of a broader reorganization across big tech as AI investment becomes the top priority.
