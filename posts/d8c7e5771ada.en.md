## Executive Summary

On July 10, 2026, Apple sued OpenAI in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California. Apple alleges that, through the hiring of former Apple executives, trade secrets including technical information on unreleased products and Apple's proprietary metal finishing technology were systematically taken. As OpenAI pushes ahead with development of its first hardware product, this is a case in which the movement of talent has turned into an intellectual property conflict between companies.

## The Core of the Lawsuit — When, Where, and Who

- On Friday, July 10, 2026, Apple filed suit against OpenAI in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California.
- Individuals named in the complaint:
- **Tang Tan** — OpenAI's Chief Hardware Officer. He previously spent 24 years at Apple, where he served as VP of product design for the iPhone and Apple Watch.
- **Chang Liu** — An OpenAI employee. He previously spent 8 years at Apple as a senior system electrical engineer.
- **Jony Ive** — Former Apple designer. His startup, "io," was acquired by OpenAI in 2025 for $6.5 billion. He is mentioned in the complaint, but he is not named as a defendant accused of wrongdoing.
- Apple's statement: "Recently, significant evidence has emerged suggesting individuals employed by OpenAI wrongfully took Apple's secret and confidential information."
## Misconduct Apple Alleges

Apple argues that OpenAI orchestrated the systematic theft of trade secrets, and the complaint lists specific methods it says were used. The allegations presented as fact are as follows:

- Using codenames for Apple's confidential projects during recruiting activity
- Asking candidates to bring Apple hardware components to interviews
- Instructing departing Apple employees on how to evade security procedures
- Attempting to elicit details about unreleased products
- Failing to return Apple-issued laptops containing confidential technical documents
- Downloading and sharing Apple's technical specifications, engineering materials, and proprietary project data
- Improperly using Apple's proprietary metal finishing technology
This is not framed as a one-off leak of information. It is a claim that the hiring process itself was used as a channel for intelligence gathering. Apple is portraying this not as individual engineers changing jobs, but as systematic acquisition through the recruitment mechanism itself.

## Background and Timeline — The Context of "Moving Into Hardware"

- OpenAI has been reported to be developing its first hardware product, and as of April 2026, there was speculation that it could be a smartphone equipped with AI agents.
- Before filing suit, Apple sent a letter to OpenAI in February 2026 expressing its concerns, but received no response.
- The relief Apple is seeking includes an injunction barring OpenAI from using or disclosing the trade secrets, the return of the materials that were taken, and preservation of evidence.
- OpenAI's response: "We have no interest in other companies' trade secrets. We remain focused on building innovative technology that empowers people everywhere."
## What This Lawsuit Reveals

What is established as fact at this point is the filing of the lawsuit and the claims made by both sides; the court's findings of fact are still to come. Even so, the structure of the dispute suggests the following implications.

- Regardless of whether Apple's claims are ultimately upheld, the focus here is not model weights or algorithms, but the "manufacturing know-how" required to build physical products. Metal finishing technology and unreleased product specifications are less the kind of thing that can simply be taken as code than a form of tacit knowledge accumulated over years of employment.
- OpenAI began as a software company, but at the moment it steps into physical products in the form of a smartphone, its hiring of people who spent 24 years and 8 years at Apple respectively has itself blurred the boundary between the two companies. The movement of talent is also the movement of knowledge, and the court will be asked to draw that line.
- OpenAI has categorically denied any involvement. Accordingly, this case is likely to become one of the first in which a court examines the boundary issue created when an AI company expands into hardware — where talent, intellectual property, and manufacturing technology intersect.
