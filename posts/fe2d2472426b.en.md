## Executive Summary

On 20 November 2026, The Kobeissi Letter wrote that US stocks had lost $6 trillion in market value in 45 days from the 6 October record high, and answered the question "Is there an AI bubble and did it pop?" with "no." It names AI capex, the midterms, Fed hikes, the Nvidia and AMD controversies and liquidity drained by Anthropic's IPO as drivers, and argues much of this is already priced in and within the range of a normal correction. It does not claim the bottom is in. These are the source's claims, not independently verified facts.

## What the source is

The source is a market commentary published on X, "-$6 Trillion In 45 Days: Is This A "Bubble" Or An Opportunity?" (an 8-minute read, posted 20 November 2026). Its author describes itself as the official account of a commentary service on global capital markets, and the piece promotes a paid daily-analysis subscription and a chart of its own strategy's performance ("nearly five times the S&P 500 since 2020").

Every figure and causal claim below is therefore attributed to the source. This article does not verify them independently.

## What the source says happened

According to the source, US stocks lost an average of -$225 billion per day for 31 straight trading days, counted from 6 October, the last time the S&P 500 hit a record. In that window came Iran War headlines, the Q3 2026 earnings season, concerns about AI capex and free cash flow, the October Fed meeting, the US midterm elections and the Nvidia controversy, for a total loss of -$6 trillion in market cap.

The author frames the whole argument around one question: "What has changed and what is priced in?"

## Six drivers, and the source's reply to each

### 1. AI capex and free cash flow

The source says the selloff began with concern over capex at Amazon, Alphabet, Meta and Microsoft. Their combined capex rose +$36.9 billion in Q3 2026 to a record $201.9 billion, more than doubling over twelve months and up +356% since Q1 2024. Big Tech has spent $1.6 trillion over three years.

The author calls these "real concerns," but argues record infrastructure spending is a necessary condition of a technological revolution and a spell of negative free cash flow is the short-term cost of a long-term buildout. Clarity on return on that capex is likely only in Q1/Q2 2027, and fears will persist until then.

### 2. The midterms and the end of the "Trump Put"

The author believes a large part of the drawdown stems from the midterm results. With a Democratic "sweep," the "Trump Put," the notion that the President will intervene when markets get ugly, has effectively ended. A US congresswoman's 4 November call for an AI ban added to this, and markets have priced in aggressive regulation and possible bans once Democrats take control in January 2027.

The author nonetheless insists a US AI ban will not happen: it would be hard to enforce and would mean surrendering the AI race to China. More regulation and likely new taxes, yes; the market is assuming something more extreme.

### 3. A Fed hiking into an anxious market

The source says the Iran War is nearing its 300th day, US inflation has been above the Fed's 2% target for 67 straight months, and Fed Chair Warsh has made lowering inflation to 2% his top priority. Building AI therefore gets more expensive. US investment-grade corporate bond sales are expected to rise +$37 billion year on year in November (+27%) to $175 billion, the highest for any November on record.

Oil prices, meanwhile, fell about 22% this month. The author thinks that if the end of the Iran War becomes clearer, the Fed's outlook will likely change, and sees growing odds of less restrictive policy into mid-2027, though "higher for longer" holds for now.

### 4. Nvidia, AMD and China chips

The source says Nvidia has lost Jensen Huang and its stock is down -40% in a matter of weeks. A 19 November short-seller report accused AMD of smuggling chips into China, a claim the author calls "far from substantiated." The tech sector's forward P/E has fallen to its lowest since March 2020.

Nvidia's forward P/E is 9x. Despite guidance of about $750 billion in revenue for FY2028, the author reads this as the market not believing the "E." The author says it took the early stance that Huang is innocent until an independent investigation establishes the facts, and called for the board to leave the door open for his return.

### 5. Liquidity and the Anthropic IPO

The source says Anthropic's IPO raised over $100 billion, the biggest IPO in history, and strained market liquidity. Institutional demand reached $400 billion and retail orders $100 billion, nearly 8x the initial $64.4 billion raise at a $78 share price.

The VIX is up over +100% from its October low and posted its biggest daily gain since February 2018 on 5 November. Stocks, gold and crypto all fell, and hedge funds posted their largest trailing six-week net outflow from tech in over a decade. The author calls it "a dash for the exit" and expects stability once liquidity returns.

### 6. Sentiment

The author says its 15 October analysis, "Stealth Bear Market," had already identified a bear market beneath the surface of the S&P 500, particularly in tech. The midterms and the Nvidia affair only accelerated a shift that had begun earlier.

## The conclusion, and what it rests on

The answer is "no": the AI bubble has not popped. The support offered is S&P 500 Q3 2026 EPS growth of +31.9% year on year, the strongest in at least four years, and earnings beating estimates for 13 consecutive quarters. In the author's words: "There has never been a market with so much fear yet so much growth."

It says another 10% fall would still be a healthy correction and urges caution about calls for a 30%+ drop. The market is pricing sustained higher rates, aggressive AI regulation, disappointing AI returns and uncertainty around key companies all at once, which leaves room for positive surprise.

It does not call the bottom. Volatility will stay elevated, liquidity remains constrained, and further downside is "entirely possible." It favours high-quality tech companies with strong balance sheets and accelerating earnings, and expects dip buyers to return as they did in April 2025.

## Evidence versus conclusion

The evidence the source offers is numerical: Big Tech capex, S&P 500 EPS growth, bond issuance, VIX moves, IPO demand. The conclusions are the author's interpretations: that the Trump Put is over, that regulation fears are overdone, that Nvidia's price over-discounts Huang's absence, and that liquidity will return. The piece also promotes a paid service and its own track record, which a reader should discount accordingly.

Most of the figures appear in charts with no links to underlying data. This article could not inspect the charts and quotes only the numbers in the text.

## Implications

The framework's practical value is that it splits one story into separate problems: capex returns, politics, rates, company governance and liquidity. Each resolves on a different timeline, and the source gives a clear date only for capex returns (Q1/Q2 2027).

For anyone weighing AI-infrastructure exposure, the useful question is not "bubble or not" but which factor resolves first and which lingers. The source argues prices already lean pessimistic; lacking independent verification, it is best read as one strong claim among others.
