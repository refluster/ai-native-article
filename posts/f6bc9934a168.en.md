## Anthropic Secretly Files for IPO, Laying the Groundwork for Public Markets Right After a $965 billion Valuation

## Executive Summary

Anthropic announced on 2026年6月1日 that it had confidentially filed a draft registration statement with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) in preparation for an initial public offering (IPO). The move came immediately after it raised $65 billion in a Series H round, pushing its valuation to $965 billion, and placing Anthropic itself at the center of an overheated IPO market that includes OpenAI and SpaceX.

## Confidential Filing With the SEC and Where It Fits in the IPO Process

Anthropic has entered the stage of advancing IPO preparations before finalizing its offering terms.

- **Evidence**
- On 2026年6月1日, Anthropic said in a blog post that it had confidentially filed for an “initial public offering.”
- The filing was made with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission.
- The company submitted “a draft registration statement.”
- At this point, neither “the number of shares” nor “the price” has been set.
- The company said the proposed IPO would depend on “market conditions and other factors.”
- The article explains that a confidential IPO filing allows a company to move forward with listing preparations without publicly disclosing detailed financial information, risks, and internal business information.
- If the process advances, the next step would be to file an “S-1 registration document,” which would disclose financials, legal matters, risks, and voting power holdings.
- **Conclusion**
- This announcement does not mean the company has decided to go public; it means it has begun preparing privately to assess eligibility and market timing.
- Anthropic has secured room to prepare disclosures on its finances, legal matters, and control structure before facing scrutiny from the public markets.
## The $65 billion Series H Raise and a $965 billion Valuation

The IPO filing came immediately after a massive private fundraising round.

- **Evidence**
- The filing came “less than a week after” Anthropic raised $65 billion in a Series H round.
- That round brought the company’s valuation to $965 billion.
- The round was co-led by Altimeter Capital, Dragoneer, Greenoaks, Sequoia Capital, Capital Group, Coatue, and D1 Capital Partners.
- According to the article, “a bevy of institutional and strategic investors” joined the round in anticipation of an IPO.
- The article also describes Anthropic as being “valued at close to $1 trillion.”
- **Conclusion**
- The scale of the private-market raise suggests it was functioning not only to secure operating capital, but also to establish price discovery and test investor demand ahead of an IPO.
- The confidential filing reinforces the idea that the Series H fundraising was not a standalone event, but part of a continuous capital strategy premised on a transition to the public markets.
## A “White-Hot IPO Season” Alongside OpenAI and SpaceX

Anthropic’s move is framed not as an isolated financing decision, but as part of an IPO environment crowded with AI and major tech offerings.

- **Evidence**
- The article describes the current moment as “an already white-hot IPO season.”
- In the same context, SpaceX’s IPO is described as “targeting a $2 trillion valuation” and “seeking to raise more than $75 billion.”
- Anthropic’s filing is portrayed as following OpenAI’s “a $122 billion round at an $852 billion post-money valuation” in 3月.
- OpenAI is also described as it “prepares for its own IPO” and “is expected to file for an initial public offering.”
- The article says IPOs from Anthropic and OpenAI would “pit the two largest AI labs against each other” and test the market’s appetite and tolerance for AI.
- **Conclusion**
- Anthropic’s IPO preparations are not merely a financing event for a single AI model company; they are one of the comparative transactions through which the public markets will decide how to price frontier AI labs.
- Investors’ benchmarks will be shaped not only by Anthropic’s standalone growth, but also by relative comparisons with giant offerings such as OpenAI and SpaceX.
## From the Company Behind Claude to an AI Powerhouse With Expanding Revenue

Anthropic is shifting from “a distant competitor” to a revenue-generating company with major enterprise customers.

- **Evidence**
- Anthropic is the developer of Claude.
- The company was founded in 2021 by former OpenAI employees.
- The article says Anthropic had long been seen as “a distant competitor” to OpenAI and ChatGPT.
- It is now described as “an AI powerhouse that has landed top-tier enterprise customers.”
- The factors cited as drawing investors and customers are “growing capabilities” and “a focus on enterprise services.”
- On the revenue side, its revenue run-rate “surpassed $47 billion,” up from “$9 billion at the end of 2025.”
- **Conclusion**
- Anthropic’s valuation is grounded not only in expectations for research and development, but also in rapid monetization supported by enterprise services.
- Market perception has shifted from seeing it as an OpenAI alternative to viewing it as a major operating company capable of being treated as a large-scale public-market deal.
## Limited Release of Mythos and Reports of Access for the EU Cybersecurity Agency

The article explicitly points to broader availability of Mythos as a potential next driver of growth acceleration.

- **Evidence**
- The article says wider availability of Mythos is one factor that “could accelerate” Anthropic’s revenue growth rate.
- Anthropic previewed Mythos in 4月.
- However, access has remained restricted, and the company warned software developers that “the model had discovered thousands of high-severity bugs that would need to be fixed before it could be made public.”
- Bloomberg reported on the morning of 6月1日, citing anonymous sources, that Anthropic was preparing to give the European Union’s cybersecurity agency access to Mythos.
- **Conclusion**
- Mythos is being treated not merely as a new model, but as an asset whose commercial rollout speed is directly tied to risk management.
- The statement that it found “thousands of high-severity bugs” shows that Anthropic itself recognizes both the model’s capabilities and the need to carefully control the scope of its release.
