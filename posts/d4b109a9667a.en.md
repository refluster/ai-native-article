## Executive Summary

On October 28, 2026, Anthropic announced that Claude for Legal now includes ready-to-use artifacts for legal work. Artifacts are interactive tools, such as trackers and dashboards, that teams can use and share in Claude. Each one opens with an example, and legal teams adapt it with their own data and connectors and share it across the business. The design assumes a division of labor: Claude completes the first pass, and a lawyer applies judgment to the results.

## What was announced

The source is Anthropic's "Introducing artifacts for legal work" (a Claude Enterprise announcement dated October 28, 2026).

- Anthropic says that since it launched Claude for Legal in May, the most common requests from legal teams have been for simpler setup and easier ways to work with colleagues.
- Artifacts are defined as interactive tools, such as trackers and dashboards, that teams can use and share in Claude.
- The new legal gallery covers specific tasks, such as reviewing a stack of contracts, tracking regulatory changes, and taking in legal requests from the business.
- Each artifact opens with an example, so legal professionals can see how the work comes together before adding their own data and connectors.
- Each answer or output has a link to its source.
- Anthropic also announced funding for public interest groups building artifacts for access-to-justice work, funding for legal education programs teaching tomorrow's lawyers to build with Claude, and a Legal Ambassadors Program so legal professionals can learn from each other.
## The built-in artifacts

Anthropic lists six artifacts that cover the work legal teams raised most often. A legal professional brings their own documents and sources, Claude completes the first pass, and a lawyer applies their judgment to the results.

- **Cite check** compares a legal document with its sources and flags quotes that don't match, wrong page numbers, and sources that don't support the claim.
- **Jurisdiction survey matrix** compares a legal topic, such as data breach notice rules, across states or countries in one table, with links to the underlying law.
- **Matter board** turns engagement letters, emails, or spreadsheets into one board of open matters, showing the owner, next step, and next deadline.
- **Playbook deviation board** compares contracts with your company's standard terms, flags each clause that differs, and sends it to the person who approves exceptions.
- **Regulatory horizon scan** maps upcoming regulations on a timeline, based on your company's business and where it operates, and ranks them by impact with links to the sources.
- **Tabular review** builds a table from a set of contracts, showing the terms you ask about, such as severance or non-competes, with links to the exact language.
## Tailoring and sharing

- Legal professionals can ask Claude to tailor artifacts to their team and use connectors to bring in their playbooks or research sources, so nobody has to upload documents manually.
- Since launching Claude for Legal, Anthropic has added more than 60 legal connectors, including Bloomberg Law, Filevine, Ivo, Legora, and Patlytics.
- Artifacts enable collaboration inside and outside the organization. Sharing settings are described as ensuring privacy and supporting ethical walls between teams.
## Artifacts built by partners

Legal tech companies and law firms built artifacts that sit in the same gallery.

- **AI Governance Benchmark**, built by Goodwin, benchmarks a company's approach to AI governance to surface potential gaps.
- **Harvey Intake**, built by Harvey, gives business users one place in Claude to send legal questions and requests, answering routine ones from Legal's own guidance in Harvey and routing the rest to a lawyer, whose reply comes back in Claude.
- **Obligations Checker**, built by Foley & Lardner, reviews agreements for the obligations of both parties and helps validate expectations pre-closing and track expectations post-closing.
- **Subpoena Response Tool**, built by PwC, turns your team's precedents, standard objections and templates into automated, audit-ready first drafts.
More artifacts are coming from Legora and Orrick.

## Access to justice and the community

The gallery includes nine access-to-justice artifacts, five of which were built by nonprofit and public interest organizations supported with Claude credits.

- **Criminal Charge Reality Check**, built by Zealous, takes a criminal charge and shows defense teams the sentencing range, public data on how those cases usually end, and standard playbooks for release. New York only.
- **Immigration Papers Reader**, built by Mobile Pathways, turns a client's immigration paperwork into a plain-language first read of key dates and missing documents.
- **Rent Ledger Reviewer**, built by Rising for Justice, helps attorneys find errors in a landlord's rent ledger when preparing an eviction case. Based on D.C. law.
- **Record-Sealing Screener**, built by Rising for Justice, helps attorneys assess whether a client's criminal record is eligible for sealing. Based on D.C. law.
- **Record Clearance**, built by The Access Project, helps legal aid teams work out whether a client's record can be cleared under their state's rules.
This builds on the Oxford Justice Innovators Programme, a 12-month program run with the Oxford Institute of Technology and Justice and Microsoft that supports up to 10 innovators expanding access to justice. The Legal Ambassadors Program is a community for legal professionals who build with Claude; there is a waitlist.

## What the customer quotes show

The announcement carries many customer quotes. Few contain figures.

- Scott Stevenson, Co-Founder and CEO of Spellbook, says the company is "approaching 500,000 contracts per month moving through Spellbook."
- Sanj Ahilan, Chief Research Officer at Solve Intelligence, says that on its chemical structure benchmark, "Claude Opus 5.5 correctly identifies over 10% more structures than Opus 5 while using less than half the output tokens."
- Min-Kyu Jung, CEO and Co-Founder of Ivo, describes contracts kept in Ivo turned into shareable artifacts, such as a renewal calendar that tracks every notice date across a portfolio, with every answer backed by the contract language it came from and Ivo staying the system of record.

## Assessment: what changed and what is unverified

**What the source establishes.** The unit of distribution has moved from a model to a working template that opens with an example and carries source links. All six built-in artifacts are document-reading, table-building tasks: cross-checking (Cite check, Playbook deviation board), cross-comparison (Jurisdiction survey matrix, Tabular review), and tracking deadlines and matters (Matter board, Regulatory horizon scan). Every artifact output links to its source.

**What follows from it.** Because a legal professional can see how an artifact behaves on an example before adding their own data and connectors, the barrier to adoption is lower than with a blank prompt. Partner artifacts from Harvey, Ivo and Legora keep an existing system of record in place and use Claude as the front door, so the stated direction is to connect to those systems rather than replace them.

**What is not shown.** The announcement gives no measured accuracy, false-positive rate, or turnaround time for any artifact. The figures it does contain are customer quotes, such as Spellbook's monthly contract volume, and none measures the artifacts themselves. A team deciding whether to adopt them will need to test them on its own contracts and documents.
