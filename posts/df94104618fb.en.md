## Executive Summary

On its second-quarter earnings call, Shopify said AI-driven traffic and orders to its stores tripled year-over-year while traditional search sessions rose 1.3x over the past two years and still account for roughly a third of all storefront sessions. President Harley Finkelstein framed AI as "a complement to search, rather than a substitute for it." What matters is that the call gave a concrete structural reason why the same AI search that is depressing click-through rates in online publishing is running the other way in commerce.

## The Breakdown — 3x and 1.3x in the Same Quarter

According to TechCrunch's 5 August 2026 report on the earnings call, Shopify's second-quarter revenue rose 36% to $3.6 billion, beating Wall Street's forecast of $3.4 billion. Gross operating profit rose 31% to $1.71 billion, also ahead of the $1.63 billion analysts expected. The company credited the beat at least partly to AI search.

The figures given:

- AI-driven traffic and orders to Shopify stores tripled year-over-year in the second quarter.
- Traditional search sessions are up 1.3x over the past two years: "Traditional search sessions are up 1.3x over the past two years, holding roughly a third of all storefront sessions."
- Half of all AI-referred sessions land directly on a product description page — 2.5 times more than with traditional search.
- 75% of AI-attributed purchases in Q2 happened outside the top 100 categories, what Shopify calls its "sweet spot."
Separate the evidence from the inference. The evidence is that "AI referrals tripled" and "traditional search grew 1.3x and still holds about a third of sessions" are both true of the same quarter. The inference Shopify draws is that AI growth is showing up as an increase in total volume rather than as share taken from search. Finkelstein put it this way: "In fact, search remains one of our largest sources of buyer traffic to our merchants, and it's still growing."

## Why It Complements — Keyword Matching Versus Constraint Satisfaction

The company explained why AI search works for its business as a difference in how search engines and AI agents operate.

Finkelstein's framing: "While search engines rank by popularity against a handful of keywords, AI agents make multiple calls into Shopify's catalog, working with richer structured data to match products with the buyer's specific intent, rather than just keywords."

The example he gave was a buyer looking for the best car seat that fits three across a sedan. "An agent, however, understands the actual need, the dimensions, the vehicle type, and the fact that they need three. It searches across all of those constraints at once to find the product that actually works, not just the one that ranks highest." Traditional search focuses on the keyword "car seat"; the agent traverses dimensions, vehicle type and quantity simultaneously.

Read structurally, that is a move from a ranking problem to a constraint-satisfaction problem. Ranking returns what is surfaced highest; constraint satisfaction returns what actually qualifies. The figure that half of AI-referred sessions land directly on a product description page is that move showing up as a measurably shorter path to purchase.

## The Benefit Skews to the Long Tail

Shopify's emphasis was that the effect is not evenly distributed: AI has particularly benefited the long tail of e-commerce, including the smaller merchants that make up the majority of its customer base. The supporting figure is that 75% of AI-attributed purchases in the quarter occurred outside the top 100 categories.

Under ranking-based search, a small merchant's product that cannot win a top slot is effectively invisible. Under constraint-based retrieval, the question is whether the product qualifies, which demotes category popularity as a selection criterion. The 75% figure is where that structural change lands in the revenue distribution.

The company also suggested it stands to benefit from AI playing a larger role in transactions thanks to its trusted checkout experience. On implementation, it has built connectors to Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity, Manus, Replit and Vercel, as well as to vibe-coding platforms like Lovable that let merchants build on Shopify however they choose.

## The Same Technology Runs Opposite Ways in Publishing and Commerce

The contrast worth holding is what AI summaries are doing to online publishing, where they have led to a measurable drop in click-through rates, which lowers traffic and consequently eats into advertising revenues.

The same AI search removes the intermediate click in one case and compresses the path to purchase in the other. What differs is where the revenue sits. Publishing monetises the click itself — revenue lives on the path. Commerce monetises the transaction at the end of it. When AI compresses the path, a business that charges along the path loses what was compressed, and a business that charges at the terminus gains the arrival rate.

So Shopify's numbers are not evidence for the general claim that AI will not kill search. They are evidence for a narrower one: in businesses whose revenue sits at the end of the path, AI search shows up as a complement. Drop that distinction and the publishing decline and the commerce increase look contradictory. They are two faces of the same mechanism.
