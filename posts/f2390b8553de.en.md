Google is putting $17 billion into a Wyoming data center and supplying its own power. Reddit, with search traffic falling, is shifting its site to a logged-in experience. The OCC has issued guidance for bank examiners that declines to treat AI as a separate risk category. Power, data and regulation are three unrelated domains, yet one principle explains all three: the party that pulls a formerly external constraint inside a boundary it controls takes the initiative.

## Power: from something procured to something designed in

According to "Google, $17 billion in Wyoming: 702 MW of fuel cells and carbon capture build 'self-supplied power' into the design", Google announced on 5 November 2026 that it will build the state's first data center in Laramie County, investing $17 billion over the long term. The core is a 702 MW fuel-cell development contracted with AEP, with carbon capture and storage. The company also says it will bear 100% of the power it uses. Start and completion dates have not been announced.

The structure matters more than the amount. Power is the biggest constraint on where a data center can go, and it used to be bought through the grid, on the terms of an outside utility. Google placed generation at the center of the build and declared the cost of the power as its own responsibility. Power is no longer a condition procured from outside; it is a component inside the design. That no start date is public also shows this internalization is still at the stage of an announcement.

## Data: from public pages to what sits behind a login

"As search traffic dries up, Reddit shifts to a logged-in experience: what the Q3 numbers show and what they aim at" reports that at its third-quarter earnings call the company said it will move the site mainly to a logged-in experience. Search referrals fell by more than one million a week on average in October. Revenue still rose 53% year on year to $895 million. The stated aims are protecting data from AI scraping and licensing deals with model providers.

Traffic is down while revenue is up. That combination suggests search, the external entry point, is no longer a requirement for Reddit's revenue. Public pages were equally open to search engines and scrapers. A login requirement lets the company decide who reads what. Data shifts from something that leaks out by default to something sold by contract. As with power, it is a move to bring a formerly external path under the company's own control.

## Regulation: from a technology label to use and harm

According to "OCC issues first AI guidance for bank examiners: no separate 'AI risk category'", the OCC on 4 November published its first framework for how examiners assess banks' use of AI. It does not treat AI as an independent risk category; it looks at how AI is used in day-to-day operations and how large the harm from AI errors could be. It is not a binding new rule.

This looks opposite to the other two, but the structure is the same. The regulator stopped drawing its boundary around the label "AI" and placed it inside the existing risk-management frame. AI becomes part of work the bank is already accountable for, not a special external factor. A bank must therefore be able to explain how it uses AI and what an error would cost, not merely whether it uses it. Responsibility sits inside the boundary of the party that uses the tool, not the party that supplies it.

## The shared principle: the constraint is controlled by whoever pulls it inside

The three facts suggest the following.

- Google placed an external constraint, power, inside its own boundary through design and a declaration of cost.
- Reddit placed an external constraint, the path to its data, under its own management through login.
- The OCC placed an external technology category, AI, inside the existing frame of use and harm, tying responsibility to the user.
Why does this happen? In the AI era, the power computation needs, the data used for training and generation, and the harm caused by errors become the real constraints on competition and accountability. The tighter the constraint, the costlier it is to leave it outside. Without power, computation stops; if data is used freely, no payment follows; if responsibility for errors is vague, someone suddenly absorbs the loss. So each party redraws the boundary of what it controls and tries to bring the constraint inside.

The principle is falsifiable. If parties that leave the constraint outside keep growing as fast as those that internalize it, the principle is wrong: for example, an operator that keeps buying power from the grid expanding data centers at the same pace, or a site that does not require login earning licensing revenue at the same rate. Reddit's 53% revenue growth despite falling traffic is consistent with the principle, but it is a single case.

## Forecast and implications

If the principle holds, the following changes are likely.

First, more operators will declare self-supply or contractual lock-in for resources they now rely on others for, such as power and data. The grounds are Google's self-supplied power announcement and Reddit's stated aims of data protection and licensing. I put the probability at medium to high, though since Google's start date is unannounced, how far the declaration is carried out is unconfirmed.

Second, regulation will move toward assigning responsibility by use and magnitude of harm rather than by technology label. The ground is the OCC's explicit choice not to create a separate category, but because this is non-binding guidance, whether other regulators follow is unknown. I put the probability at medium.

Third, parties that cannot pull the constraint inside will end up dependent on those that can. As Reddit's licensing deals show, whoever holds the entry point to the data sets the terms.

A practical takeaway: list the external constraints your business or team depends on, in terms of power, data and responsibility. Then check which you can bring under your control and which are held by someone else. That is the starting point for the next move. What separates parties from here on is not how new the technology is, but which constraints they hold inside.
