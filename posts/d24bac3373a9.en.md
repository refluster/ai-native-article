## Executive Summary

On 5 November 2026 the AI evaluation group METR published an assessment finding that Anthropic's models are gaining capability on clearly defined tasks about twice as fast as in the 19 months to October 2025. METR attributes most of the speed-up to Anthropic's own heavy use of AI in research. Anthropic, however, redacted most of the evidence METR found most informative, so the public must lean on METR's judgement.

## What was measured

- METR staff worked inside Anthropic from 12 August to 29 October 2026. They were given internal figures on pretraining, reinforcement learning, compute use and benchmarks, plus time with staff.
- The central measure was AECI, Anthropic's in-house take on Epoch's capabilities index. Since October 2025 those scores have risen at an estimated 1.9 times the earlier rate, with a 95% confidence interval of 1.5 to 2.8 times.
- METR's framing: roughly two years of past progress packed into one.
- METR says it is confident the jump did not come from faster growth in training compute. What employees told METR fits a picture in which AI help, rather than AI working alone, doubled the pace, though METR heard there are signs autonomous contributions will soon grow.
- METR saw no evidence of significant bottlenecks in algorithms, compute or data over the next 12 months, but says its view changed several times and it is not confident in that assessment.
## How uncertain the number is

METR treats its central estimate as highly uncertain and would not be surprised by anything from 1.3 to 3 times. Two conditions could make it too high: benchmark gains may overstate real-world impact, or the historical rate it measures against may have been unusually slow.

## The redaction problem

- Under the agreement, Anthropic could redact non-public information. It removed most of what METR considered the strongest evidence, describing this as protecting information that could help rival developers.
- METR says the public must therefore rely on its judgement far more than is ideal.
- METR took no payment; Anthropic supplied laptops and token credits.
## Conclusion: evaluation only works if evidence reaches outsiders

The points above are the source's account; what follows is the implication drawn from it.

- The risk under examination is that AI accelerating AI research could bring sudden jumps in dangerous capability. METR warns that, without observed bottlenecks, further progress could speed up acceleration itself.
- The outlet that reported this argues that the redactions are the policy lesson. An evaluation-led oversight model depends on evidence reaching outsiders. Here a lab with unprecedented openness still withheld the material that mattered most.
- Two tests come next: whether other labs grant similar access, and whether regulators get to see what the public could not. METR's full report runs to around 100 pages.
