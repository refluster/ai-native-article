## Executive Summary

On October 2, 2026, Amazon Web Services (AWS) announced that it will stop requiring nondisclosure agreements (NDAs) with government agencies involved in its data center projects, and committed more than $1 billion over five years through a program called Built Together, covering education, workforce development, energy affordability and water preservation. The backdrop is that more than 100 U.S. communities are weighing moratoriums on new data centers. The environmental figures are company-reported, however, and Amazon does not separate AI-specific facilities from conventional cloud facilities in them. The credibility of the package will be set by annual disclosures and independent verification, not by the announcement.

## What was announced

The source is a Gadget Review article dated October 5, 2026. According to it, AWS CEO Matt Garman said on October 2 that more than 100 U.S. communities are considering data center moratoriums, and Amazon announced a package of transparency measures, community investment and environmental benchmarks in response.

The commitments as reported:

- **NDAs dropped:** AWS will no longer require NDAs with government agencies involved in its data center projects.
- **Earlier disclosure:** community open houses, and project details disclosed earlier in the development process.
- **Annual figures:** energy consumption, water use, and the share of electricity from carbon-free supplies, published each year.
- **Built Together:** more than $1 billion over five years to education, workforce development, energy affordability, water preservation and locally selected priorities. It runs alongside Amazon's existing community spending, not in place of it.
The article adds that whether local governments then release related records to the public is a separate question. Dropping the secrecy requirement does not by itself put information in residents' hands.

## The numbers behind the promises

The article reports the following (attributed to Amazon and not independently verified):

- **Education:** more than 300,000 students could receive access to free community college education, with eligible out-of-pocket costs covered after financial aid is applied.
- **Workforce:** three Modular Training Centers operating today, six more under development and 16 additional planned, with a stated goal of training up to 100,000 workers annually by the end of 2028.
- **Efficiency upgrades:** more than 300 schools and community buildings and 30,000 homes. Amazon projects participating households could cut energy bills by 20% to 40%, or roughly $700 per year.
- **Electricity costs:** power arrangements structured so local utility customers do not absorb costs tied to electricity production for its facilities. The scope of that protection depends on the terms negotiated in each jurisdiction.
## The environmental claims, and the gap

Amazon reports a global power usage effectiveness (PUE) of 1.14 for 2025. PUE is an efficiency ratio: lower means less energy wasted on cooling and overhead. By Amazon's figures the industry average is 1.25 and conventional on-premises enterprise data centers average 1.63.

On water, Amazon says its facilities use roughly one-seventh the water withdrawals of an average industry data center. More than 65 water-replenishment projects are expected to return over 8 billion gallons annually to local communities, more than twice what Amazon says its data centers consumed in 2025. It reports having reached 75% of its goal to become water-positive across its data centers by 2030. New AWS sites are expected to meet EPA Tier 4 emissions standards for backup generators, though the article notes Tier 4 requirements vary by engine power and equipment category.

The limitation the article stresses: **Amazon does not separate its conventional cloud facility data from its AI-specific facilities in these figures.** AI data centers pack dense arrays of specialized accelerators that generate substantially more heat and draw significantly more power, so the headline averages may not reflect what a new AI-focused facility demands from a local grid or water supply.

## Separating evidence from implication

**Evidence (what the source says)**

- The October 2 announcement, the end of NDAs, annual published metrics, and Built Together at more than $1 billion.
- Every figure (PUE 1.14, 300,000 students, 100,000 workers, 30,000 homes, 20% to 40%) is an Amazon report or projection.
- The issues behind the moratoriums — siting, noise, land use and grid capacity — remain unresolved.
**Implications (this article's reading)**

- Ending NDAs is a structural change, but how much it opens depends on whether local governments release the records.
- Unless the annual disclosures break out AI facilities, the load of a new facility — what residents most want to know — stays invisible.
- As the source concludes, credibility will come from measurable annual disclosures, accessible project information and independent verification of energy and water performance, particularly for AI facilities.
## What a reader should check

Three checks follow. Do the annual metrics report AI facilities separately? Is the ratepayer protection written into each jurisdiction's terms? Are the figures verified by a third party? For now all three remain at the announcement stage; the track record is still to be built.
