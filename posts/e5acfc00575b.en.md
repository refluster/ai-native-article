## Executive Summary

The practical guide published on X by “Sho | AI × Indie Development × Investing, time-saving working dad” (@sho_ai_magic) on June 20, 2026—titled “SLIDE.md Practical Guide | A Complete Guide from Installation to Slide Generation”—lays out a four-step process, from installation to slide completion, for solving the problem of slide designs generated with Claude Design “varying wildly like a gacha every time,” using a slide-specific design system called SLIDE.md. The central claim is straightforward: design reproducibility does not reside in prompt skill at generation time, but in pre-externalized design documents (SLIDE.md / SLIDE-PATTERN / SLIDE-DECK.md).

## The Problem It Aims to Solve: Variation in Outputs

The problem statement at the start of the guide goes beyond tactics and speaks directly to the evaluation axis itself. The author writes, “We’ve entered an era where high-quality slides can be created at blazing speed with Claude Design. However, the biggest problem was that the design varied wildly like a gacha every time.”

The issue here is not speed, but consistency. Generation speed is treated as a problem already solved; the unresolved challenge is explicitly identified as controlling “color, font, spacing, and composition.” SLIDE.md is defined as a design system created for the purpose of “completely controlling” these four elements while generating presentation materials with a unified look and feel tailored to one’s own preferences in a short amount of time.

The implication is that, as AI generation workflows mature, the evaluation axis is shifting from “Can it make something?” to “Can it keep making things to the same standard?” Once the output quality of generation tools passes a certain threshold, the next bottleneck is no longer the quality of individual outputs, but the absence of a unified standard that runs across multiple outputs. That structural assumption underpins this guide.

## The Four-Step Procedure

The guide breaks slide generation down into four steps. The author notes, “Step 1 is only needed the first time; from the second time onward, you can start from Step 3,” making the cost of repeated use explicit (and if you are only using samples, Step 2 is unnecessary).

- **Step 1: Install** — The two prerequisites are “Claude Code (paid plan or API access)” and “access to Claude Design (included in Claude’s paid plan).” Installation is done simply by talking to Claude Code in your working folder, which automatically sets up three skills (slide-md-creator, slide-pattern-creator, slide-deck-builder), four sample design systems (SLIDE.md), and 99 slide patterns (SLIDE-PATTERN). If it fails, there is also a manual route: download the ZIP from GitHub, copy the three folders under `skills/` into `~/.claude/skills/`, and copy `docs/SLIDE-md/` and `docs/SLIDE-PATTERN/` into your working folder.
- **Step 2: Prepare a design system** — Unnecessary if you are using a sample as-is. Open the `sample.html` in each sample folder in your browser and check whether it fits your needs. If you are creating an original system, you can attach images of existing slides, PowerPoint files, or reference site URLs to the `slide-md-creator` skill, which then runs an interactive flow: read the design elements → ask only 3–5 clarifying questions where something is unclear → generate SLIDE.md and sample.html. The author adds this note: “sample.html is a simple sample generated by Claude Code for confirmation. When you actually generate slides with Claude Design, the final result will be higher quality.”
- **Step 3: Generate SLIDE-DECK.md** — The author explicitly calls this “the most important step.” The `slide-deck-builder` skill proceeds in six stages: (1) briefing interview (4 questions), (2) receive the presentation content (paste text or attach a `.md` / `.pdf`), (3) select an SLIDE.md, (4) review the slide structure proposal (where instructions such as “swap the order of slides 5 and 6” are possible), (5) review the pattern assignments for each slide (for example, “Change Slide 4 to image-left-text-right”), and (6) generate SLIDE-DECK.md. The output file, `SLIDE-DECK-{name}.md`, consolidates the design system, the selected patterns, and the content template for each slide into a single file.
- **Step 4: Generate slides with Claude Design** — Open claude.ai/design, select Slides, upload the SLIDE-DECK.md from Step 3, and send it. Claude Design then reads the design system and pattern definitions and automatically generates the slides.
## Design Implication: Externalize Control Before Generation

The design decision that emerges from the structure of the procedure is this: rather than leaving design consistency to the conversation at generation time, fix it in a design document before generation. Step 3 is described as “the most important,” and the workflow—consolidating content, design system, and pattern assignments into a single file (SLIDE-DECK.md) before handing it off to Step 4—reflects the idea of constraining the non-determinism of generation through explicit upstream specifications.

The discussion of evaluation criteria is also concrete. On post-generation fine-tuning, the author mentions the toolbar options “Mark up” and “Edit,” but candidly writes, “At this stage, the feel of these operations is not very good, so in practice it’s best to keep changes minor,” adding that they are developing a separate tool to address the problem. The same differentiation appears in the discussion of download formats: PDF is “the most reliable for presenting and sharing” and “outputs exactly as designed”; HTML works offline; and PowerPoint is described as “so-so, because the design may break at this stage.” The conclusion is blunt: “If you’re unsure, choosing PDF won’t steer you wrong.”

The implication is that consistency in this workflow is guaranteed not by the performance of a single tool, but by investment in an intermediate artifact: the design document. The FAQ adds that you can also hand SLIDE-DECK.md to NotebookLM or ChatGPT, though “Claude Design is the most specialized for visual design and delivers the highest quality”; it also notes that patterns beyond the 99 included can be extracted and added from images of your own slides using `slide-pattern-creator`. Together, this shows a structure in which the design document functions as a tool-agnostic asset. Once you are familiar with the process, the author concludes, it effectively collapses into two steps: “Talk to slide-deck-builder → hand it to Claude Design.”
