## Executive Summary

On Thursday, November 5, 2026, just after Democrats won control of the US Congress in the midterms, the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52% to 24,315.00, its steepest one-day drop in 19 months. It closed more than 10% below its October 6 record, confirming a technical correction. According to Reuters, the trigger was fear of tougher regulation of AI data-center construction, which shows the market had priced the AI growth story as dependent on the speed of infrastructure buildout.

## What happened: the index and stock-level numbers

The facts below come from the Reuters report by Noel Randewich and Utkarsh Hathi.

- The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52% to 24,315.00, "its deepest one-day drop in 19 months," the largest since President Donald Trump's "Liberation Day" tariff announcement in April 2025.
- The S&P 500 fell 4.36% to 7,353.73. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 3.44% to 51,523.06.
- The selloff wiped out $3 trillion in market value from S&P 500 companies.
- Even after the drop, the S&P 500 remains up 7.4% in 2026 and the Nasdaq is up 4.6%.
- Volume on US exchanges was very heavy: 26.9 billion shares, against a 20-session average of 17.7 billion.
By name, the damage concentrated in chips and in the companies that supply AI data centers with components and power:

- Nvidia dropped over 9%; the Philadelphia SE Semiconductor index plunged over 12%.
- Data-center companies: IREN and Applied Digital fell more than 14%, and CoreWeave dropped almost 10%.
- AI-exposed utilities and equipment suppliers: GE Vernova fell 7.5% and Constellation Energy 2.5%.
- Large-cap tech: Meta slid 4%; Amazon and Alphabet each dropped 2.2%.
- Qualcomm fell 9.5%, but for a separate reason: it fell short of Wall Street estimates for fourth-quarter smartphone chip sales.
The day before, the S&P 500 had logged its biggest one-day percentage gain in three months as political uncertainty eased and risk appetite rose, so Thursday's fall partly reversed a sharp rally.

## The stated reason: election results and data-center opposition

Reuters ties the chipmakers' decline to fears of tougher AI regulation after Democrats won control of Congress. The article makes two points:

- Tuesday's Democratic wins included states where opposition to new data centers has mounted.
- Tighter regulation could therefore slow the AI infrastructure buildout that has helped lift stocks to record highs this year.
Melissa Brown, global head of investment decision research at SimCorp, said investors appear to be recognizing that some newly elected members of Congress and governors are looking to slow the pace of AI infrastructure development. "While it seems unlikely that they will be able to stop it altogether," she said, "any slowdown would raise concerns because the AI growth story has largely depended on the rapid buildout of supporting infrastructure." Jed Ellerbroek, a portfolio manager at Argent Capital Management, called it "the capitulation phase for the data center capex beneficiary stocks, especially the semiconductors," but added: "I think the magnitude of the moves is outsized."

## Separating evidence from conclusion

**What the article establishes (evidence)**

- The index declines, volume and stock moves are the figures above.
- The explanation, AI-regulation fear, rests on comments from two market participants and the reporters' own framing. The article says nothing concrete about what regulation will actually look like or how fast it would move.
- The article also records a different analyst view: a divided government could mean fewer sharp legislative changes and could make it harder to agree on fiscal spending.
**What we can draw from it (conclusion)**

- This was a reaction to regulation being possible, not to regulation being enacted. Until actual policy takes shape, the size of the move is a signal, but its directional reliability is low.
- AI-infrastructure stocks fell together across three layers: semiconductors, data-center operators, and power and equipment suppliers. The market treats them as one dependency chain: if construction proceeds, all benefit; if it stalls, all hurt.
- It is worth recording that the market itself voiced the premise that the AI growth story depends on the speed of infrastructure construction. Business plans and AI investment discussions need to account for that premise being exposed to external factors such as political and local permitting decisions.
## Monetary policy as a second pressure

The same article reports a separate rate story. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack and Richmond Fed chief Thomas Barkin hinted on Thursday at the possibility of future rate hikes. Traders currently expect the Fed to hike rates next month, following the September rate hike. October nonfarm payrolls data is due Friday, and focus shifts there.

Regulation fear and rate-hike expectations landed on the same day, and the article does not separate their contributions. How much of the fall came from regulation concerns versus the rate outlook or a reversal of Wednesday's surge cannot be determined from this article alone.

## What this means for readers

- This piece summarizes a single article; it does not cover specific regulatory measures or the effect on any company's capital-expenditure plans.
- What can be confirmed is limited to this: on November 5, 2026 the Nasdaq had its sharpest fall in 19 months, and Reuters reported AI-regulation fear as the main cause.
- Concrete data-center policy from the new Congress and state governments, plus Friday's jobs data and the monetary-policy moves that follow, will help distinguish whether this drop is transient or structural.
