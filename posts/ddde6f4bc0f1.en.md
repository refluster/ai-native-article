## Executive Summary

Financial commentary outlet The Kobeissi Letter said on July 9, 2026, that the AI “arms race” is driving record corporate bond issuance by Big Tech. Amazon, Alphabet, Nvidia, Meta, Oracle, and SpaceX have issued a combined $182 billion in investment-grade bonds so far in 2026, a surge of about 1,300% from roughly $13 billion in the same period of 2025. These six companies alone account for nearly 15% of year-to-date U.S. corporate bond issuance and more than 50% of this year’s increase in issuance, suggesting that AI’s capital demands are reshaping the corporate bond market itself.

## Record-Breaking Issuance: $182 Billion Across Six Companies, Up 1,300% Year on Year

The central fact highlighted by The Kobeissi Letter is that both the absolute scale of issuance and the growth rate are without recent precedent.

- The six companies in focus are Amazon ($AMZN), Alphabet ($GOOGL), Nvidia ($NVDA), Meta ($META), Oracle ($ORCL), and SpaceX ($SPCX).
- Together, these six companies have issued a total of $182 billion in investment-grade bonds year to date in 2026. That is described as a record level.
- In the same period in 2025, issuance totaled roughly $13 billion (~$13 billion), meaning 2026’s $182 billion represents a +1,300% increase year on year.
The fact that the growth rate has reached four digits is difficult to explain as a normal one-year economic cycle. The jump from $13 billion to $182 billion means that all six companies have dramatically raised the scale of their fundraising at the same time. The Kobeissi Letter summarized the backdrop to this move as: “The AI arms race is driving record Big Tech borrowing.” The implication is that retained earnings alone are no longer enough to fund capital spending, and that these companies have clearly shifted toward external financing in the bond market.

## An Unusual Degree of Concentration in the Bond Market: Nearly 15% of Issuance, More Than 50% of the Increase

The scale becomes even clearer when these six companies are placed in the context of the broader U.S. corporate bond market. The Kobeissi Letter presented two key ratios.

- These six companies account for nearly 15% of total year-to-date U.S. corporate bond issuance.
- Moreover, when looking only at this year’s growth in corporate bond issuance, the six companies account for over 50%.
These two figures describe different things. The first is a share of the market’s overall level, while the second is a share of the incremental increase. The fact that six companies account for more than half of the increase means that the expansion of the U.S. corporate bond market in 2026 is being driven by the fundraising of a very small number of technology companies. This is not a case of the market growing modestly and broadly across issuers. Instead, bond issuance by a specific group of players has become the main engine of market growth, revealing a highly concentrated structure.

## Mega-Deals Have Changed Shape: Seven Deals of More Than $25 Billion in a Single Period

This concentration is visible not only in the list of issuers but also in the growing size of individual transactions.

- During this period, a record 7 bond deals of $25 billion or more were completed.
- That total of 7 is comparable to the total number of such large deals seen between 2019 and 2025.
- Of those 7 deals, 6 were by the six companies mentioned above, with the remaining one by Salesforce ($CRM).
In other words, a volume of mega-deals equivalent to roughly seven years’ worth was executed in a limited stretch of 2026. Bond issuance of more than $25 billion is normally rare, so the fact that 7 such deals occurred in quick succession is itself an outlier. What is more, the vast majority of them—6 out of 7—came from the same six companies, while outside that group only a single deal, from Salesforce, appeared. This again shows just how heavily large-scale fundraising has tilted toward a small number of AI-linked companies.

## AI’s Capital Demands Are Rewriting the Capital Markets

From these facts, The Kobeissi Letter draws a straightforward conclusion: “AI capital needs are reshaping the corporate bond market.”

- Every one of the indicators points in the same direction: the record total issuance ($182 billion), the four-digit growth rate (+1,300%), the high share of the overall market (nearly 15% / more than 50% of the increase), and the concentration of mega-deals (7 transactions of more than $25 billion).
- Together, they indicate that funding demand for AI infrastructure investment is no longer just a matter of individual corporate balance sheets. It has become a macro factor influencing the size, composition, and growth drivers of the U.S. corporate bond market.
This article is based on a post published by The Kobeissi Letter on July 9, 2026, and the figures, company names, and ratios above are all explicitly stated in that post. The key interpretive point beyond those raw facts is that the spending race around AI is no longer affecting only stock market valuations or capital expenditure statistics. It is now reaching into the structure of the bond market itself as a core financing infrastructure. When such a small number of issuers accounts for this much of the market, it suggests that the capital plans of those companies could influence funding conditions for other issuers as well through overall market supply and demand.
