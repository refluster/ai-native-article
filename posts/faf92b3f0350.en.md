## Executive Summary

A confidential Meta initiative code-named "Project OT," driven by CEO Mark Zuckerberg, aimed to have AI take over the work of thousands of employees and shrink many internal teams by as much as 60%. Internal backlash after the plan leaked, AI productivity that fell short of expectations, and a surge in security and reliability incidents combined to effectively derail the effort. Employee satisfaction scores plunged from 74% to 55%, and Zuckerberg himself told staff in a July internal meeting that the restructuring "hasn't been going well."

## Origins: chasing an "AI-native" company

According to a Reuters investigation summarized by GIGAZINE, Project OT traces back to 2025, when Meta Chief Data Officer Alex Schultz and Head of Product Naomi Gleight traveled to Asia and were struck by how AI startups were building their entire organizations around AI. On their direction, Meta commissioned its own research into the organizational structures of these "AI-native" companies and launched a pilot project to assess the approach's potential.

In January 2026, Zuckerberg and a group of close associates held a retreat at a villa in Hawaii to refine Project OT, an effort to make Meta more AI-native. Internal documents obtained by Reuters show the plan considered scenarios in which AI would take over the work of thousands of employees, with the potential to shrink the size of many internal teams by up to 60%.

## A two-phase restructuring plan

The organizational overhaul was designed to unfold in two phases: a first round of layoffs scheduled for May 2026, followed by a larger second round in November. The plan went beyond straightforward dismissals to include closing vacant positions and reassigning staff to new departments; one HR executive predicted the eventual scale of the cuts would exceed 25%.

Alongside Project OT, Meta was also pushing AI deeper into product development. In July 2025, a pilot led by Vice President of Product Management Yemei Archiborn replaced the traditional 10-to-20-person product team with a "small technology pod" of just two to three engineers and one designer, with the goal of having every member use AI tools to build product prototypes in a short period of time.

An internal post from Archiborn's team, titled the "AI-Native Playbook," stated that traditional roles such as product designer and engineer would be abolished within the pods. Members would instead be given a new title, "Builder," overseen by a single managerial role called "Pod Leader."

## Confusion sown by the pod system

When Project OT launched in earnest in early 2026, at least 11 departments — including engineering and research teams — had adopted the pod structure by June. But reporting indicates pod leaders were not granted formal management authority such as the ability to conduct performance evaluations, and had not received adequate manager training, sowing confusion among employees.

In March, before many vice-president-level executives had even been briefed on Project OT, Reuters reported that Meta was planning to lay off up to 20% of its workforce. The report caused considerable anxiety among rank-and-file employees, and executives — unprepared for the backlash — had little choice but to decline to discuss it with staff.

In April, Reuters reported further detail, putting the number at roughly 8,000 people, or 10% of the total workforce. At this point Zuckerberg formally announced the layoff plan, telling employees the cuts were necessary because of the company's heavy capital investment.

While proceeding with the May layoffs, Meta reassigned some engineers to a newly created Applied AI Engineering department, where they were tasked with creating puzzles to be used as training data to improve the coding capabilities of AI models. In late April, Meta also began collecting employees' mouse movements and keystrokes to use as AI training data.

## Employee backlash and doubts about AI's payoff

The employee-data collection effort fueled widespread suspicion among staff that they were being used to train the very AI meant to replace them. Combined with frustration over the lack of transparency around the layoff plan, this triggered a flood of angry and self-deprecating posts on Meta's internal communication tool, Workplace. Employee morale declined as a result, and Meta's semi-annual employee satisfaction survey showed the company's score plunging from 74% to 55%.

Internal data also indicated that the AI at the core of Project OT was not performing as hoped. Internal documents noted that while the volume of code produced by employees using generative AI increased significantly, the actual gain in productivity was questionable. One internal post found that code changes to internal software platforms and infrastructure rose 220% year over year, while changes that led to new features or upgrades increased by only 36%.

## A surge in security and reliability incidents

In March, Meta's infrastructure team issued a "reliability warning" tied to the rise in AI-generated coding. In April, the company reported "large-scale, disruptive activity by uncontrolled AI agents that would be unlikely to be carried out by humans." As a result of these issues, critical technical and security incidents — including service disruptions and potential data breaches — increased 40% year over year, and the time staff spent resolving problems rose 70%.

In June, hackers tricked Meta AI's support chatbot and stole the Instagram accounts of several celebrities. The compromised accounts reportedly included the White House account of former U.S. President Barack Obama and the account of a Space Force chief sergeant.

## Zuckerberg's reckoning — and the embers left behind

Amid mounting internal pressure, Zuckerberg and his staff consulted with aides just hours before the May layoffs were implemented and decided to cancel the second round originally planned for November. The May layoffs went ahead, but remaining employees were told there were "no further company-wide layoffs planned for this year."

Management also moved to repair morale: the mouse- and keystroke-tracking program for AI training was temporarily suspended, and some employees who had been reassigned to the newly formed Applied AI Engineering department were returned to their original teams. Workplace saw a series of empathetic posts from management, along with promises to improve the quality of snacks in office kitchenettes and to increase spending on business trips and social events.

Speaking at an internal meeting in July, Zuckerberg acknowledged that the large-scale organizational restructuring "hasn't been going well," saying, "At least the progress in AI agent development over the past four months has not accelerated as much as we had hoped."

Still, when Zuckerberg discusses layoffs with employees, he tends to use qualifiers like "company-wide" and "this year" — leaving some staff concerned that team-by-team layoffs could still occur, or that another large-scale round could arrive in 2027.
