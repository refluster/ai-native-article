TSMC's board approved buying up to USD 1 billion of equity in its own customers, Anthropic and OpenAI. Anthropic filed for an IPO with first-half revenue above USD 16 billion and a net loss of USD 80.7 billion. And on 5 November, right after the US midterm elections, the Nasdaq fell 5.52%, with reports pointing to fears of tighter regulation of AI data-center construction. A chipmaker, a model developer and an equity market look like separate stories, yet one principle explains all three: the AI supply chain is starting to bind itself together not with payment for products but with a shared collateral, the expectation of future demand.

## Supply relationships become equity: TSMC's stake

TSMC's board approved up to USD 500 million for Anthropic shares at the IPO price and up to USD 500 million for OpenAI preferred stock, up to USD 1 billion in total. The article "TSMC Board Approves Up to USD 1 Billion for Anthropic and OpenAI: The Equitization of Supply Relationships Ahead of Listing" points to a structural change: a contract manufacturer becomes a shareholder of its customer.

Traditionally a manufacturer's income was limited to payment for the products it made. With a stake, TSMC gains a second revenue path, the rise in the developer's valuation. The same article names the other side: because those valuations depend on the outlook for AI demand, TSMC also takes on concentration risk. Both effects come from one cause. If the customer grows, manufacturing revenue and the gain on the stake both rise; if the demand outlook breaks, both suffer together. TSMC has layered a second relationship, betting on the same variable, on top of its trading relationship.

## Most of the loss is an accounting of expectations: Anthropic's filing

According to the article "Anthropic Files for One of History's Largest IPOs: First-Half Revenue Above USD 16 Billion, Net Loss of USD 80.7 Billion", Anthropic submitted its IPO documents on 26 October. The 335-page filing reports first-half 2026 revenue above USD 16 billion, 14 times the same period a year earlier, and a net loss of USD 80.7 billion, of which USD 72.4 billion is a one-time accounting adjustment tied to financing agreements.

Subtracting gives USD 8.3 billion of loss excluding the adjustment (80.7 minus 72.4; this is my own arithmetic). Against 14-fold revenue growth, most of the loss is not an operating deficit but the effect that financing terms had on the accounts. A large part of this company's statements is therefore a record of commitments about future demand and valuation. Amount raised, valuation and offer price are not yet set; the company plans to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under "ANT". The price will be set by the market's expectations.

## A market that moves on one expectation: the Nasdaq on 5 November

According to the article "The Day the Election Lit the Fuse on Fears of an AI-Infrastructure Slowdown: Nasdaq's Biggest Drop in 19 Months", on 5 November 2026, right after Democrats took control of Congress in the US midterms, the Nasdaq Composite closed down 5.52% at 24,315.00, its largest drop in 19 months. It fell more than 10% from its recent high, entering a correction. Reuters attributed the selling to concern over tighter regulation of AI data-center construction.

What was sold was not a particular product or company. It was the expectation that AI data-center construction will keep expanding. A political event moved prices in the same direction across a wide range, from chip manufacturing to developer valuations. That a single expectation, not any company's earnings, moved prices shows how tightly this market is coupled.

## The common principle: expectations have become the collateral

Laying the three facts side by side supports the following inference. These facts suggest that what determines value in the AI supply chain is shifting from the price of products to expectations of future demand.

First, in TSMC's stake, a trading relationship was converted into equity, a claim on that expectation. Second, in Anthropic's filing, most of the loss was an accounting adjustment on financing agreements, a record of commitments built on expectations. Third, in the Nasdaq's fall, a wobble in a single expectation moved prices across the market. At different layers, supply, financing and pricing, the three events are tied to one variable: the outlook for demand, including AI data-center construction.

Note also the distance between the three articles. The TSMC article describes the supply side, the Anthropic article the demand side, and the Nasdaq article the market that prices both. Normally these would be separate objects of analysis, tracked by separate indicators. Here the supply side's investment amount, the demand side's loss breakdown and the market's percentage fall all refer to the same single outlook. When indicators are separate but their reference is one, they should be treated as one linkage.

We call this structure "a mutual shareholding collateralized by demand expectations". In calm periods it is a strength: TSMC earns twice from a customer's growth, from manufacturing revenue and from the gain on its stake. But because the collateral is single, when the expectation breaks, manufacturing, financing and share prices are hit together rather than spread apart. This is not a failure of diversification; it is a design that presumes none.

The principle can be refuted. If the supply chain were really diversified, then when the demand outlook worsens, TSMC's manufacturing revenue, the value of its stakes and the developer's share price should move differently. If all three move the same way on the same day, the principle is supported. The 5 November drop is one example of the latter, but it is a single day's observation, not conclusive evidence.

## Outlook and implications

Three predictions follow. Each is an inference from the facts in the three articles, and the probabilities are my own estimates.

First, the day Anthropic's offer price is set will be read as a thermometer of expectations. With amount raised, valuation and price still open, the market conditions on the pricing day will be priced straight in. If the IPO proceeds while the Nasdaq is in a correction, the price is likely to depend more on market mood than on the growth rate in the filing (moderate confidence).

Second, similar stakes will increase. TSMC's USD 1 billion shows that becoming a customer's shareholder is an available option. As long as a second revenue path from customers' rising valuations stays attractive, other suppliers may follow (moderate confidence). But if regulatory fears materialize, the following will stall and the value of the stakes already held will fall through the same variable.

Third, regulatory and political moves will reach prices faster than technical ones. What 5 November showed is that one report of concern over data-center regulation moved an index by 5.52% in a day. Since the outlook for demand is the shared collateral, an announcement of policy that affects it can cause larger price swings than any single company's earnings (high confidence).

The practical takeaway for readers: when evaluating an AI-related company or asset, check not only the strength of its business but also how many times over it is betting on one expectation. If manufacturing revenue, the value of a stake and the share price all depend on the same outlook, apparent diversification is not real diversification. Write down the one expectation your own judgment depends on, and check in advance what would fall together if it broke.
