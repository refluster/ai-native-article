## Executive Summary

In his long-form X post published on July 15, 2026, “The Best Model Routing is Task Specific,” LlamaIndex co-founder Jerry Liu argued that the optimal answer to model routing is not a general-purpose monolith, but a task-specific approach. OpenRouter’s “Fusion” and Cognition’s “Devin Fusion,” both introduced over roughly the past 1.5 months, put that claim into concrete form by adopting designs that bundle or switch among multiple models depending on the task rather than relying on a single frontier model. This article organizes how those two implementations embody “task-specific routing,” based on their published specifications and benchmark figures.

## Why “Model Routing” Is the Key Battleground Right Now

Jerry Liu’s article opens with the line, “Model routing is so hot right now.” The backdrop is that, rather than using a single best-in-class model all the time, the idea of selecting among multiple models depending on the input or task has rapidly been productized over the past 1.5 months.

The debate is shifting from “which model should you choose?” to “how should you orchestrate them, and when should you switch?” The article’s title makes the core argument clear: the best routing is not a universal, one-size-fits-all strategy, but a design specialized for the task you are trying to solve. As evidence, Jerry Liu points to two contrasting implementations: OpenRouter’s Fusion, which fans a prompt out across a panel of models and synthesizes them into a single answer, and Cognition’s design, which is optimized for the specific domain of coding.

- The article was published in X’s long-form article format, and the post recorded 43.4K views (as of July 15, 2026).
- The two implementations presented both assume a “multi-model” approach, but their philosophies of orchestration are nearly opposite.
## OpenRouter Fusion: “Synthesizing” a Panel of Frontier Models

Fusion, published by OpenRouter, is a compound model that sends a single prompt in parallel to a panel of multiple frontier models, collects each model’s independent answer, and then uses a dedicated “judge” model to synthesize those responses into one structured final answer. It has exactly the structure Jerry Liu summarized in his article as a “compound model that fans your prompt out to a panel of frontier models and synthesizes one answer.”

Its published specifications and benchmarks are as follows.

- Fusion first appeared as a public experiment on March 31, 2026, and reached production integration in the OpenRouter API in mid-June.
- Model slugs use the format `<task>-<tier>`. `task` indicates the task the panel is optimized for, while `tier` refers to the quality/cost/speed tradeoff (`high` is the strongest model group, `budget` is a cheaper panel while retaining the same frontier-class judge, and `fast` is a low-latency panel in which each model’s time to first token is aligned).
- In benchmarks, a panel combining Fable 5 and GPT-5.5 recorded 69.0%, outperforming every individual model, including Fable 5 alone at 65.3%.
- A low-cost configuration (Gemini 3 Flash, Kimi K2.6, DeepSeek V4 Pro) outperformed GPT-5.5 and Opus 4.8, and came within 1% of Fable 5’s standalone score while costing half as much.
- The default “Quality” preset uses Claude Fable 5 and GPT-5.5 in the panel, with Claude Opus 4.8 serving as the synthesizing judge.
What this makes clear is that Fusion builds “task” directly into the slug naming convention itself as a first-class axis. The panel composition and tiering are designed not as a single general-purpose setting, but on the assumption that you will choose them according to the task you want to optimize.

## Cognition Devin Fusion: Switching “Mid-Session”

The other implementation is Devin Fusion, published by Cognition on June 29. This one is a coding harness that combines one expensive “main agent” with one inexpensive “sidekick,” and claims to deliver frontier-class output with a 35% reduction in cost.

Where Fusion performs parallel synthesis at the prompt level, Devin Fusion’s routing is continuous and happens mid-session.

- In engineering work, task difficulty often only becomes clear near the end. Cognition’s example is a case that looked like a “simple bug” but turned out to be a race condition spanning multiple files; once that complexity is exposed, the work can be handed back to a frontier model. It is not locked into an estimate made upstream.
- Model switching is aligned with “compaction” (conversation summarization) boundaries. Normally, changing models means losing expensive cache state, but at the exact moment when the conversation is being summarized anyway, a cache miss is unavoidable regardless. As a result, the switching cost becomes effectively free.
What Devin Fusion embodies is the idea of designing routing around properties specific to the coding domain: that difficulty is often only revealed after the fact, and that cache boundaries exist. In a general-purpose router, optimizations such as “hand it back midstream” or “switch at the summarization boundary” are much harder to arrive at.

## What “Task-Specific” Means

The two implementations are opposites in how they orchestrate models—“parallel synthesis” versus “sequential switching”—but they arrive at the same conclusion. The direction is to move away from dependence on a single model and design routing around the structure of the task.

- OpenRouter Fusion embeds the task into routing as a selection axis through its `<task>-<tier>` slug design.
- Cognition Devin Fusion dynamically switches models within a session by exploiting coding-specific timing around when difficulty becomes clear and where cache boundaries fall.
Jerry Liu’s claim, “The Best Model Routing is Task Specific,” becomes a concrete design principle when these two examples are placed side by side. The answer is neither a general-purpose “always use the strongest model” nor a general-purpose “always use the cheapest model,” but to build routing around the nature of the task—whether parallel perspectives help, or whether difficulty only becomes clear later. That is the answer the market has produced over the past 1.5 months.
