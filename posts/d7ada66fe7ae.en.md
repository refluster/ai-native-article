## Executive Summary

South Korea has unveiled "AI for All," a plan to give every citizen free access to premium generative AI. Three consortia — including the country's two largest telecom carriers and the company behind the super-app Kakao — will share up to 512 Nvidia B200 chips allocated by the government and begin beta testing in September. Usage will carry no token caps, but at least 80% of queries must run through Korean-built foundation models — making free access the delivery mechanism for an explicit industrial policy of reducing dependence on U.S. and Chinese models.

## The Plan: Free, Uncapped, Beta in September

According to reporting by the Wall Street Journal, relayed by Decrypt, South Korea is moving to treat generative-AI access as something close to a public service — the first attempt by a national government to do so.

- On Friday, August 28, the Ministry of Science and ICT named three consortia to deliver the service, among them the country's two largest telecom operators and the company behind Kakao, the messaging platform that functions as a general-purpose app across Korean life.
- Each consortium will launch a dedicated app while also folding the AI into products people already use.
- The government will divide up to 512 of Nvidia's high-end B200 chips among the three groups and contribute toward running costs. The Ministry has not disclosed the programme's total cost.
- Usage will be uncapped, with no token limits.
- Beta testing starts in September; full rollout is planned later this year.
- The WSJ cites potential use cases including booking doctor's appointments, apartment searches, and tax advice; small businesses get help calculating taxes and checking eligibility for state support; parents receive suggested educational material for their children.
The distinguishing feature versus other national AI strategies: Seoul is handing citizens a finished product, not building infrastructure for others to build on top of.

## The Rule Design: Subsidy Flows Only to Domestic Models

Alongside the free rollout comes an allocation rule that effectively locks out foreign models from the subsidized pool.

- Operators must route at least 50% of all queries through their own certified Korean foundation model, and another 30% through models built by other Korean firms — putting the domestic floor at 80% combined.
- Foreign models are permitted only where the ministry judges them necessary, and that usage draws no subsidy.
- The government has set no explicit target for uptake.
Read together, the policy pairs consumer-facing appeal (free, unlimited) with an industrial-policy lever (an 80% domestic floor) in a single package. Free access isn't the end goal — it's the mechanism for redirecting traffic toward Korea's domestic AI industry.

## Current Usage: Widespread Adoption, but on Foreign Free Tiers

The rule exists because of where usage already sits.

- Roughly two-thirds of South Koreans have tried generative AI, and about 23 million use it regularly.
- Per figures in the ministry's own tender documents, as of April, ChatGPT counted 23.45 million Korean users, against 8.45 million for Gemini and 2.41 million for Claude.
- Only about 1.8 million people in the country pay for anything.
In other words, AI adoption in Korea is already broad — but overwhelmingly riding on foreign services' free tiers. "AI for All" is built to invert that structure.

## How It Compares: Infrastructure Investment vs. Finished-Product Delivery

Set against other regions pursuing "model sovereignty," Korea's approach stands out by what it is not building.

- The European Commission launched InvestAI in February 2025, aiming to mobilise $232 billion (€200 billion) across the bloc, including $23 billion (€20 billion) earmarked for large compute sites meant to reduce reliance on non-EU providers.
- India approved its IndiaAI Mission in March 2024 with roughly $1.25 billion, since putting tens of thousands of subsidised GPUs within reach of startups and researchers while funding 20 homegrown model projects.
- Where the EU and India are primarily building the machinery, Seoul is distributing the finished product directly to consumers.
President Lee Jae-myung's administration has earmarked roughly $7.2 billion (10 trillion won) for AI spending in 2026 — three times what it committed the previous year. Taken together, the domestic-model mandate, the free rollout, and the sharply increased budget read as a government racing to grow both its user base and its domestic industry at the same time.
