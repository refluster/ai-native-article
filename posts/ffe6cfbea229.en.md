## Executive Summary

On Wednesday, November 4, the US Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) released its first framework telling examiners how to evaluate banks' use of AI. The core of it: do not treat AI as a standalone risk category; examine how a bank uses AI in its everyday business and how much damage AI errors could cause. It is not a binding new regulation.

## What was published

The source is an Analytics Insight article dated November 4, 2026. According to it, the OCC released the framework on Wednesday, November 4, to help examiners evaluate how banks are implementing AI in their everyday business.

- Examiners should not treat artificial intelligence as a standalone risk category.
- Banks will not be required to develop separate risk standards for AI applications.
- The article quotes: "Examiners should be focused on how a bank uses AI in its everyday business." In the article the speaker is identified only as "he"; no name is given.
- Examiners must take into account the size of the bank and the complexity of its business.
## Measuring risk: consequences of error, not technology

The framework asks examiners to pay more attention to the damage that AI errors could cause. A model's risk level should not be based primarily on its technical features.

The article's example: a simple AI tool that can carry out financial transactions may pose a bigger risk than an advanced system used only for internal work. What the system can do, and what happens when it is wrong, matters more than how sophisticated it is.

The framework also suggests tighter restrictions on independent AI operations in higher-risk situations. Banks may need stronger human supervision when their systems can take important decisions or carry out financial activities.

## Guidance, not regulation

The OCC said the document is not a mandatory regulation. It sets no new rules that financial institutions must follow; instead it gives examiners direction on how to review the risk management systems banks already have in place. The article reports the regulator as saying: "The OCC expects that bank activities are conducted in a safe and sound manner and consistent with applicable laws and regulations."

The guidance also applies to smaller community banks. During their reviews, examiners must consider each bank's size, business activities and the risks it faces.

As background, the article says financial regulators in several countries are paying closer attention to the growing use of AI in banking, studying possible threats linked to cybersecurity, AI systems that operate independently, and financial stability. Banks are using AI more often for customer services and other daily activities, and regulators want to make sure these tools are used safely and under proper supervision.

## Implications (our reading, not the source's)

- **Fact**: There is no AI-specific risk category and no obligation to build AI-specific standards. Assessment extends the existing risk-management framework, weighted by the consequences of error and by degree of autonomy.
- **Reading**: This design shifts a bank's burden of accountability from "which model do you use" to "what have you delegated to the AI, and where can a human stop it when it is wrong." Per the source's own example, a simple tool that can execute transactions may be weighed more heavily than a sophisticated system confined to internal work. The way agentic authority is granted becomes itself an object of examination.
- **Limits**: The source is a short news item. It does not cover the framework's full text, its specific examination items, or when it applies; those need to be confirmed against the OCC's primary document.
