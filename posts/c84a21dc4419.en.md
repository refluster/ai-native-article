## Executive Summary

In a July 5, 2026 post, The Kobeissi Letter projected that AI capital expenditure (CapEx) by Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Microsoft, and Oracle will reach about 3.2% of US GDP in 2027, surpassing defense spending, which is estimated at about 2.7% that year, for the first time in history. The post says the five companies’ combined AI CapEx will exceed $800 billion in 2026 and expand to a record $1.1 trillion in 2027. The core point is that the scale of capital spending is no longer being discussed as the budget of a single industry, but as a component of the macroeconomy.

## The Numbers Show CapEx Swelling

The figures cited in the post line up AI-related capital expenditure using a single yardstick: share of GDP.

- The five companies in scope are Alphabet ($GOOGL), Amazon ($AMZN), Meta ($META), Microsoft ($MSFT), and Oracle ($ORCL).
- AI CapEx as a share of GDP is expected to rise from 1.5% in 2025 to about 2.5% in 2026 ("this year").
- It is then projected to climb further to about 3.2% in 2027.
- In dollar terms, the five companies’ combined AI CapEx is expected to exceed $800 billion in 2026 and reach a record $1.1 trillion in 2027.
What matters here is not the growth rate alone, but the change in the basis of comparison. Rather than looking at individual companies’ quarterly CapEx guidance, the post reframes this spending from a "corporate investment decision" into a "demand component of the overall economy" by aggregating the five companies and measuring the total as a share of national output (GDP).

## A Baseline Defined by Overtaking Defense Spending

The post’s strongest emphasis is the comparison showing AI CapEx overtaking defense spending.

- US defense spending is estimated at about 2.7% of GDP "next year" (2027).
- As of 2026, AI CapEx (about 2.5%) is approaching defense spending (about 2.7%).
- If AI CapEx reaches about 3.2% in 2027, annual CapEx will exceed defense spending "for the first time in history."
Defense spending has long served as a benchmark: a large and stable line item in the US federal budget over an extended period. By presenting private-sector capital expenditure by five companies as surpassing that benchmark, the post gives readers a comparison that makes the order of magnitude immediately legible. The author of the post itself described the figures as "Truly mind-blowing numbers."

## What the Post Is Claiming — and What It Is Not

As a matter of fact, what the post presents is a forecast for the five companies’ AI CapEx as a share of GDP and the relative relationship in which it exceeds defense spending. It is useful to separate the implications that can be drawn from that from the areas the post does not address.

- Implication: AI-related capital expenditure is no longer merely an IT budget item for individual companies; it is approaching a scale that can be discussed on the same footing as GDP and defense spending. Investment in data centers and computing infrastructure is beginning to be treated as a variable that can shape macroeconomic demand.
- What the post does not address: it does not say whose estimates these forecasts are based on, whether return on investment (ROI) or demand supports the projections, or what risks might arise if the share of GDP continues to climb. What is presented is only a forecast on the spending side; sustainability and outcomes are outside its scope.
- Accordingly, this article likewise does not evaluate the source or validity of the figures; it simply records the forecast values and comparison benchmark presented in the post as they are.
The post’s central point can be reduced to a single claim: AI CapEx will "exceed defense spending." This should be read not only as a statement about the absolute size of the money involved, but as an indicator that the benchmark for comparison has shifted to items on the scale of the national budget.
