## Executive Summary

On September 21, 2026, Texas Governor Greg Abbott directed the Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) to stop issuing any permits sought by data centers until the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) completes its audit. The move responds to concern that rapid data center growth is straining the state's electric grid and water resources, and it requires TCEQ to report its compliance to the Governor's Office by October 19. Abbott also said he will work with the Legislature next session to eliminate financial incentives for data centers.

## The Directive: No Permits Until the Audits Are In

Abbott directed TCEQ that no state agency shall move forward with regulatory approvals related to data center development until the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT), ERCOT, and the Texas Water Development Board (TWDB) have the information they need to make informed decisions. The press release states: "Because the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUCT), ERCOT, and the Texas Water Development Board (TWDB) require that information to make informed decisions, no state agency shall move forward with regulatory approvals related to data center development until it is acquired."

The governor's own language was blunt: "Simply put, Texans must come first," said Governor Abbott. "Data centers must pay their own way, protect our grid and water, and complete the ERCOT and TWDB audits. Until they do, TCEQ will issue no permits sought by data center projects."

## Grid Strain: An ERCOT Interconnection Audit

Abbott directed the PUCT and ERCOT to audit all data centers currently undergoing ERCOT's interconnection process, so that their development does not compromise the reliability of the Texas electric grid or put Texans' natural resources at risk. The release lays out concrete conditions: data center projects must cover all electrical infrastructure costs, must result in lower residential electrical bills, and must complete the ERCOT audit.

## Water Resources: A September 14 Directive to TWDB

This is not a standalone move. On September 14, Abbott had already directed TWDB to compel data centers to comply with water-use reporting requirements, to impose legal consequences for past and future failures to comply with state law, and to partner with ERCOT on the audit to gather additional water-use information. Together, PUCT, ERCOT, and TWDB will determine the impact of data centers on grid reliability and security and on the water resources needed by Texas communities. The release specifies that data centers "must not use water needed by local communities, must report electricity and water usage, and must abide by setback requirements that will protect the communities in which they operate."

## What Comes Next: Legislative Rollback of Incentives

TCEQ must provide an update on its compliance with the directive to the Office of the Governor by Monday, October 19, 2026. Abbott also said that next session he will work with the Legislature to eliminate financial incentives for data centers — a step beyond a temporary permitting pause, toward revisiting the economic terms under which Texas has courted data center investment in the first place. Texas has been one of the most aggressive states in attracting data center projects; this directive signals that grid headroom and water availability are now acting as a political brake on that growth.
