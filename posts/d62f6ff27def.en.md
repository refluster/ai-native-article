In June 2026, three seemingly unrelated developments surfaced at almost the same time. Satya Nadella argued that companies should “build a frontier ecosystem, not a frontier model”; Anthropic opened its cutting-edge Mythos class to the public and cut the price to less than half; and Mercari assigned CHRO, CAIO, and CTO responsibilities to a single person. The parties involved seem completely different: the one that owns the frontier, the one that sells the frontier, and the one that binds an organization together. But in fact, all three can be explained by the same principle. The center of gravity for value, governance, and safety is shifting from “the strongest standalone unit” to the layer that combines capabilities and allocates them.

## The advantage of owning “the strongest standalone unit” is diminishing

The first thing to observe is that the relative value of owning a frontier “standalone unit” is being eroded from two directions at once.

The core of Nadella’s argument, as conveyed in “Nadella’s theory of ‘human capital and token capital,’” is his diagnosis that a future in which value is concentrated in a small number of frontier models is not politically or economically sustainable. In his words, “A frontier without an ecosystem is not stable.” In other words, no matter how powerful a model may be, it cannot by itself serve as the foundation of durable advantage. What gives this diagnosis real weight is that it is being stated by the very person who ought to benefit from owning the frontier—Microsoft’s Chairman and CEO.

The same pressure is at work on the other side of supply and demand. As reported in “Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5,” Anthropic has taken the general-purpose capabilities of the Mythos class, previously limited to Preview, and released them publicly as Claude Fable 5, pricing them at $10 per 1 million input tokens and $50 for output—less than half the price of Mythos Preview. Frontier capability is rapidly changing shape, from something to be enclosed and restricted into a commoditized good distributed widely at low cost.

When the suppliers themselves say that “standalone units are unstable,” and frontier pricing is cut in half within six months, the ability to profit simply by “owning the strongest model” starts to disappear. Scarcity is fleeing the standalone unit.

## Value now resides in the dynamics of integration and allocation

So where has it gone? Nadella’s answer is clear. He argues that value resides in a **“learning loop”** that compounds and amplifies human capital—people’s knowledge, judgment, and pattern recognition—and token capital, the AI capability owned by the company. His claim is that the new IP is neither the model nor the human being, but the loop itself that keeps the two meshed together and in motion. The crucial point is that the unit of value has shifted here from a “static asset” to the “dynamics of integration.”

What this abstraction looks like when implemented literally in an org chart is “Mercari appoints Toshiya Kimura as ‘CHRO, CAIO, and CTO Japan Business.’” Mercari has bundled into one person three governance nodes that were previously split across separate lines: CHRO (human capability = human capital), CAIO (AI capability = token capital), and CTO (the technical foundation that makes it all run). That is effectively a translation of Nadella’s learning loop into role design. Rather than optimizing human and AI capabilities separately, Mercari is combining and allocating them from a single decision point. The dissolution of role boundaries appears here as the result of shifting control toward the integration layer.

Nadella expressed it as a concept, and Mercari implemented it through personnel design. The direction is the same. The real battleground is moving away from “what you own” and toward “how you mesh human and AI capabilities, and where you allocate them.”

## Even governance and safety are being implemented in the “router layer”

This shift extends not only to value, but also to where governance and safety reside. That is the third line that ties the picture together.

The design core of “Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5” is that it does not **refuse** queries in potentially dangerous domains; instead, a classifier automatically falls back to Claude Opus 4.8. Safety is no longer a static boundary drawn inside a standalone model—“beyond this point, it will not answer.” It is implemented instead as dynamic **routing**: reading the context and deciding “which model should handle this query.” In more than 95% of all Fable sessions, no fallback occurs and the query is processed normally; only dangerous domains are redirected to a different capability. On top of that, Mythos 5, with some safety mechanisms removed, is allocated only in the limited contexts of cyber defenders and critical infrastructure operators.

In other words, the control point is not the model itself but the router layer that determines “to whom, in what context, and with which capability” something is delivered. Structurally, this is isomorphic to Nadella’s integration loop and Mercari’s integrated governance node. Even safety—the least negotiable requirement of all—is being guaranteed not inside the standalone unit, but at the layer that allocates capability.

## The shared principle: the locus is shifting from the standalone unit to the integration and allocation layer

Set these three facts side by side, and a single outline comes into view. Nadella says value has moved; Mercari says governance has moved; Anthropic says safety has moved—from “the strongest standalone unit” to the layer that combines capabilities and allocates them according to context. Taken together, they suggest that **durable advantage, governance, and safety in the AI era will reside not in ownership of the frontier model, but in the design and ownership of the “loop/router layer” that combines and allocates human and AI capability.**

Why is this happening? Because the frontier standalone unit is unstable, as Nadella says, and it is becoming obsolete so quickly that its price can be cut in half. Long-term advantage does not accumulate around goods that can be imitated and matched so quickly. By contrast, a company’s own way of integrating and its rules of allocation—specific to its people, data, and context—are difficult to reproduce from the outside, and the more they are run, the more the gap widens through compounding. Scarcity is moving away from the standalone unit and into the integration dynamics outside it.

So what? It means the focus of competition, regulation, and organizational design all shifts by one layer. A game built around securing the strongest model does not create advantage if everyone can buy the same thing for half the price. The outcome will be determined by how a company lays its own unique loops and routers on top.

## Forecasts and implications

If this principle is correct, then over the next 1 to 2 years, three changes should be observed with high probability.

First, the unit of corporate competition will shift from “which model do you use?” to “do you own a loop that combines and allocates people and AI?” Once frontier model prices have fallen to less than half, as in Anthropic’s case, model selection can no longer serve as a differentiator; the difference gets concentrated in the design of the integration layer. Second, org charts will move in Mercari’s direction. As long as the governance of humans and AI remains split across separate lines, the integration loop cannot run, so the number of integrated nodes that bind the two together will increase—including CAIO-like roles pushing into HR. Third, debates over regulation and safety will shift from evaluating the capabilities of standalone models to auditing the routing layer responsible for allocation. Anthropic’s fallback design is an early sign that the object of regulation will become not “what the model answers,” but “which capability is delivered in which context.”

In practical terms, the implication converges to one point regardless of company size. Rather than spending resources chasing the strongest tool, the stronger investment against the obsolescence of any standalone unit is to **own and reliably run a single loop that compounds the judgment of your people with the capabilities of AI.** The frontier is no longer inside some individually owned model. It is emerging instead at the layer that connects and distributes them.
