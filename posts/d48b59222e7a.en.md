## Executive Summary

On October 2, 2026, Anthropic launched Claude Frontier Academy, a $100 million commitment to train 10,000 Frontier Deployed Engineers (FDEs) by the end of 2027. The company frames the scarcest resource in enterprise AI as people who can take Claude from an idea to a production system inside a real business, not the technology itself. The training follows a medical model of learning from practitioners, practising on real cases and being assessed before working alone, and graduates earn credentials.

## What was announced

According to Anthropic's announcement, the Academy is the first of its kind from an AI company and trains FDEs to the same standard of skills as Anthropic's own engineers.

- Investment: $100 million
- Target: 10,000 FDEs by the end of 2027
- First cohorts: Accenture, Bain, Capgemini, Commonwealth Bank of Australia, Deloitte, McKinsey, Morgan Stanley, Novo Nordisk and others
- Locations: San Francisco, New York and London, with cohorts running now
- Access: by nomination; organizations ask their Anthropic account team or Partner Account Manager about eligibility
The announcement says the people with the skills to make AI work inside a real business have become the hardest talent to find, and that a small group of deeply skilled people drives an outsized share of what AI delivers inside the companies Anthropic works with.

## How the program is built

The first program is the Frontier Deployed Engineer Residency. Anthropic likens it to the medical model: doctors learn from practicing physicians, train on real cases and are assessed before they practice alone. Residency engineers likewise learn from practitioners, practice on realistic cases and are assessed on their work before earning the credential.

The target participant is a hands-on software engineer with strong fundamentals, a record of building with LLMs, experience helping others adopt AI and a habit of tackling the business's most important problems. Organizations nominate their strongest engineers, each arriving with a named Claude project to lead on return. Prior experience building AI agents is not required.

There are two stages.

1. **A multi-day in-person program** with Anthropic engineers and licensed instructors: a simulated enterprise deployment, from choosing the right use case through security review to handover, ending in a graded practical on a new scenario. Those who pass earn the Claude Resident Engineer badge. 2. **A 12-week residency** in which the engineer leads a real Claude use case at their own organization, supported by Anthropic engineers and their cohort. They are assessed again at the end; those who pass earn the Claude Frontier Deployed Engineer badge, with the first expected in early 2027.

Two features stand out: assessment happens twice, and what is assessed is output on a real project of the engineer's own organization, not knowledge.

## What the partners say

The partner statements each name the kind of skill they want.

- Accenture (Ram Ramalingam): the residency tests "judgment against a simulated enterprise deployment, not technical skills in isolation", which is how Accenture thinks about the role.
- Capgemini (Geoffroy Pajot): clients are not asking about "certification counts or training volume" but about knowing firsthand how to solve real-world problems.
- Commonwealth Bank of Australia (Rodrigo Castillo): its engineers use AI tools every day, and its teams produced "up to 3x more code changes in the past year".
- Deloitte (Faruk Muratovic): enterprise-scale deployment needs people who can drive a project from first requirements through security review to real adoption.
- Novo Nordisk (Loic Giraud): points to Claude Science in R&D workflows and Anthropic engineers working alongside its scientists.
McKinsey and Bain also cite hands-on application of frontier techniques in client work.

## Relation to existing certification

The Academy builds on the Claude Partner Network, where, per the announcement, professionals across 46,000 firms have earned more than 175,000 Claude certifications and nearly 4,000 people have completed Basecamp, an immersive program teaching the curriculum Anthropic's Applied AI teams receive on onboarding. The Academy is for "the people who will lead the work", and training 10,000 FDEs is described as "the first step of many".

## Evidence and implications

**Evidence (confirmed by the announcement):** the scale ($100M, 10,000 engineers, end of 2027); the structure (in-person program plus 12 weeks, two assessments, two badges); the participating organizations; and nomination-only access.

**Implications (this article's reading):**

- The credential rests on assessment rather than attendance. The proof that consultancies and large enterprises want is shifting from certificate counts to whether someone can deliver to production on a real engagement, which is how Capgemini's statement summarizes client interest.
- The unit of training is an organization sending a person with a named project, not an individual enrolling. Talent development and delivery are tied into one mechanism.
- The cohort list shows the AI deployment workforce spread across AI companies, IT services, consulting, finance and pharma.
**Not established:** the announcement gives no cost breakdown, cohort sizes, pass rates, badge validity period, or timeline for opening to non-participating firms. The 10,000 figure is a target, not a result. The first FDE badges are expected in early 2027, which is when the program's effect can first be checked.
