## Executive Summary

Samsung Electronics estimated its third-quarter 2026 operating profit at 107.4 trillion won ($80.2 billion), up 782.5% year on year, and on the same day TSMC reported September revenue of NT$511.86 billion, up 54.6% from a year earlier. Memory and contract chip manufacturing, two distinct layers of the AI supply chain, posted records in the same week. Samsung's shares nonetheless fell 2.42% that day: the market is already asking how long the demand can last.

## What was announced

According to Euronews (8 October 2026), Samsung Electronics estimated on Thursday that its third-quarter operating profit jumped almost ninefold to a record 107.4 trillion won, or $80.2 billion.

- Operating profit: 107.4 trillion won (previous quarter: 89.49 trillion won; a year earlier: 12.17 trillion won)
- Year-on-year increase: 782.5%
- Sales: 195 trillion won, or $145.6 billion, up 127% year on year
- Operating margin: about 55%
- Analyst expectation: 106.1 trillion won, according to LSEG
- No breakdown by division; full results come on 29 October
The report treats the figure as conditional ("if confirmed"). It is an estimate, and the confirmed number waits for the 29 October results.

The nature of the record is also spelled out:

- It would be the biggest quarterly operating profit ever reported by a technology company, beating the $63.7 billion in operating income that Nvidia booked in its latest quarter.
- It would be the first time a South Korean company's quarterly operating profit topped 100 trillion won.
- It is Samsung's fourth record quarter in a row.
## TSMC and Taiwan's exports point the same way

On the same day TSMC, the world's largest contract chipmaker, said September revenue rose 54.6% year on year to NT$511.86 billion, or roughly $16 billion.

- September was only 0.6% below August's all-time monthly high.
- Estimated third-quarter revenue, calculated from the monthly figures, reached a record NT$1.49 trillion ($46.8 billion), up 51% on a year earlier and 17.6% on the previous quarter.
- That beat the top of TSMC's own guidance of $44.6 billion to $45.8 billion.
- Revenue for the first nine months rose 41.1% to NT$3.9 trillion ($122.1 billion).
Taiwan reported that its exports jumped 60.9% year on year to a record $87.2 billion in September, driven by global demand for AI hardware. GDP expanded 12.9% year on year in the second quarter.

## Why memory is lifting profit

The report's explanation is simple. Samsung is one of the world's biggest memory chipmakers, and AI data centres are snapping up high-bandwidth memory (HBM), stacked chips that feed data to AI processors. Tight supply is also pushing up prices of DRAM and NAND memory.

So the profit has two sources: demand for HBM, an AI-specific product, and rising prices for general-purpose memory. Because Samsung gave no divisional breakdown, how much each contributed cannot be verified from outside until 29 October.

## Why the market sold off

Despite the results, Samsung shares closed 2.42% lower at 262,000 won on Thursday and the benchmark KOSPI lost 2.62%. The report says investors took profits and questioned how long the memory boom can last. Samsung's stock has still almost quadrupled since last September. TSMC shares had closed 1.35% lower in Taipei before its figures were released.

This is a gap between fact and judgment. The fact is record profit and revenue. The judgment is a forecast of whether those levels persist, and the share price reacted to the latter.

## Dates that will answer the question

The report lists the next checkpoints:

- 14 October: ASML, the Dutch chip equipment maker, reports
- 15 October: TSMC reports full third-quarter results and gives its outlook
- 29 October: Samsung publishes detailed results
- November: Nvidia's results, described as a key indication of how long the AI chip rally can run
## Conclusion

What is established is that records landed at the same time in two layers of the semiconductor supply chain, memory (Samsung) and foundry (TSMC). Samsung's number is an estimate and its divisional breakdown is unpublished. Only two implications follow. First, demand for AI infrastructure had not slowed, at least as of the third quarter. Second, market attention has already moved from "is it a record" to "is it durable", and the results on 14 October, 15 October, 29 October and Nvidia's in November will answer that in stages.
