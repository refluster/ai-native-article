## Executive Summary

According to a report jointly prepared by OpenAI, Columbia, Duke, and the University of Pennsylvania, token-based usage of the agentic coding and work platform “Codex” is growing rapidly. While 99.8% of OpenAI employees’ output tokens now come through Codex, the figure is 63% for external organizations and 16.5% for individuals, highlighting major differences in adoption by user type. Among external organizations, Codex’s usage share rose from just over 0% in August 2025 to about 17% in less than a year, suggesting AI use is shifting from chat and search toward “delegated work.”

## Source of the Report and Measurement Method

This article is based on reporting by Axios’s Megan Morrone on June 25, 2026, drawing on a report OpenAI shared with Axios in advance. In addition to OpenAI, the report involved researchers from Columbia, Duke, and the University of Pennsylvania.

- The researchers divided Codex users into three categories: OpenAI employees, external organizations, and individual users.
- Measurement was conducted by comparing Codex and ChatGPT usage by token volume.
- The figures for individual users are based on a 0.1% random sample of individual users who allowed their queries to be used for training. The task-time thresholds are model estimates.
The report explicitly states that these are “model estimates” and that they rely on a specific sample. Accordingly, the figures below should not be treated as definitive values for the full population, but as model-based estimates for a limited sample.

## Differences in Adoption by User Type

There is a clear hierarchy in the share of output tokens generated by Codex across categories.

- OpenAI employees: **99.8%** of output tokens were generated by Codex.
- External organizations: **63%**.
- Individual users: **16.5%**.
The report positions OpenAI’s own Codex usage as an indicator of “how far users can shift toward agents when barriers such as cost, access, training, and organizational alignment are almost entirely removed.”

It also presents changes over time among external organizations. Of the external organizations actively using both ChatGPT and Codex, only just over 0% were using Codex in August 2025, but that share has now reached about 17%.

## The Scale of Delegated Work

For the individual-user sample, the report presents estimated “scale” of the work handled by Codex in terms of equivalent human work time.

- **80.6%** of the sample made at least one Codex request estimated to correspond to more than 30 minutes of work by an “experienced human.”
- **70.2%** made at least one request estimated to save more than 1 hour of human work.
- **25.6%** had delegated work estimated to take a human more than 8 hours to complete.
The report notes that while the absolute number of individual users remains small, those who do use Codex tend to use it intensively.

## Expansion Beyond Developers and the Limits of Adoption

Software development remains Codex’s core use case, but the report says the fastest-growing user segment is non-developers.

The shift toward agentic work began in earnest in early 2026. From that point, the report says, ordinary users started allowing tools such as Codex, OpenClaw, and Anthropic’s Claude Code to operate desktops, manage calendars, read and write files, control web browsers, and run scripts.

Workplace culture expert Jessica Kriegel described the effect of agents to Axios as follows.

- “Agents are reducing what I call the ‘psychological cost of action.’”
- Agents “make unfamiliar tasks feel more approachable. As a result, I get started faster, experiment more, and spend less energy worrying about what I don’t know.”
At the same time, clear limits to adoption remain. Among consumers on the Go, Free, Pro, and Plus plans who had actively used either ChatGPT or Codex in the past 28 days, fewer than **1%** used Codex. The report’s picture of the current reality is that most AI users are still chatting with bots, not operating fleets of agents.
