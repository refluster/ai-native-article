## Executive Summary

In a November 11, 2026 piece, a16z's Olivia Moore and Justine Moore argue that AI agents are becoming the primary users of the Internet, and that companies must shift from keeping bots out to competing for their business. Their evidence is a set of measurements — AI-driven traffic to Shopify stores up eightfold year over year in Q1 '26, agentic browser traffic up 8x between August and October — plus coding agents as a leading case study. They frame this as the early stage of a distribution shift after the Web and mobile.

## The claim: who is the "user"?

The article opens with a flat statement: "The primary users of the Internet are no longer human. They're AI agents." In the authors' account, agents now make up the majority of web traffic, write more than half of new articles published online, and generate most of the code pushed to production.

They contrast this with earlier platform shifts. The Web gave businesses a storefront anyone could visit; mobile put that storefront in everyone's pocket. AI, they say, has already changed what people can do, but its transformation of distribution is just beginning. They also attribute the limited consumer adoption outside ChatGPT to that lag on the distribution side.

## Evidence: changes already measurable

The figures the article cites:

- Shopify: AI-driven traffic to its stores grew eightfold year over year in Q1 '26; orders from AI-powered searches grew nearly thirteenfold.
- Human Security: agentic browser traffic grew 8x between August and October, with Muse driving the largest share (60%) and OpenAI products reaching a combined 30%.
- AI-referred consumer traffic shows higher engagement, converts better to revenue, and has a lower bounce rate than non-AI traffic.
- Even before agents, an estimated 11% of shopper journeys in the U.S. visited AI before a transactional site.
Most of today's traffic arrives through browser use: an agent either takes over the user's browser or spins up its own virtual machine and browser, then clicks around like a human. In parallel, something like an App Store for agents is emerging — connectors, plug-ins and MCPs through which agents reach third-party tools and sites directly. As an example, the authors asked ChatGPT to create and edit a video ad using the Krea, Descript and Eleven Labs plug-ins; it coordinated across the products and upsold them on a Descript paid plan.

Identity is moving too. Using an agent to sign up or pay gets easier once platforms let a user "lend" identity or tokens to other products (for example, Sign in with ChatGPT). Claude already lets enterprise admins connect plugins for an entire organization. And "just yesterday," OpenAI launched Businesses in ChatGPT, where companies can claim their business, add product feeds, and see insights on how users interact with them on ChatGPT.

## The precedent: coding agents and their appetite for outside tools

The authors treat coding agents as the case study for how the agent economy may play out. As of September 2026, upwards of 40% of GitHub PRs started with an agent's draft, versus just 17% six months prior.

Coding agents rely on external tools to finish a job. Asked to build a website, an agent may need a database, hosting, authentication, email delivery and monitoring. The article lists reported figures:

- Supabase (June '26): more than 60% of new databases are launched by AI tools.
- Neon (May '25, as written in the source): automated agents deploy 80% of its new databases.
- Stripe (June '26): agents accounted for nearly 40% of documentation traffic and 70% of CLI requests for API resources.
- Vercel (October '26): agent-triggered deployments up 28x over the last twelve months.
The piece closes with a question: fewer than 1% of the global workforce is developers — what happens when the other 99% starts using agents?

## Four kinds of company that may benefit

The authors name four categories that may stand to benefit from the B2A economy.

1. **Agents and agentic models.** Beyond standalone consumer harnesses like Instinct, Muse and Wabi, they expect every product to operate agentically as models become more action-oriented. ChatGPT, with 1.2 billion weekly active users, has a particularly intriguing opportunity. 2. **Products newly enabled by agents.** Amazon needed the Internet and Uber could not have existed without the mobile phone; the authors expect agents to produce their own examples. They name themes — self-running SMBs, AI-intermediated marketplaces or network products, auto-optimizers — while admitting that exactly how is hard to predict. 3. **Incumbents who optimize for agents.** LLMs are already a significant traffic source and formed the basis of the generative engine optimization (GEO) industry. GEO largely stops at discovery; agents take it into evaluation and execution of purchase or signup choices, an even larger market. 4. **Agent infrastructure.** To act for their humans in the real world, agents need their own tools. The authors already see system strain, at fairly low agent volume, on payments, fraud and identity, and communication (iMessage, calls, email).

## Reading it: from keeping bots out to winning their business

**Fact.** According to the article, most enterprises still block agent traffic over fraud and chargeback concerns; the authors believe they will have to embrace an increasingly valuable wave. This is the first piece in a series on the agent economy, and over the next few weeks they plan to publish more on what consumer agent usage looks like now, how agents decide which sites and products to send traffic to, how infrastructure is developing, and what hurdles remain before agents can complete transactions.

**Conclusion (our reading).** The load-bearing move in the argument is a change in who counts as the customer. If agents do the comparing and choosing, then what an agent can evaluate — price, features, specifications, APIs, machine-readable product feeds — shapes the choice more directly than brand awareness or funnel design. The reader comments show the implication. One commenter notes that a person may stay with a bank for ten years out of laziness, while an agent compares fees and rates in seconds; businesses that profit from customers not bothering to shop around face an uncomfortable future. Another, who is building in this space, asks how small or niche businesses get an agent's attention, and suspects that SEO and GEO still matter and that getting chosen will remain a long game of building brand and domain authority.

Caveats remain. The numbers are a collection of self-reported measurements from Shopify, Human Security, Supabase, Stripe, Vercel and others, with differing definitions and time windows. The authors themselves say the space is changing daily and that their market map is a "best current mapping." That a16z is a venture firm investing across AI companies is also relevant context for reading the piece; the newsletter carries its own disclaimer that it is not investment advice.
