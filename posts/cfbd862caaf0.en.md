## Executive Summary

Finance and accounting functions are approaching a turning point for agentic AI, but the real opportunity lies not in general-purpose tools, but in specialist agents built with business context embedded. Deloitte research shows that 48% of finance professionals who see themselves as playing a leading role in organizational strategy have already adopted AI agents, far above the 18% among those in more supportive roles. What determines success is not the model’s intelligence alone, but a full redesign of the operating model, including clean data foundations, auditable data lineage, and human verification.

## Adoption Is Advancing, but General-Purpose Tools Are Not Enough

Finance is a domain with an extremely low tolerance for error. Inaccurate financial management or reporting can expose companies to serious operational, regulatory, and reputational risks. An FT article (June 18, 2026) argues that, for this reason, general-purpose AI agents are not sufficient.

- According to Deloitte research, **48%** of finance professionals who identify themselves as playing a leading role in organizational strategy have already adopted AI agents. By contrast, among those who remain in a more supportive role, the figure is only **18%**.
- In another Deloitte figure, **23%** of companies are using agentic AI at “at least a moderate” level. However, this form of AI is expected to become widespread within the next 2 years.
Scott Stern, Vice President at BlackLine (which develops cloud software for agentic financial operations), says the difference between a general-purpose tool and an agent that companies can trust enough to deploy in finance and accounting comes down to “context.” “These agents need to have the context required for AI to understand the rules and nuances of finance and accounting processes.”

**Assessment:** The gap between 48% and 18% suggests that AI agents are becoming a tool that “those at the core of strategy adopt first.” The tipping point in adoption depends not only on the maturity of the technology, but on how deeply it can be specialized for the company and its workflows.

## Specialization Requires Organizational Context

Finance is both highly specialized and deeply company-specific. As a result, each company needs to build an AI architecture trained on finance and accounting processes and shaped to reflect its own structure. The article identifies the following concrete questions companies should consider.

- What is the specific nature of the workflow the agent is meant to handle? How does it connect with multiple other workflows?
- What entities, business units, and functions make up the company?
- What other technology systems, including ERP (enterprise resource planning), does the agent need to integrate with?
Stern puts it this way: “In this new world, the more specific you are about a particular use case, the better you can tune the AI. If you really want agentic AI to work well, you need a sense of organizational context so the agents can orchestrate with one another.”

**Assessment:** The implication here is that the value of finance agents lies not in the performance of any single agent, but in coordination among multiple agents and integration with existing systems. If deployment proceeds as a patchwork of standalone tools, that coordination will not materialize.

## Clean Data and Auditable Lineage

Another essential condition is to build the transformation on clean and accurate data. The article argues that if agents cannot access a single source of truth, the implementation is doomed to fail. This is necessary to avoid the trap of “rubbish in, rubbish out.”

- Finance teams require an unbroken audit trail. In other words, they need complete data lineage that allows every AI-generated output to be traced back to the original transaction and source documentation.
- Alistair Brisbourne, head of Technology Research at ACCA (the Association of Chartered Certified Accountants), adds that this does not mean a single giant database. “It doesn’t mean you need a monolithic database with all the data in one place. But you do need a coherent, integrated environment in which the AI can query the correct systems of record and generate data lineage back to the original systems from which it is pulling the data.”
**Assessment:** The data foundation is not a secondary prerequisite; it is the core of reliability. Without an established audit trail, the outputs of finance agents cannot be defended in regulatory or audit settings.

## Humans Stay in the Loop

Even with a strong foundation in place, humans cannot be removed from the loop entirely. Finance and accounting professionals will still need to devote time to validating the work produced by AI agents.

- Brisbourne of ACCA characterizes the current state this way: “We’re still a little way off being comfortable with agents executing decision-making on our behalf. Given the current risk profile, they are not yet handling core accounting processes. More work is needed not only on capability, but also on controls and governance.”
- In the invoice-processing example cited in the article, an AI agent matches purchase orders against goods-receipt records, detects anomalies, and proposes a resolution. Even then, it is the finance professional who decides whether to follow that recommendation.
The human role shifts toward strategic orchestration and governance at every critical control point. Stern ultimately argues that the focus of the discussion should be on how AI agents can enable finance teams to reinvent themselves. “By giving users tools that can intelligently execute repeatable processes on their behalf, you allow them to completely rethink their roles. That opens up a new discussion about what the finance function of the future will look like.”

**Assessment:** The more autonomous execution advances, the more human work shifts from performing individual tasks to designing control points and validating outcomes. The introduction of agents should therefore be understood not as a workforce replacement exercise, but as a redesign of the finance function itself.
