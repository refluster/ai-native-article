## Executive Summary

Alibaba Group CEO Eddie Wu told the Apsara Conference in Hangzhou on September 22, 2026, that Alibaba Cloud aims for more than 20 gigawatts of global data center capacity by 2032, alongside a decision to pull forward mass production of its new Zhenwu V900 AI chip by two quarters, to the first quarter of 2027. The announcement marks a shift in China's AI race: from a contest over which model is smartest to a contest over who can keep the most chips running.

## Light bulbs, not power plants

Wu opened with a history lesson: Thomas Edison demonstrated a practical light bulb in 1879, and commercial central power arrived three years later when Pearl Street Station began serving 59 Manhattan customers, according to U.S. Census Bureau data. "AI coding is simply the light bulb of the machine intelligence era," Wu said, per CNBC — then spent the rest of his keynote on the power plants. Bloomberg reports the concrete target: more than 20 gigawatts of global data center capacity by 2032. That eclipses the goal Wu set a year earlier — a tenfold increase in data center energy use by 2032, according to TechNode.

## The Zhenwu V900, six months early

The plan rests on the Zhenwu V900, a new chip from Alibaba's T-Head semiconductor unit that triples the performance of May's M890 and links up to 500,000 chips in a single cluster — what Wu called "the most powerful AI chip in China today." Yicai reported the chip was originally slated for the third quarter of 2027; CNBC now says mass production starts in the first quarter of that year. Pulling a flagship chip forward two quarters signals demand Alibaba cannot yet fully serve. Huawei made a similar move: on September 17, 2026, per The Next Web, it moved its Ascend 960DT chip up three quarters, to early 2027. Huawei's system is designed to link up to 1 million processors, setting China's two leading homegrown chip programs on a collision course for the same buyers.

## A fast-growing customer base, and an export-control tailwind

Reuters reported more than 400 external customers for Zhenwu chips as of May, as U.S. export controls pushed Chinese firms away from Nvidia (NVDA) processors. Benzinga now puts that count above 650 — up more than 60% in four months. Wu also said Alibaba expects its next-generation Qwen 4.5 and Qwen 5 models to reach 5 trillion to 10 trillion parameters, according to Seeking Alpha — up to four times the 2.4 trillion in the current flagship, Qwen 3.8 Max. Last year Wu framed self-improving AI as the final stage on the road to superintelligence, per TechNode; this year he said the Qwen team made "meaningful progress" on models that generate their own training data, per Seeking Alpha. Such models keep chips busy around the clock, tying his research agenda directly to his power target.

## The bill investors are watching

Hong Kong shares rose about 3% the day after the announcement, CNBC reported — a muted reaction. A year earlier, Wu's Apsara spending pledge had sent the stock to a four-year high, per the South China Morning Post; U.S.-listed shares were down about 25% for 2026 as of Monday, September 14. This year's bill is heavier: capital spending jumped 75% to 67.7 billion yuan in the June quarter while profit fell by the same proportion, per CNBC. Cloud revenue still grew 45%, according to Alibaba's own results filing, and Wu has told investors he puts growth ahead of margins. Shareholders have also absorbed dilution — Alibaba sold HK$80 billion of new shares at HK$112.70 each in August, per its SEC filing, after which Wu and Chairman Joe Tsai bought more stock. By June, per an Investing.com earnings-call transcript, Alibaba had spent 190 billion yuan of the 380 billion yuan it pledged over three years — meaning the new 20-gigawatt goal runs years past that existing budget.

## The race for a seat at the table

At last year's Apsara, Wu predicted only five or six global cloud platforms would ultimately dominate. The 20-gigawatt plan is his price of admission to that shortlist. Huawei is chasing the same seat with a system built to link up to 1 million processors. The contest inside China is shifting from who builds the smartest model to who can power the most chips. The first real test comes in the first quarter of 2027, when both the V900 and the Ascend 960DT are due. Ship on schedule, and 20 gigawatts becomes working compute; slip, and investors keep paying for power plants with too few bulbs to light.
