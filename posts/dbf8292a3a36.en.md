## Executive Summary

On its Q3 2026 earnings call, Reddit said it will move to a primarily logged-in experience. Search referrals fell by more than 1 million per week on average in October as AI-powered search spreads, yet revenue rose 53% year over year to $895 million. The stated aims are to protect data from AI scraping and to forge lucrative licensing deals with model providers.

## What was announced

On the Q3 earnings call on Thursday, October 29, 2026, Reddit announced it is transitioning to a primarily "logged-in experience." According to TechCrunch, the move is designed to protect Reddit's data from AI scraping so the company can instead forge lucrative licensing deals with model providers, and it could also help raise revenue even as U.S. daily active users decline.

The change will not be overnight. Reddit will first run tests in select markets outside the U.S. in the fourth quarter, and has no timeline yet for a worldwide rollout. It acknowledged that access to a logged-out front page is important, that being logged in does not mean giving up anonymity, and that it will keep anonymous browsing through an "incognito mode" experience that is being rebuilt.

## The numbers behind it

- Global daily active users were flat quarter over quarter at 130.3 million.
- U.S. daily active users fell from 53.2 million in Q2 to 50.7 million in Q3, down 2% year over year.
- Search traffic referrals dropped by more than 1 million per week on average in October. Reddit warned that if the trend continues, daily active users could decline by a low-double-digit percentage by next quarter.
- Revenue rose 53% year over year to $895 million, ahead of Wall Street expectations of about $875 million. Net income rose 74% to $283 million.
- Advertising revenue grew 54% to $847 million; non-advertising revenue, which includes AI data licensing, grew 35% to $48 million.
- Average revenue per daily active user rose 53% to $13.82 in the U.S. and 36% to $6.87 globally.
TechCrunch's own back-of-envelope: a 10% to 12% decline would bring Reddit to roughly 115 million to 117 million daily actives from 130.3 million. That is the article's hypothetical, not a Reddit forecast.

## Why logged-in

The article gives two lines of reasoning. First, logged-out users often arrive via search, while logged-in users provide more signals about interests and behavior, which makes them worth more to advertisers and improves targeting and personalization. Second, the same signals support a more personalized experience: personalized feeds, recommendations of new communities, and nudges to vote, comment and participate. The new algorithmic "For You" feed, announced the day before, combines posts from users' communities with recommendations based on their interests.

Nor is the problem Reddit's alone. The article says the shift to an AI-centered web has hit most websites and publishers, with fewer click-throughs as AI summaries answer questions without a visit and Google's "ten blue links" lose relevance, and that earnings showed it is now affecting Google's bottom line too.

## In the CEO's words

Co-founder and CEO Steve Huffman wrote in a post on Reddit: "I don't know about you, but as someone whose life has been defined by the open internet, it's a big change for me," adding that he has "come to the conclusion that this is the right decision for our platform and the right decision for our business." He also acknowledged that "there are still plenty of questions that are unanswered, particularly around where the boundaries should be between logged-in and logged-out experiences."

## Assessment: separating fact from interpretation

**Reported facts (per the source article):** the move to a logged-in experience, the Q4 tests outside the U.S., the figures above, and Huffman's statements.

**Interpretation:** TechCrunch calls the change a possible "canary in the coal mine for the open web." That is the article's reading. Whether the move helps or hurts revenue and daily actives cannot be confirmed until test results arrive. What is established is that the decline in search referrals is already happening and that monetization is improving. The open question is whether mandatory login deepens the decline in daily users, or whether data protection and licensing income outweigh it.
