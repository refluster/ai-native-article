## Executive Summary

Google will pay SpaceX $920 million per month from October 2026 through June 2029 for access to compute resources including approximately 110,000 NVIDIA GPUs. The deal was disclosed in an SEC filing on June 5, 2026, one week before SpaceX’s Nasdaq listing. The fact that even Google, often described as one of the world’s largest holders of AI compute, is sourcing outside “bridge capacity” reflects a structural squeeze in which AI demand is outstripping the pace of internal infrastructure expansion.

## Deal Terms—Amount, Duration, and Scope

First, here are the confirmed contractual terms. All figures are based on the SEC filing.

- **Amount**: Google will pay SpaceX $920 million per month.
- **Term**: From October 2026 through June 2029.
- **Scope**: Access to "approximately 110,000 NVIDIA GPUs, CPUs, memory, and other related components."
- **Disclosure**: Revealed on Friday, June 5, 2026, in an SEC filing submitted to regulators.
At roughly $900 million for a single month, the total value over the life of the agreement reaches into the tens of billions of dollars. This is not a one-off procurement, but a multi-year capacity reservation arrangement.

## Anthropic and xAI—SpaceX as a Compute Landlord

This deal is not an isolated event. It sits on the same trajectory as SpaceX’s emerging compute leasing business.

- It is similar in duration and scale to the deal with Anthropic disclosed in late May 2026. **Anthropic will pay SpaceX $1.25 billion per month through 2029** to lease the full amount of compute available at the Colossus 1 data center near Memphis, Tennessee.
- Colossus 1 is a data center that xAI—now part of SpaceX—originally built for its own AI development.
- Google’s deal is believed to amount to **roughly half** the compute Anthropic is getting at Colossus 1.
- SpaceX has not specified which data center Google will use. CEO Elon Musk had previously suggested that Colossus 2 would be reserved for xAI.
What this suggests is that SpaceX, having integrated xAI, is repositioning itself as a “neutral compute landlord” that leases capacity to both AI labs like Anthropic and hyperscalers like Google. Infrastructure built for its own AI business, xAI, is becoming a revenue-generating asset serving outside customers, including competitors.

## Why Is Google Buying Externally?—Demand Pressure

The striking point is that the buyer here is not an upstart AI lab struggling with compute shortages. It is Google itself.

- By some estimates, Google is **the world’s largest single holder of AI compute**. That puts it in the opposite position from Anthropic, which was reportedly constrained by compute and raised usage limits on the same day the deal was announced.
- Even so, Google says it moved to buy externally because of a sharp demand surge. The company’s statement reads: "Google Cloud and SpaceX are long-time partners. This is a short-term, timely agreement to ensure we have bridge capacity to meet surging customer demand for our agent platform, Gemini Enterprise, which has been even higher than we expected."
- Parent company Alphabet has already committed more than **$180 billion** in capital expenditures this year, and has said it expects to "significantly increase" that in 2027. To secure funding, it recently also announced a stock sale worth **$80 billion**.
Even a giant holder cannot expand fast enough on its own. The phrase “bridge capacity” signals Google’s own recognition that demand for its agent product Gemini Enterprise is temporarily exceeding supply, and that this deal is essentially a stopgap to buy time.

## Termination Clauses and the IPO—Risk Management Built Into the Deal

The agreement includes termination and phased rollout mechanisms designed to handle uncertainty in supply and demand.

- **Termination clause**: After December 31, 2026, both SpaceX and Google may terminate the agreement with 90 days’ notice.
- According to the filing, Google’s access will ramp up in stages "through September at a reduced fee."
- The filing states: "If we fail to deliver access to the committed amount of GPUs by September 30, 2026, then following a one-month grace period, Google may immediately terminate the agreement or accept the number of GPUs provided" (if the committed number of GPUs cannot be delivered by September 30, 2026, then after a one-month grace period Google may either terminate immediately or accept the number delivered, with a corresponding reduction in the monthly fee).
The timing of the deal and the capital relationships around it also matter.

- The deal was disclosed **one week before** SpaceX shares are set to begin trading on Nasdaq. SpaceX is seeking to raise about **$75 billion** at a valuation of roughly **$1.75 trillion**, which would make it one of the largest IPOs in history.
- Google has long been a SpaceX investor, and its post-IPO stake is expected to be worth more than **$100 billion**.
- The two companies are also reportedly discussing the construction of orbital data centers.
The termination rights and phased rollout are a hedge for both sides in a market where neither can perfectly forecast capacity needs. At the same time, disclosing a multi-year agreement with a major customer like Google just before listing gives investors a concrete signal about the revenue prospects of SpaceX’s compute business. The overlap between the capital relationship—Google as investor—and the customer relationship—Google as lessee of capacity—is what gives this deal its unusually distinctive structure.
