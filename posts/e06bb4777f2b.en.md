## Executive Summary

In a post dated 3 August 2026, The Kobeissi Letter states that US businesses invested an annualized $797.6 billion in software in Q1 2026 — "nearly 2x the pace seen a decade ago and one of the highest levels on record." The account frames this as evidence of "the next phase of the AI boom," defining that phase as a shift away from building new AI models and toward companies "investing trillions of dollars to deploy these models across various industries." This piece separates what the post actually asserts from what it leaves unestablished.

## The Figures the Post Presents

The post makes three factual claims.

- **Level**: "In Q1 2026, US businesses invested an annualized $797.6 billion in software."
- **Comparison**: "That is nearly 2x the pace seen a decade ago and one of the highest levels on record."
- **Change in character**: "As AI adoption accelerates, software is increasingly becoming core infrastructure rather than a discretionary expense."
The third is not a figure but an interpretation. The post infers a change in the *nature* of the spending from a change in its level, without supplying the data that would connect them — a breakdown of discretionary versus committed spend, say, or evidence of how the line behaves in a downturn. A rising level is equally consistent with discretionary spending simply having grown.

## The Structure of the "Next Phase" Claim

The second half of the post is explicitly marked as opinion. After the qualifier "In our view, this reflects the next phase of the AI boom," the phase is defined in two sentences:

> It will no longer be about simply building new AI models.

> Rather, it will be about companies investing trillions of dollars to deploy these models across various industries.

The contrast is construction versus deployment. What deserves attention is that the figure presented ($797.6 billion annualized) and the magnitude claimed ("trillions of dollars") sit at different orders of magnitude: the first is offered as a measured outcome, the second as a forecast of future corporate behaviour, and the post supplies no path between them. Nor does it break out how much of the software-investment growth represents spending directed at deploying AI models.

The reaction is visible on the same page. A same-day reply reads, "this bubble has so much more to give" — the same statistic read as evidence that the boom is inflating rather than maturing. One number is doing service as proof of a phase transition and proof of overheating at once.

## What the Post Does Not Establish

Editorially, the most important gap is the absent source. The post cites $797.6 billion to one decimal place while naming no issuing agency, no series, and no publication date. The word "annualized" signals that this is a quarterly figure scaled to an annual rate rather than actual Q1 outlays, but the method of that conversion is not stated either.

The Kobeissi Letter's own profile describes the account as "an industry leading commentary on the global capital markets." The post is therefore commentary on statistics, not a release of them. It was published at 6:29 PM on 3 August 2026 and shows 182.4K views.

That leaves clear work for anyone citing the number. First, go to the underlying series behind the $797.6 billion. Second, establish whether the "nearly 2x a decade ago" comparison is nominal or real. Third, since the post does not say, determine separately what share of the growth in software investment is AI-related. Commentary is a useful starting point, but a figure carried without its source loses that source the moment it is quoted.
