01Summary

Summary and health board

asp-cloud is the cloud side of a dynamic-pricing pilot (product name CHARGE) in which about 25 households in California take part. It collects measurements from home batteries, power sensors, water heaters and EV chargers, controls the devices against the utility’s price signals, and gives operators and participants screens and weekly reports. Its constitution (L0) sets the order ① the connection loop closes → ② operations run → ③ daily life is not disrupted, and puts optimization, scale and full automation after that.

Why this check is needed now

Since April the development setup has changed from “one person pushing directly” to “58 agents + 1 human, run through PRs and weekly audits.” In half a year the PR numbers passed 1,000, but over the same period the median PR wait grew about 4.5× and the rework rate about 2.5×, and the reliability indicator (SLI-1) went back to Red in W40–W41. Throughput held, yet speed and stability did not improve. If the next installation rush (24 households) arrives with this structure unchanged, fixes for field-caused defects will stall for days waiting for human approval (10).

Approval, merging, wiring and on-site work all converge on one person, and whatever exceeds that person’s capacity becomes the length of the queue. We added agents and we added audits, and what we added flowed into this queue.

Three things to try in the next 6–8 weeks (details in 09)

Change 1

Narrow the human queue

Wire the worker for the author lane, take non-L0/L1 PRs that are 🟢 from every reviewer out of the human queue, and batch hand-offs into a daily digest. A merge that overrides a 🔴 leaves a one-line reason.

Change 2

Make the disabled and deferred gates bite

Remove the || true from the restapi lint, get pip-audit running again, and set the coverage floor to the measured value. Required checks go forward as an ADR amendment proposal (L1).

Change 3

One yardstick for outcomes

A “one line of value” at the top of the weekly report, SLI-1 shown next to its raw value, a single definition of the lane merge count, and a CI check for Epic/ADR status drift.

Reading

  • The pipeline is not the constraint. merge → production is p50 1.5 min, p90 2.4 min. All 212 PRs that touched the deploy path were eventually deployed, and the 10 that took longer than 24 hours were all either “a failed deploy left alone” or “a workflow not yet in place.”
  • The wait is concentrated in human hand-offs. Since August, the 105 PRs with autopilot:needs-human took p50 15.3h (the first row of the three definitions of wait time) / p90 80.5h, and the 143 without it p50 0.8h / p90 7.3h — a difference of about 19×.
  • Deploys have levelled out. In May, 87 deploys were packed into 10 days; in September, 69 were spread over 23 days.
  • Change failure rate is flat; production stability did not improve. SLI-1 was 87.58% in W40 (248% of the budget consumed). Both the Sev-2 and the Sev-3 were detected by a person, not by an automatic alarm (after about 4 hours / about 8 days).
  • A “dormant failure” produced the longest recovery time. In grafana-cloud, a failure on 06-09 was not re-run by anyone for 76 days, until a change matching the path filter came in, and it resurfaced on 08-24 as 10 consecutive failures. Nothing keeps a failed main deploy open and tracked.
  • Rework doubled. On the single day of 08-25, 23 PRs were merged, 15 of them fixes. A loop of shipping artefacts that can only be checked in production (Grafana panels, CFN stacks) as small PRs and then fixing them in production has become routine (hypothesis).
  • The July dip was supply drying up. From W26 (week of 06-22), PR creation fell to 6–15 a week, and in July only 8 PRs touched the deploy path (all successful). The sister repo fell from 425 → 82 commits at the same time. There is no record of a stop, and the cause is a hypothesis.

Success factors

What the fast stretches had in common

  • One person carries it through in continuous sessions — the 161 non-L0/L1 PRs authored by refluster took p50 0.8h
  • Pre-validated input and a precedent to copy — E-019 had its round trip confirmed in a sandbox and replicated the structure of E-008
  • Review catches real defects and they are fixed in the same PR — in #891, most of the 15 findings were fixed in that PR
  • Small deploy units — a production 501 was detected in 30 minutes and fixed in 11
  • The lane merges non-L0/L1 PRs in minutes — verdict → merge in 2–4 minutes
Bottlenecks

Where the waiting piled up

  • B1 Waiting for a human merge — 168 needs-human PRs (created → merged) p50 21.3h / p90 128.3h (three ways to measure it)
  • B2 Architect decisions — median 34 days across 8 settled; 3 open at 87–101 days
  • B3 CI does not run on bot PRs — 34 of 60 refusals (after the 09-24 fix, the bot PR median went 36.4h → 2.9h)
  • B4 No worker for the author lane — 10 author-stale PRs at p50 128.3h. Merged while still 🔴 and cleaned up in follow-up PRs
  • B6 Field work does not get scheduled — #862 (P0) 27 days, #599 101 days
  • B7 First deploys are verified in production — reporter stack down for 90.6h
  • B8 A wrong outcome metric — lane merges reported as “0” for 10 weeks

Hypothesis. B1, B2 and B6 are all carried by the same one person. B3 and B4 (wiring) push work that should flow automatically back into B1 (about 53% of hand-offs are wiring and runtime failures). Adding audits or agents only turns whatever exceeds this one person’s capacity into queue length; that is the consequence of single-operator scale (C-3).

Data caveats that affect how firm the conclusions are

  1. Three actors share the refluster name (the human, the operator’s Claude Code sessions, and personas via CCR). Identifying human activity is an approximation from branch prefix, writing style and cron windows.
  2. Squash merge (from 06-10) hides fix commits made after review. Rework cannot be counted from merge commits.
  3. CI runs on bot PRs were recorded as “failure without ever running” (09-23 diagnosis). This was confirmed not to apply to deploy-*.
  4. The lane merge count swings between 0 and 65 depending on the definition. The main text takes the “about 17%” of the 10-09 root-cause document as the representative value and shows the others alongside.
  5. There is no record of the client’s Amplify deploys (85 merged PRs are out of scope).
  6. Measured coverage comes from a single run’s log. No tests were run locally.
  7. fix / rework / work type are heuristic (title regex + references + file overlap; keyword classification ±10%).
  8. 2026-10 is a partial month (days 1–10).
  9. The cause of the July dip, the cause of the chronic red on secret-scan / ci-sbom, and the ratification status of the analytics lake ADR are not identified.
  10. The cost field of the workforce ledger is 0 for all 58 agents (the API does not return it). Costs are the modelled values from /stats.
  11. The Health Check’s Mission split across analyses (the product analysis said concern to good, the workforce analysis said problem). The main text settles on 🟡↘ as “clear direction, inverted time allocation.” The ↑ on Easy to release refers to fewer ramp-up failures (August was 11/54 = 20%, 10 of them from one cause in grafana-cloud).
