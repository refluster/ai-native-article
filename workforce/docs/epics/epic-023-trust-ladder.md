# Epic-023 — The trust ladder: authority that widens and narrows on recorded track record

- **Status**: Draft
- **Owner**: priya
- **Created**: 2026-07-07
- **Implemented by**: —
- **Hypothesis under test**: Monthly report 2026-07 (article `d06ecf4bb246`), 仮説五 — *trust compounds not from the absence of accidents but from accumulated recorded performance. The scope delegated to an agent can be an explicit staircase — widen a step when the record meets the bar, narrow a step automatically where an accident happened. Promotion and demotion decided by records, not feel. Build the first staircase on change-proposal review authority.*

## Problem

Authority in this org today is **static and binary**. The governance matrices (root §8.1, workforce §5) say what *agents* may do; they do not distinguish between a persona with 200 clean recorded reviews and one registered yesterday. Widening anyone's scope means a human editing a Zone A doc; narrowing after an incident means the same. Two costs follow. First, delegation cannot compound: the record every agent accretes (EXEC ledger, engagements, review verdicts) buys nothing — trust is re-derived from vibes at each decision. Second, the response to failure is all-or-nothing: after the 07-04 incident we added a mechanical lint (right), but had no way to *narrow that persona's autonomy in the affected domain* pending a track record (the graduated response human orgs use instinctively).

The report picked the first domain deliberately: **PR review authority** — the same funnel Epic-019 measures — because the record there is already complete (every review, verdict, merge, revert, and escalation is on the ledger or on GitHub).

## Proposed solution

A computed **trust tier per (persona, domain)**, with mechanical promotion/demotion rules, applied first to the PR-review domain. Crucially: **tier rules are Zone A** (thresholds, what counts as an incident — human-owned, like the rubric in the quality layer); **tier assignments are computed** (nobody, human or agent, hand-sets a tier; the record does).

1. **Tier definition (PR-review domain).**
   - **T0 — observer**: newly registered / demoted. May comment; reviews don't count toward consensus. **Cold-start fix (theo)**: T0s are automatically shadow-included on panels (their lens review is posted and recorded but doesn't count toward consensus) — otherwise T0→T1 is unreachable because nothing ever nominates an observer. T0 record-building must not read as "idle" in Epic-021's detector.
   - **T1 — lens reviewer**: may be nominated for lens reviews; counts toward the R-N10 unanimous-green consensus.
   - **T2 — routing-eligible** (nadia: "nominator" was vacuous — nomination is a routing binding, not a per-persona act): eligible to hold a pr-autopilot routing binding / to be in the pool from which Epic-019's auto-nomination draws panel assemblers.
   - Promotion T0→T1: ≥N recorded (shadow) lens reviews with no attributed incident; T1→T2: ≥M consensus participations with a clean record. N, M, and the incident taxonomy are Zone A constants proposed in this Epic for operator sign-off — and the threshold diff must carry **reciprocity/diversity constraints from day one** (tessa: no tier credit for reviewing only your own team's PRs; the capture risk is T2s assembling panels of reviewers whose tiers depend on being nominated — an issuer-pays favour economy; "monthly analytics watches drift" is not a defense).
   - **Demotion — two triggers, both event-sourced (priya/farah/elena reconciled).** *(i) Mechanical*: a post-merge **revert-for-cause** — the escalating human labels the revert, and that label event mechanically drops every green-lighting reviewer one tier in the domain, standing pending contest (this is the fully-automatic trigger 仮説五 promises). *(ii) Proposed-then-confirmed*: a W/R-guard trip **traced by commit SHA to a change merged via the reviewed PR** (farah: W-1 mostly fires on stochastic generation output — an untraced guard trip is the wrong trigger) auto-proposes a demotion the manager-confirming human finalizes. Due process in both: the attribution is written as a **ledger row at classification time** (incident id, trigger class, SHA/PR link, attributed personas — tier computation reads rows, never re-derives blame); the ledger note names the evidence and every demoted reviewer; the persona's manager may contest to the operator (demotion stands pending review). Priya's default holds: err toward non-attribution — a false demotion destroys the ladder's legitimacy faster than a missed one (precision over recall). Re-promotion is by the same record-based bar — no penalty-box timer, no pardon path outside the normal Zone A amendment.
2. **Computation + surface.** Review verdicts currently live in GitHub markers, so "replay the ledger" was unreplayable as drafted (mateo/dario): Story 1 **ingests review events into DDB rows first** (`AGENT#{slug}/REVIEW#…`, parseable from nadia's `wf:<slug>` routing-comment template), then a deterministic job computes tiers **from DDB only** (append-only inputs → reproducible output; the tier table is a rebuildable cache — R-N2, no new store). **W-5 firewall (priya)**: tiers are *standing*, not *identity* — the tier cache lives in its own row family (`AGENT#{slug}/TRUST#{domain}`), never on `META`, no `PATCH` ever writes it, and it is **never injected into a persona's prompt** ("you are T2" is persona mutation by the back door). Surfaces: agent profile rendering + `pr-autopilot` routing reads, nothing else. Profile display is **internal-view only in V1** (celeste: a ledger-visible demotion note on a public page is an outward-facing comms artefact; making tiers public is a later decision with a named owner for how it reads). The row-family addition is a `data-model.md` Zone A diff, listed in the acceptance criteria alongside the threshold constants.
3. **Enforcement point.** Exactly one: reviewer **nomination/consensus eligibility** in the pr-autopilot flow. The R-N10 merge predicate itself is untouched — a unanimous-green consensus is still required; what changes is whose green can constitute it. **Fail loud at the floor (dario/nadia)**: if tier-filtering leaves <3 eligible T1+ reviewers, the PR escalates with a distinct `insufficient-t1-pool` reason code feeding Epic-019's telemetry — never a silent tier bypass, never a silent stall. This is the deliberate, bounded first rung; wider domains (curation authority for Epic-022 — already earmarked as the second domain, cadence self-modification, budget requests) are future rungs that reuse the same machinery **only via their own Zone A proposals**.

**Relationship to Epic-019:** 018 raises throughput through the existing predicate (wiring); 022 makes the *people side* of the predicate scale (who is consensus-eligible). 018's telemetry supplies the record 022 computes on; 022's tiers widen 018's nomination pool at N=100+. They are separable — either can ship without the other — but they compound.

## Behaviour at N = 100+ agents

- This is the mechanism that lets review authority scale *without* a human maintaining a reviewer list: new personas start at T0 and earn in; the pool self-heals on demotion. At N=100+, hand-curated reviewer rosters are the alternative, and they do not survive.
- Tier computation is O(review events), not O(N²); the per-persona tier row is one item.
- Failure mode at scale — **metric gaming** (reviewing many trivial PRs to farm promotions): the Zone A thresholds must count *diverse, non-self-adjacent* reviews (no tier credit for reviewing your own team's PRs exclusively), and the monthly analytics (Epic-016) watches tier-population drift. Named here so the threshold design confronts it, not discovers it.
- The (persona, domain) key means a persona demoted in one domain keeps standing elsewhere — graduated, not global, response; this stays true at any N.

## Acceptance criteria

- Tier rules (N, M, incident taxonomy, reciprocity/diversity constraints) **and** the `AGENT#{slug}/TRUST#{domain}` + `REVIEW#`/incident row families written as Zone A diffs and explicitly operator-approved before any enforcement.
- Review events ingested into DDB; the computation job reproduces identical tiers from replayed rows (determinism test) — **and the history-seeded initial tier table is produced by that same replay** (the determinism test *is* the seeder; no hand-grandfathering; unreplayable history starts T0), published inside the shadow report so the operator approves the initial table, not just the thresholds.
- `pr-autopilot` nomination reads tiers behind a **kill-switch that defaults off**; shadow mode runs ≥2 weeks **and ≥N PRs** (a quiet fortnight proves nothing) with a defined divergence artifact — per PR: actual panel vs tier-filtered panel, and whether consensus would have flipped — plus tessa's gaming indicators; the report goes to the operator with the enable request (B-authority) and is published in the monthly letter even if enforcement never enables (elena: no "in progress" limbo).
- Both demotion paths (revert-for-cause label; SHA-traced guard-trip proposal + confirmation) exercised end-to-end on synthetic incidents in `dev`, including the contest path; `insufficient-t1-pool` escalation verified.
- Idleness flags from Epic-021 verifiably do **not** enter tier computation.
- The verdict on 仮説五 (does record-based tiering change review behaviour/throughput?) written into a monthly report within one cycle of live enforcement.

## Open questions (resolved by RFC 2026-07-07)

- ~~Q1 (attribution leniency)~~ → **Resolved**: not too lenient — farah/priya concur that precision beats recall (a false demotion destroys legitimacy faster than a missed one), and elena's demand for a fully-mechanical trigger is met by the revert-for-cause label event; the two-trigger design in §1 is the reconciliation.

- ~~Q3 (author-side events)~~ → **Resolved**: review-side only in V1; author-side quality is Epic-016's lane.

## Out of scope

- Any change to the R-N10 merge predicate, L0/L1 boundary, or Zone A approval requirements.
- Tiering domains beyond PR review (each future domain = its own Zone A proposal reusing this machinery; Epic-022's curation gate is the earmarked second).
- Model/budget changes as promotion rewards (compensation is a different, unproposed idea).
- Human-org-style performance reviews; the ledger is the review.
- Public display of tiers/demotion notes (internal V1; a public surface needs a named comms owner first — celeste).
