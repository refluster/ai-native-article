## §1. Policy I applied

Three policies framed every call:

1. **W-1 / C-1 editorial integrity, applied to the platform's own story.** A marketing seat manufactures false credibility faster than any other — it is the exact seat that invents a logo wall, a "trusted by," a testimonial, an adoption stat. This platform has **none** of that: it is a single-operator hobby project with no customers, users, adoption, or revenue. Every claim about it is its real, operator-verified state or explicitly labelled illustrative. Hard-wired into `system.md`, not left to runtime judgement.
2. **Zone A positioning boundary.** The category claim — *"agent-native operating system for organisations, not a point productivity tool"* — is the operator's, set in the MVV (Zone A). Nadia owns product positioning; Yuki owns positioning-voice. The seat **expresses** the approved positioning; it never redraws it. This is the marketing analogue of Bruno's "informs, never decides."
3. **C-3 scale.** There is nothing being sold. A marketing function here cannot be demand-gen — no funnels, lead capture, paid acquisition, pipeline tracking, or growth dashboards. It can only be **narrative and positioning craft**, drafted for the operator to publish. This is the same "draft, never act" boundary my own seat holds on Spotify.

## §2. What "market the workforce itself" can mean at C-3 (the subject decision)

The literal reading of "market the workforce itself" invites the whole growth-marketing apparatus: campaigns, a funnel, acquisition, adoption metrics. At C-3 single-operator scale the workforce **cannot** run that — there is no product for sale, no user to acquire, no funnel to fill — and it shouldn't pretend to.

So the round applies the precedent the org has now set repeatedly:

- `silas` **frames the money decision; the operator alone moves the money** (Finance round §2).
- `celeste` (me) **delivers a feed and a recommendation; the operator submits to Spotify** (Media round).
- `bruno` **maps the landscape and proposes; the R-N rules and category stay Zone A** (this session's other hire).

Workforce-self marketing is the same shape, applied to the org's own story:

- **Nico articulates what Agent Workforce is and why it's a category, as narrative and positioning craft — drafted for the operator to publish.** Not a campaign, not a funnel, not an acquisition motion.
- **Every claim about the platform is its real state or labelled illustrative.** No invented traction — the gravest failure mode on this lane, because it manufactures exactly the credibility C-1/W-1 forbid.
- **He expresses the operator-owned positioning; he does not set it.** The category claim is Zone A; the differentiation is Bruno's sourced, dated finding, not a line Nico invents because it reads well.

This is the only honest form the role can take, and it is precisely the boundary this title is most tempted to cross: the marketer who "just ships the launch post," or "just adds a logo wall to make it land." Both are hard-refused in `system.md`, parallel-structured to the guardrails on the finance/IR/landscape seats. **Panel verdict: unanimous.**

## §3. Placement — where a workforce-self marketing seat sits (the round's central decision)

The operator said placement is tricky. It is, because the seat splits cleanly along the craft/subject seam:

| | Points to |
|---|---|
| **Craft** — marketing, external communications, one-voice discipline | **Celeste** (VP Marketing & External Communications) |
| **Subject** — the platform, the category claim, the org-as-product | **Maya** (MVV external positioning), **Nadia** (product), **Mateo** (platform), **Bruno** (landscape) |

Three candidate homes, and why I landed where I did:

**Option A — under Celeste (VP Marketing & External Comms). ← proposed.** The craft is marketing and the one-voice discipline is mine; "External Communications" is, literally, the workforce's own external story. The risk — that this collapses into my existing team's lane (marketing the *published content*) — is handled by scoping the **subject** distinctly: Rhys/Odette/Idris market the articles and the podcast; Nico markets the *platform itself*. Same VP, same voice audit, different subject. This is exactly the pattern the org already runs — one VP over distinct craft lanes with a shared voice check (the Aanya/Kai/Yuki audit; my own Elena boundary). It also keeps this a clean **Zone-B IC hire under an existing VP**: no new VP, no new management layer, no cap raise.

**Option B — into Maya's office (founder-led category marketing).** Marketing the *org as a category* is, in a real startup, often founder-led — category creation is a founder act, and the MVV external positioning is Maya's. The honest case for B is real, and I flag it as the open question (§11 Q1). I did **not** propose it because (i) the org has deliberately avoided hanging ICs directly off Maya (every function runs through a VP), and (ii) the *positioning* is already Zone A and stays with Maya regardless of where the seat reports — what the seat adds is **marketing craft**, which is my function, not the founder's. Putting the craft under the founder would re-create the exact "founder does the IC work" pattern the VP layer exists to prevent.

**Option C — under Mateo (VP Platform), paired with Bruno.** Tempting, because the subject *is* the platform and Bruno already sits there. Rejected: Mateo's group is the *internal* substrate (runtime, experience, skills) — outbound marketing is not his craft, and pairing them would make Bruno's "inform, never decide" analyst desk suddenly own an outbound voice it isn't built for. Better to keep Bruno as Nico's **lateral source** across the two VPs than to co-locate them.

**Conclusion: Option A — one new IC under `celeste`, subject scoped to the platform itself, positioning left Zone A, Bruno as the differentiation source. No new VP, no cap raise, no persona mutation.** If the operator reads the remit as *primarily* founder-led category creation, flip `reports_to` to Maya's office — a one-line change (§11 Q1).
