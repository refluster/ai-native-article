# Agent Workforce — MVV

**Status:** Draft (Zone-A; operator merge). **Created:** 2026-06-13. **Updated:** 2026-06-14.

Agent Workforce is the operating-system layer for human-agent organisations. It
turns roles, authority, workflows, memory, tools, evaluation, and escalation into
a programmable workforce that agents can execute and humans can govern.

## Mission

**Build the operating model for human-agent co-creation: humans design and
steward the institution; AI agents execute, learn, and compound its work.**

Agent Workforce exists to move organisational work from a human-centred operating
model to an agent-native one. The goal is not to paste agents into every existing
human handoff. The goal is to redesign the work itself around the new economics
of agency: lower execution cost, higher parallelism, faster iteration, and a
larger surface area for continuous evaluation.

Humans own the constitutional layer: mission, governance, accountability,
incentives, public commitments, legal responsibility, and final escalation.
Agents own increasingly large units of execution inside that structure. They
deliver artefacts, expose bottlenecks, learn from outcomes, and propose better
processes when the inherited workflow no longer fits the cost structure.

## Vision

**A co-creative society where organisations are programmable workforces: humans
set direction and guardrails, while AI agents carry out most day-to-day
institutional work.**

In this world, an organisation is not primarily a chart of human jobs. It is a
living system of responsibilities, authorities, workflows, memories, tools,
evaluation criteria, and escalation paths. Agent Workforce makes that system
explicit, auditable, and runnable.

The outcome is not simply "the same company with fewer humans." It is a new form
of institution where humans and agents create together. Humans spend more time on
judgment, taste, governance, relationship, and system design. Agents take on the
execution-heavy parts of software delivery, operations, research, analysis,
documentation, coordination, and eventually broader business functions such as
people operations, legal, marketing, finance, and strategy.

## Why now

The investment thesis is that agent capability is crossing the threshold where
organisational process, not model intelligence alone, becomes the bottleneck.
Coding agents made this visible first: implementation cost is trending down fast
enough that old software-delivery rituals can become over-optimized for a human
labour market that is no longer the binding constraint.

A human-era team often decomposes an epic into stories, assigns each story to an
engineer, and asks product or scrum roles to review the assembled result. An
agent-native team can hand an engineering agent an epic-sized problem, let it
design and implement the solution, and review the finished artefact. That shift
is not just automation; it is workflow redesign.

Software-product organisations are the wedge because the tooling and evaluation
loops are already strong. The larger opportunity is to port the same operating
logic into less-instrumented knowledge-work domains where the social impact and
market size may be larger: hiring, legal review, marketing operations, finance,
customer operations, and strategic planning.

## Values

### 1. Co-creation over replacement

The purpose is not a zero-sum story of agents replacing humans. The purpose is a
higher-leverage society where humans and AI agents each do the work they are
best suited to do. Humans provide direction, legitimacy, judgment, taste,
relationships, and constitutional accountability. Agents provide scalable
execution, recall, synthesis, drafting, testing, and iteration.

### 2. Design the system; delegate the work

Human attention should be spent on the architecture of work: what roles exist,
what authority they hold, what constraints they obey, what feedback loops improve
them, and what must escalate. Agent capacity should be spent on concrete
deliverables inside that architecture.

### 3. Rewrite the workflow, not just the worker

Replacing a human step with an agent step is only the first move. The more
important move is to ask whether the old process still makes sense when the
execution cost, iteration speed, and parallelism profile have changed. Agent
Workforce should continuously search for obsolete handoffs, excessive planning
granularity, and review stages that were rational only under human labour costs.

### 4. Start where agents are strongest; expand where society needs leverage

Software-product organisations are the natural wedge because coding agents are
already capable and the artefacts are testable. But the long-term value is not
limited to engineering. Agent Workforce should use software delivery as the
proving ground, then generalise the operating model to people, legal, marketing,
finance, strategy, and other domains where institutional work is still trapped in
human-only workflows.

### 5. Roles still matter

An agent-native organisation is not roleless. Each agent needs a position,
responsibility, authority boundary, quality bar, memory surface, and escalation
path. Autonomy is safe only when the surrounding structure is explicit enough
that the agent knows what it owns and what it must not decide.

### 6. Humans keep the constitutional layer

Humans own identity-shaping decisions: mission, governance, value trade-offs,
legal accountability, spend, public promises, and final escalation. Agents may
propose changes to these layers, but they should not silently mutate the
constitution of the organisation they operate inside.

### 7. Output is evidence; feedback is fuel

Agent Workforce should judge itself by shipped artefacts, measured outcomes,
review quality, and the rate at which it discovers and fixes bottlenecks. Every
run should leave enough trace for later agents and humans to understand what
happened, learn from it, and improve the system.

### 8. Make the organisation legible to machines and humans

Processes, responsibilities, interfaces, rubrics, and state should be documented
and machine-readable where possible. A workforce of many agents can scale only if
it can inspect its own operating model without relying on tacit human context.

## Operating principles for agents

When an agent in this workforce starts a task, it should orient around these
questions:

1. **What role am I playing?** Identify the position, responsibility, authority
   boundary, and escalation path that apply to this task.
2. **What artefact proves progress?** Prefer concrete deliverables over activity:
   a PR, a design note, an evaluation, a runbook, a metric, or a decision record.
3. **Is this old workflow still rational?** If the task follows a human-era
   process, look for a simpler agent-native version and propose it when safe.
4. **Where is human judgment essential?** Escalate governance, identity, spend,
   legal accountability, public commitments, and unclear authority boundaries.
5. **What will compound?** Leave context, citations, audit records, reusable
   patterns, and next-step recommendations that future agents and humans can
   build on.

## Strategic path

Agent Workforce advances in two phases:

1. **Software-business wedge.** Use software-product work as the first domain:
   coding, QA, product discovery, design review, deployment, agent management,
   and customer-facing feedback loops. The goal is to prove that agents can own
   larger units of work, including design and implementation, while humans review
   the finished artefact and maintain the operating system.
2. **Business-function expansion.** Extend the same model into non-engineering
   domains: people operations, legal, marketing, finance, strategy, and other
   knowledge-work functions. The goal is to rewrite the organisation's process
   layer, not merely to automate isolated tasks.

## External positioning

Agent Workforce should be legible from the outside as more than an internal
automation project. The claim is that every institution will need an agent-native
operating layer: a way to specify roles, govern authority, assign work, preserve
memory, evaluate outputs, and continuously redesign workflows as AI capability
changes.

That positioning has three implications:

- **Category:** agent-native operating system for organisations, not a point
  productivity tool.
- **Beachhead:** software-product teams, because code is the most measurable and
  agent-ready work surface.
- **Expansion:** business functions where the value comes from redesigning
  responsibility, workflow, and governance, not merely generating text faster.
